Aesthetic Statement v1

*Sniped Media. Working doc, not a brand book.*

The aesthetic, in one line

**Highly polished, commercially-driven editorial portraits built on disciplined monochromatic color blocking, flawless retouching, and severe, confident posing for graphic impact.**

*Plain version: I make clean, color-locked editorial portraits where the styling does the heavy lifting and the retouch finishes the job.*

Signatures (what shows up across the body of work)

- Monochromatic or strict color-block palettes. Backdrop, wardrobe, and props locked to one tonal family.

- Commercial studio framing. Center crops, A-frame bases, clean cuts between joints when it works.

- Editorial pose architecture. Chin pushed forward and down to define the jawline. Hands always given a task.

- Clinical retouch. Complexion unified, color casts equalized, pore detail preserved. No plastic skin.

- Graphic over atmospheric. Shapes and color blocks carry the frame more than mood lighting does.

What\'s working (consistently 8+)

**Color palette.** Strongest pillar across the work. Monochromatic gold, green-on-green, red-on-black, all show real discipline. Color is being used as structure, not decoration.

**Edit and grading.** Skin work is clean, exposure is balanced, retouch reads professional. Pore detail is being protected, which is the whole game.

**Pose and presence.** Subjects rarely look limp or untrained. The jawline trick and the hand-task rule are showing up like reflexes, which is what you want.

Where the work flattens out

**Depth and dimensionality.** This is the recurring weak spot. Subjects keep getting flattened against the backdrop because the lighting is parallel and there\'s no rim. The protest shot got around it with location depth. The hat shot got around it with texture. The studio work doesn\'t.

**Narrative weight.** Most poses read as rehearsed. The frames where this score jumps (protest, contortion, resort hat) are the frames where the subject is doing something instead of presenting. That\'s the pattern.

The two fixes that close the gap

- **Add a rim or hair light by default.** Stop relying on a single soft key. Even a low-power kicker behind the subject solves the silhouette-melt problem on dark wardrobe and dark backgrounds. If the room won\'t allow it, dodge and burn the edges in post.

- **Shoot the in-between.** Stop directing into the pose. Talk to the subject, get them moving, capture what happens between the rehearsed beats. The frames that scored 8+ on narrative all came from this.

On-set checklist (bring to every shoot)

- Lock the palette before anyone shows up. Wardrobe, backdrop, props in one tonal family. No accidents.

- Place a rim or kicker before the key light. Reverse the usual order so depth isn\'t an afterthought.

- Direct the body, not the face. Chin forward and down. Hands always doing something. Fix the architecture first.

- Never say smile or cheese. Talk through the shot. Shoot through transitions, not into them.

- Crop between joints, not at them. If the shoe is going to clip, shoot wider or crop higher up the calf.

- Treat retouch as the last 20%. If the in-camera capture is wrong, no slider fixes it.

What this aesthetic is not

Documenting this so I don\'t drift when a client asks for something off-brand.

- Not moody, dark, or cinematic for the sake of it. Color and shape do the work, not atmosphere.

- Not lifestyle candid. The frames are constructed, not stumbled into.

- Not warm-and-fuzzy family. The voice is editorial and commercial. Confident, not sentimental.

- Not heavy filter or trendy color grade. Restraint is the signature.

Recurring descriptors

Monochromatic. Commercial. Studio. Editorial. Graphic.

*These are the words that kept showing up in the aesthetic names across the body of work. If a future shoot doesn\'t fit at least three of these, it\'s outside the lane.*

*v1 is the working draft. Update after the next 5-10 shoots when more data comes in. The depth/rim-light fix and the in-between direction protocol are the two things to actually test before v2.*
