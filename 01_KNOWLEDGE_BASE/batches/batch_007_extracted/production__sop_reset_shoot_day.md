# SOP · The Reset · Shoot Day

**45 minutes on camera. End-to-end on-location time: 90 minutes. Output: 60-80 raw frames, 20-30 keepers, 20 final retouched.**

Reset shoot day is structured around two facts: (1) the prospect paid for the operator's eye, not the photographer's improvisation; (2) the camera reads two layers (mechanical + psychological) and direction has to address both.

---

## 24 hours before · pre-shoot brief

Calendly automation should send this email at 24h. If automation isn't built yet, send manually.

**Email subject:** `Sniped Reset · tomorrow · 1 of 2 things to read`

**Email body:**
> Hey [First name],
>
> Tomorrow at [time] at [location].
>
> Two things to know before:
>
> **1. Wardrobe:** [specific guidance based on diagnosis. Examples: "monochromatic, dark tones · black, charcoal, navy. Avoid logos, busy patterns, or three-piece suits unless they're already your visual identity." or "executive register · solid color, well-fitted, single layer. Bring two options if you're unsure."]
>
> **2. Mood:** the protocol we're correcting is [Protocol N · Name]. On set we'll work through the mechanics first, then the energy. The session moves quickly; trust the direction.
>
> Park: [specific parking note]. Door / suite: [specific entry]. My number if anything: [phone].
>
> See you tomorrow.
>
> · BJ

## 60 minutes before · arrival + setup

- [ ] Arrive 60 min early. Confirm location access (key, code, contact, parking)
- [ ] Set up before the client arrives. The space is ready when they walk in; you are not unboxing gear in front of them.
- [ ] **Lighting setup:** start with one of the three working setups from `The Production Stack` (window+bedsheet · two-strobe+beauty dish · key+fill+rim). Match to the look the prospect agreed to.
- [ ] Test the setup on a stand-in (a chair with a coat, your own face on a self-timer). Adjust before the client is in the chair.
- [ ] Confirm the moodboard, call sheet, and shot list are on your phone or tablet. Don't print; the paper trail is friction in a 45-min session.
- [ ] Open Lightroom on the laptop · ready for tethered ingestion if applicable
- [ ] Music ready: quiet, instrumental, low-BPM. Subjects shoot looser when there's audio in the room. (Avoid lyrics; they pull attention.)
- [ ] Water available. Mirror near set if possible.

---

## Client arrival · 5-10 min · the warm-up

- [ ] Greet at the door, not the studio entrance. The walk to the set is the social warm-up.
- [ ] Address the insecurity question first thing, conversationally:
  > "Before we get into it: anything specifically you're self-conscious about that I should mind on the day?"
  Whatever they say, take it seriously. If they say "my left side" or "my chin" or "my neck" · the entire shoot mitigates that. This is the trust-builder.
- [ ] Walk them through the set. Show them where they'll stand, where the camera is, where the lights are. Demystify the room.
- [ ] **7-second physical warm-up.** "Before we shoot, shake the legs out, roll the shoulders, take a breath. The first three poses always feel weird; that's fine."
- [ ] Frame the next 45 minutes:
  > "We're going to run through the 10-protocol direction. Mechanics first, energy second. I'll talk you through the body · chin here, shoulders here, hands here · and then we'll move to where the eyes go. The session goes fast. Trust the calls."

---

## On camera · 45 min · the session

The 45 minutes are sequenced into 5 nine-minute beats. Each beat ends with a wardrobe / pose reset.

### Beat 1 (0-9 min) · Architecture · Establishing shots
- [ ] Subject in the agreed wardrobe. One pose vocabulary (per the male/female default in the Production Stack).
- [ ] Run the 7 posing points in order: feet → knee → hip → torso → shoulders → arms/hands → head/eyes
- [ ] Direct one variable at a time. Confirm the hits ("yes, hold that"). Keep the camera away from your face during direction so they read your face, not the lens.
- [ ] First 30-40 frames are architecture. Rough frames; no keepers required yet.

### Beat 2 (9-18 min) · Mechanics correction · the named protocol
- [ ] Bring the subject's awareness to the protocol named in the VIB. "The thing we talked about · [Protocol N · Name] · is what we're working on for the next 9 minutes."
- [ ] Direct the correction explicitly. "Chin push. Forward. Down. There. Hold. Now eyes to me. There." Short commands, beat rhythm.
- [ ] 30-40 frames in this beat. The keepers start emerging here.

### Beat 3 (18-27 min) · Energy · Psychological presence
- [ ] Mechanics held; now the eyes commit. "Mechanics are locked. What I need now is the eyes ahead of the smile. The eyes commit, then the rest follows."
- [ ] Use Thought Narration: "I know the impulse is to perform; we don't need that. We need the version of you that walks into a room you own."
- [ ] Direct in the in-between. Talk through the shot, not into the pose. Capture transitions.
- [ ] 30-40 frames. The strongest 5-8 frames of the day usually live in this beat.

### Beat 4 (27-36 min) · Rotation pass
- [ ] Same pose vocabulary, rotated 45° → 90° → back-turned-with-glance. Same lighting setup; the subject moves, not the lights.
- [ ] Quick beats. 20-30 frames.

### Beat 5 (36-45 min) · Variation + safety
- [ ] One variation: a chair, a wall lean, or a hand-prop (glasses, a folder, a phone). Adds variety to the gallery without changing the visual claim.
- [ ] Cover the safety frames: the un-corrected version of the diagnostic protocol (subject mostly looks the same as their old LinkedIn) so the gallery includes a "before" reference if needed.
- [ ] 20-30 frames.

---

## Wrap (5 min)

- [ ] Power down lights. Camera stays in hand.
- [ ] **Show one frame** on the camera back. Pick the strongest of the 5-8 from Beat 3. Show it for 2 seconds, no more. The Pixieset gallery is the reveal; the camera-back preview is the trust deposit.
- [ ] Confirm the delivery date in person: "Gallery in your inbox in 5 business days, [day, date]. Twenty retouched, password protected, 14-day window. Plus there'll be 10-15 additional images available for purchase for 48 hours after delivery · those are the extras that didn't make the contracted twenty but are still strong frames."
- [ ] Walk them out. Thank them. End the conversation.

---

## Same day · 4 hours after wrap

Run Phase 1 of `SOP_capture_to_delivery.md`. Same day, no exception. The 5-day SLA starts ticking from shoot end, not from the day you ingest.

---

## Failure modes (and the recovery)

| Failure | Cause | Recovery |
|---|---|---|
| Subject won't unfreeze in Beat 1 | Anxiety; pre-shoot brief didn't land | Add 2 more minutes of warm-up. Move to a chair / lean before standing. Talk about anything except the shoot. |
| Mechanics correct but no keepers | Beat 3 (energy) didn't land; pose is technically right but psychologically empty | Stop. Reset. "Tell me about the deck this is going on. Walk me through what you're announcing." Then: "That's the energy. Hold it. Frame." |
| You overshot 45 minutes | Subject getting pose-fatigue; quality drops past minute 50 | Wrap on time. The strongest frames are in Beat 3 anyway. The discipline of finishing on time is the operator move. |
| Lighting setup failed mid-shoot (battery, modifier blow-out) | Gear health issue | Have the second working setup pre-staged on a backup stand. Window + bedsheet is the no-fail fallback if a strobe dies. |
