# SOP · Discovery Call

**10-minute Calendly slot. Mom Test discipline. End with a commitment, an advancement, or a clean rejection. No zombie leads.**

The discovery call is a diagnostic conversation, not a sales call. The Reset offer is announced only when the diagnosis confirms fit. Before the call, the prospect has seen the VIB and has at least one specific protocol failure named.

---

## Pre-call (5 min before)

- [ ] Open the prospect's Outreach card · review trigger, protocol named, deployment context cited in DM
- [ ] Open their LinkedIn profile in a separate tab
- [ ] Open their company's About page or recent press
- [ ] Pull the Direction Stack one-pager PDF · ready to send post-call
- [ ] Open Calendly to confirm the 10-min slot
- [ ] Quiet space · headphones · camera off (audio call); they don't need to see your face during diagnosis · keep visual presence for the actual shoot
- [ ] Notepad or Notion side panel open: a single empty bullet list to take notes on three things only · past behavior · current state · stated commitment

---

## The call structure (10 min · 6 phases)

### Phase 1 · Open (60 sec)
**You speak first, briefly.** Confirm the appointment, state the call's purpose.

> "Hey [First name], BJ from Sniped Media. Thanks for the 10 minutes. The frame I sent you was the diagnostic. The next 10 minutes is just confirming whether the diagnostic actually fits before either of us commits to anything. Sound good?"

Wait for confirmation. Move on.

### Phase 2 · Their life · 3 minutes (Mom Test Rule 1)
**Talk about their life, not your idea.** Open with one question, then listen.

> "Walk me through how your visual presence comes up in your week. Where are people seeing you visually right now? LinkedIn, press, decks, podcast appearances?"

If they answer in generalities, anchor to specifics:
> "When was the last time you felt the photo you have wasn't doing the job?"

If they give a hypothetical, redirect to past:
> "Has there been a moment where you actually noticed it? Not where you should, where you did?"

**Listen for: deployment context (where the photo is showing up), recent triggers (a press feature coming, a fundraise, a podcast booking), and specific frustration language (their words, not yours).**

### Phase 3 · The diagnostic (2 min)
**Connect their stated context to the protocol you named in the VIB.**

> "Here's what the frame I sent you was doing. The protocol was [Protocol N · Name]. The reason that one matters specifically for you is · [link the protocol to the deployment context they just named]. So the gap shows up specifically on [their context]."

Wait. Let them respond. Most prospects either (a) confirm the diagnosis with "yeah, exactly" or (b) refine it to a slightly different context that's actually more useful.

### Phase 4 · Past behavior (Rule 2) · 2 min
**What have they actually tried, paid for, or considered?**

> "Have you done a photo session for this before? With who, what was the outcome?"

> "What didn't work about it?"

If they haven't tried: "What's the closest thing you've done?"

**You're listening for two signals:**
- They've already paid for a similar session and it disappointed → they understand the value, the price will not be the friction.
- They've never paid → the price will be the conversation, but commitment will be lower; harder to close, longer cycle.

### Phase 5 · The offer · 2 min
**Now you announce the Reset.** Not before. Not as a pitch. As the next logical step the diagnostic implies.

> "Based on what you just described, the move is The Reset. One look, one location, 45 minutes, twenty retouched in five business days. Fifteen hundred. Half on booking to lock the date, half on delivery."

**Stop. Wait. Let them speak.**

If they ask follow-up questions: answer factually, not defensively.

If they ask about price flexibility: hold the price.

> "The price is the price. Scope can change · fewer images, half-day rate, a stripped-down version · but the per-image standard doesn't move. What scope works for you?"

If they're ready to book: send Calendly link or schedule directly on the call.

### Phase 6 · Close · 1 min
**End with a commitment, an advancement, or a clean rejection.**

Three options, in order of preference:

**Option A · Commitment (book the date):**
> "Great. I'll send you the Reset MSA + the deposit invoice in the next hour. Once the deposit clears, the date is locked. Talk soon."

**Option B · Advancement (specific next step within 7 days):**
> "If [next step they've named · talking to spouse, internal sign-off, checking a calendar conflict] is what's holding it, when do you want to circle back? I'll put it on the calendar for [day]."
Get a specific day. Not "next week."

**Option C · Clean rejection:**
> "Sounds like the timing isn't right. No worries. If anything shifts, my contact info stays the same. Take care."
Mark Outreach card `Declined polite`. Move Pipeline to `Re-engage 90d`. End the conversation gracefully.

**The fourth option (zombie lead) is what you're refusing:**
> "Sounds interesting, let me think about it." → Reply: "Totally. If by [specific day] it still feels right, send me a yes; if not, no worries either way. I'll close the loop on this one."
Force the binary. Polite stalls cost more than explicit rejections.

## Post-call (5 min · same day)

- [ ] Update the Outreach card: Reply = `Yes · call held` · Discovery call date
- [ ] Update the Pipeline card: Status moves to `Discovery Booked` → `Proposal Sent` (if Option A) or `Closed Lost` (Option C) or stays `Discovery Booked` with a Next action date (Option B)
- [ ] If Option A: send the Reset MSA via HelloSign + Stripe deposit invoice. Subject: `Sniped Reset · contract + deposit · [Date]`
- [ ] If Option B: put the follow-up day on Calendar. Send a one-line confirmation DM: "Catching up [day] · if it shifts, ping me."
- [ ] If Option C: send the polite close DM. Move on.

## Mom Test discipline · the bad-data filter

If during the call the prospect:

| Says | Means | You do |
|---|---|---|
| "Great idea, love it" | Nothing. Compliments are free. | Redirect to specifics: "When was the last time you actually paid for a session?" |
| "I would totally book that" | A guess about future behavior | Redirect to past: "What did you do the last time you needed this?" |
| "I think I might want this for [hypothetical]" | Idea space, not behavior space | Test commitment: "What's the budget you've allocated to that?" |
| Asks for feature add-ons (more images, more locations) | Symptom of unclear value | Ask why: "Why is more images / more locations the variable that matters?" |
| Pushes on price | Either tire-kicking or scope mismatch | Hold price. Offer scope flexibility. Never discount the per-deliverable rate. |

---

## Conversion rate target

Phase 1 (months 1-3): **1 in 3 discovery calls converts to a booked Reset** (33%). If lower, the issue is upstream (VIB → DM → call setup); if higher, the discovery is over-qualifying and the funnel can be widened.

Phase 2 (months 4-6): **1 in 2** as the social proof and case studies stack.

Track `Conversion rate` as a Notion formula on the Pipeline DB: count(`Reset Booked` AND `Reset Delivered`) / count(`Discovery Booked`).
