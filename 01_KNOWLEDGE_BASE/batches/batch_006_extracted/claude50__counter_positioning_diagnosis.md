# Counter-Positioning Diagnosis

**Skill ID:** 1.5  
**Category:** Strategy & Competitive Analysis  
**Source Document(s):** 7_Powers_Strategic_Power_FRAMEWORKS.docx

## Purpose

Determine whether a challenger's business model advantage constitutes true Counter-Positioning versus other competitive dynamics, and classify the type of collateral damage preventing incumbent response.

## When to Use

When evaluating business operations, financial structure, or revenue growth opportunities.

## Instructions

Follow this workflow precisely. Each step is grounded in the source document(s) listed above. Do not skip steps. Do not invent frameworks, models, or terminology not present in the source material. Execute each step in order, using the exact logic and decision criteria documented.

## Workflow

1. Assess Stand-Alone Attractiveness: If the incumbent were a de novo entrant, would they invest in the challenger's business model? If NO, this is NOT Counter-Positioning.
2. If YES, assess Collateral Damage: Does the expected damage to the incumbent's existing business outweigh gains from adopting the new model?
3. If YES to collateral damage, classify the type: Milk (rational calculation, combined NPV negative), History's Slave (cognitive bias, overweighting known losses), or Job Security (agency problem, division heads protect their domain).
4. Determine whether multiple collateral damage types are operating simultaneously (they frequently are additive).
5. Estimate the timeline to the Capitulation Point: When will collateral damage decline enough that the incumbent belatedly adopts?
6. Recommend tactical pricing: Set prices low initially so the incumbent underestimates the model's profitability.

## Output Format

Produce all of the following deliverables:

- A binary determination: Counter-Positioning confirmed or denied, with reasoning
- Collateral damage classification (Milk, History's Slave, Job Security, or combination)
- Estimated capitulation timeline
- Tactical recommendations for the challenger
