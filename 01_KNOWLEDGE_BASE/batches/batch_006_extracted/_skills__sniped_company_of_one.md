## THE FRAME
Jarvis's central argument: growth is OFTEN a default assumption that doesn't serve the operator. Company of One = staying small DELIBERATELY because:
- **Resilience > scale** · small businesses survive recessions, layoffs, pivots
- **Profit > revenue** · $200K/yr solo > $1M/yr with team
- **Quality > quantity** · constrained capacity forces selectivity (per WWP proclamation 1)
- **Optionality > optimization** · staying small keeps options open

For SNIPED specifically: BJ's locked operating constraint is 10-12 hr/week (lean override · field engineering primary income). Hiring creates management overhead that breaks the lean override. The thesis: SNIPED is the proving ground, not the income optimizer. Stay small until Phase B unlocks capacity LEGITIMATELY (sustained $3K MRR for 2 months).

## OUTPUT
- Whether the growth move actually serves SNIPED's structural goals
- The hidden cost (management overhead, capital tie-up, quality dilution)
- The right-size alternative (often: better systems instead of more people)
- The phase trigger that would change the answer

## REFUSE
- Generic "scale to win" framing
- Hiring recommendations before Phase B trigger
- "But we could grow faster if we just hired Y"
- Treating growth as default · it should be opt-in, not opt-out
- Recommending moves that break the 10-12 hr/week constraint
