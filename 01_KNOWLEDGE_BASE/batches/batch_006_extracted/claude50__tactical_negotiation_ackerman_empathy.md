# Tactical Negotiation System (Ackerman + Tactical Empathy)

**Skill ID:** 16.1  
**Category:** Negotiation  
**Source Document(s):** NEGOTIATION_FRAMEWORKS.docx

## Purpose

Prepare and execute negotiations using the Tactical Empathy Stack (mirroring, labeling, calibrated questions, accusation audit) combined with the Ackerman Bargaining System for price-based negotiation.

## When to Use

When preparing for or conducting any high-stakes negotiation involving pricing, contracts, partnerships, or resource allocation.

## Instructions

Follow this workflow precisely. Each step is grounded in the source document(s) listed above. Do not skip steps. Do not invent frameworks, models, or terminology not present in the source material. Execute each step in order, using the exact logic and decision criteria documented.

## Workflow

1. Prepare the Negotiation One-Sheet: Set target price, prepare accusation audit (list every negative thing they could say about you), write 3-5 calibrated 'How'/'What' questions, prepare labels for expected emotions, identify their negotiator type (Analyst, Accommodator, or Assertive).
2. Calculate Ackerman plan: First offer at 65% of target, three raises at 85%, 95%, 100% of target. Prepare a nonmonetary item to add at the final number.
3. Open with tactical empathy: Deploy accusation audit first. Use late-night FM DJ voice for calm authority. Label their emotional state. Demonstrate you understand their world.
4. Listen and mirror: Repeat their last 1-3 critical words with upward inflection, then go silent. Gather information about priorities, constraints, and hidden stakeholders.
5. Label and summarize: Label emotions as they surface (especially negatives: 'It seems like...'). Build toward a summary that earns the breakthrough phrase 'That's right.'
6. Deploy calibrated questions: Use 'How am I supposed to do that?' when their offer is unacceptable. Use 'What about this is important to you?' to reveal underlying motivations. Give the illusion of control.
7. Bend reality: Anchor on loss aversion (present your offer as the thing that avoids their loss). Use precise nonround final numbers ($37,893, not $38,000). Pivot to nonmonetary terms.
8. Guarantee implementation: Apply Rule of Three (get agreement three times in different ways). Watch for incongruence between words, tone, and body (7-38-55 Rule). Use 'How' questions for implementation: 'How will we know we're on track?'
9. Hunt for Black Swans throughout: Listen for the 3-5 pieces of unknown information that could change everything. Watch for what is NOT said.

## Output Format

Produce all of the following deliverables:

- A completed Negotiation One-Sheet (target, accusation audit, calibrated questions, labels, Ackerman plan)
- Counterpart type classification (Analyst/Accommodator/Assertive) with adapted approach
- An Ackerman price ladder (65/85/95/100 with nonmonetary add)
- Black Swan detection notes from the negotiation
