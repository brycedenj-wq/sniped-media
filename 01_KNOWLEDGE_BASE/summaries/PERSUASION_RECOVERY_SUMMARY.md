## Key signal installed (Ariely's own named experiments)

1. **Relativity (Ch1):** we judge by comparison, not absolute worth (the cheap-pen / expensive-suit asymmetry).
2. **The decoy effect (Ch1):** an asymmetrically dominated option (the Economist subscription) reshapes which real option looks best.
3. **Arbitrary coherence / anchoring (Ch2):** the social-security-number auction · the first (arbitrary) price anchors all later willingness to pay (Tom Sawyer / Assael pearls).
4. **The high cost of zero cost (Ch3):** FREE is an emotional trigger that distorts demand (Amazon free shipping · Hershey's Kiss).
5. **Social norms vs market norms (Ch4):** introducing money into a social exchange breaks the relationship and is hard to undo.
6. **The hot-cold empathy gap (Ch5):** we underpredict (by 70 to 136 percent) how arousal/emotion changes our decisions.
7. **Procrastination and self-control (Ch6):** external, pre-committed deadlines beat willpower.
8. **The endowment effect (Ch7):** the Duke basketball-ticket study · we overvalue what we own (a ~14x sell/buy gap) via loss aversion.
9. **Keeping doors open (Ch8):** Xiang Yu burned his ships · we irrationally pay to keep dying options alive; closing weak doors focuses.
10. **The effect of expectations (Ch9):** belief and presentation physically change the experienced quality.
11. **The placebo of price (Ch10):** Veladone / SoBe · a higher price raises the real perceived effect; discounting can erode felt value.
12. **The context of our character (Ch11-12):** the fudge factor · honest people cheat a little, and moral reminders (not penalties) stop it.
13. **Predictable irrationality is systematic (intro + 2008 crisis):** biases are repeatable and persist in markets; arbitrage does not wash them out.
14. **Synthesis (015):** behavioral economics as honest decision-support and the free-lunch design (shape environments/defaults toward better decisions; watch your own biases) + the optionality guardrail.
