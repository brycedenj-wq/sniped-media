# Art Series. Frame 5. Fred Herzog.

*Production plan. Phase 2 of the series. Recreation of Man with Bandage, Vancouver, 1968.*

---

## 1. The original frame

Tight three-quarter portrait of a middle-aged white man on a Vancouver sidewalk. White gauze bandage wrapped around his head, partially over one eye. Dark coat. Stoic, not posing. He's just standing there. The storefront behind him is weathered, painted, vernacular. The frame is built around the geometry of the storefront. He stepped into it.

What's actually doing the work in the frame:

- **Lighting.** Late afternoon, ambient, shadowless. No hard light anywhere. No catchlight games. No rim. The mood comes from color saturation and tactile surface, not from contrast. This is the part most copyists miss. They shoot vintage signs in golden hour and call it Herzog. He killed the contrast on purpose.
- **Lens.** 35mm Leica. Tight crop on the body but immersive. You feel close to him without being on top of him. Not the 50mm portrait compression I default to. Wider, more environmental.
- **Composition.** Frame built first. Storefront signage and architecture are the structural pillars. The body sits inside that geometry, not in front of it. The man is roughly centered but the storefront text and shapes hold the frame together independent of him.
- **Pose.** None. He's standing. Hands at his sides. He didn't get directed. That is the opposite of how I work. Naming it now so I know what I'm walking into.
- **Color palette.** Muted. Olive-brown coat, beige skin, the white of the bandage as the brightest element, dirty-warm storefront tones behind him. Kodachrome saturation but restrained. Reds protected. Detail in shadow. This is the closest the palette comes to my monochromatic blocking, but it's restrained ambient color, not constructed studio color. Different mechanism, same target.
- **Wardrobe.** Whatever he was wearing. Lived-in. Not styled.

The bandage is the hook. Without it, the frame is a man on a street. With it, the viewer asks what happened. That question is what the picture runs on.

---

## 2. My version

The borrow from the study was specific: pre-framed geometry, ambient warm light, one piece of cultural specificity, color discipline. The lesson is the methodology, not the genre. So I'm not shooting street photography. I'm shooting a portrait the Herzog way.

**What stays faithful:**

- Ambient late-afternoon shadowless light. No strobes on this shoot. That's the discipline check.
- Vernacular signage or weathered storefront as the architectural pillar of the frame. The frame gets built before the subject walks in.
- Restrained color palette. Muted. No saturated monochromatic torque. The location and wardrobe carry the palette together.
- The subject doesn't get directed into a pose. Closest move I have to the Herzog "just stand" is the Avedon shoot-the-after move from Frame 1. Let them get bored. Shoot what's left.
- A visual hook on the subject that asks a question without explaining itself. Equivalent to the bandage. Not a literal bandage, that's cosplay. Could be a tool they hold, a uniform, a visible scar, an instrument case. Something that makes the viewer ask what they do.

**What bends toward SNIPED:**

- The body stays the subject. The study said it directly: don't shrink the human. I'm a portrait photographer, not a street photographer. The subject fills the frame the way Herzog framed the man, not the way he framed his sign-density street shots where the human is small.
- Minimal direction is still direction. Chin forward and down for the jawline, hands given a task. Then I leave them alone. I'm not abandoning the Direction Stack on this frame, I'm using a quiet version of it.
- LA specificity instead of Vancouver specificity. The study said the missing piece in my work is cultural anchor, that there's nothing in the frame that says LA, says 2026, says me. This is the frame to fix that. Crenshaw, Leimert Park, or Boyle Heights. Vernacular signage from the 70s and 80s that's still standing. That's the closest LA gets to Herzog's Vancouver.
- Retouch stays clinical. I'm not faking film grain. I'm not simulating Kodachrome. Pull my usual SNIPED edit back about 30%. Skin still clean. Color casts still equalized. But no fashion-magazine finish on a frame that's supposed to feel like the room it was shot in.

The bet is that emotional weight can come from ambient light, the right subject, and a real location, instead of from a studio key and a rim and a polished retouch. If it doesn't, the frame fails and that's the lesson.

## 3. The production

**Subject.** Not a model. Not a friend with style. Someone whose face holds weight without me directing it. Candidates:

- A barber on Crenshaw or in Leimert
- A musician at the World Stage or near the jazz row
- A shop owner with a face that's been on the corner for twenty years
- An elder. Someone whose face is the work, not the wardrobe.

Find them by walking the block. Not by DMing models. Pay them or trade prints. Don't make this a favor.

The visual hook needs to be solved at the casting stage, not on set. Whatever the subject brings (the apron, the scar, the instrument case, the uniform), that's the bandage equivalent. If they don't have one, keep looking. The frame collapses without it.

**Location.** Crenshaw Boulevard between Vernon and Stocker, or Leimert Park Village around 43rd Place. Boyle Heights along Cesar Chavez or 1st Street is the backup. The criteria: weathered storefront signage, painted text or hand-lettered window, palette in the muted-warm family. Scout it 2-3 days before. Walk it at 5pm to verify the light. Note 2-3 candidate storefronts. The frame is built before the subject is locked.

**Wardrobe.** Subject brings their own. Tonal family aligned with the location, but I'm not styling it. If they show up dressed up, I send them back. The Herzog rule is whatever the subject was already wearing. If I style a corner-store coat that's a SNIPED frame in Herzog drag.

**Time of day.** 4:30 to 5:30pm, LA, spring. Sun behind the buildings, sky still bright, open shade on the storefront. Not golden hour for the warm glow. Late afternoon for the shadowlessness. Overcast is even better if it shows up.

**Lighting.**

- No strobes. No Godox. That's the discipline.
- One 5-in-1 reflector, white side, only if the eyes go too dark.
- One 4x4 silk in the bag in case direct sun creeps in. Probably won't get used.

That's it. Removing the kit is the test.

**Camera and lens.** EOS RP, 35mm if I have it (rent the 35 1.4 if not, it's the right move). Otherwise the 24-105 at 35. f/4 to f/5.6. ISO 200-400. Shutter whatever the ambient gives. Handheld. No tripod. Shore was a tripod-locked frame. This one is a body-and-camera frame. The working method should feel different.

**Crew.** Just me, or me plus one for the reflector. Solo is closer to the Herzog method but if a second set of eyes helps, fine. Don't bring a styling team. The whole point is what's already there.

**Session length.** 30-45 minutes on the corner once the frame is locked. The build (scouting, framing, lighting check) is most of the time. The actual shoot is short. Same Herzog discipline as the Eggleston frame. Build the room, shoot fast, walk.

## 4. The post

Same carousel as the prior four. No reason to break the pattern.

- **Slide 1.** Recreation, full bleed, no text.
- **Slide 2.** The original Herzog. Caption-credited under the image: *Fred Herzog. Man with Bandage. Vancouver, 1968.*
- **Slide 3.** Text card. Two or three lines on what the exercise was for.

Optional fourth slide: a behind-the-scenes detail. Not the lighting setup, because there isn't one, that's the joke. Could be a wider frame of the storefront with the subject in it, showing how much environment was actually used. Skip it if it doesn't land.

Caption template:

```
Frame 5 of the Art Series. Fred Herzog, 
Man with Bandage, Vancouver, 1968.

Recreation: [subject name, trade]. 
[Neighborhood], Los Angeles, [date].

What I was studying: emotional weight 
without dramatic light. Ambient warm 
afternoon. No strobes. Frame built

piece of cultural specificity in the 
geometry instead of seamless paper.

What worked: [one line, specific.]

What didn't: [one line, honest. The

```

Under 100 words. The "what didn't work" line is non-negotiable on this frame. This is the first study where I'm deliberately removing a tool I rely on, and the honest version of how that went is the whole point of posting the series.

## 5. Timeline

Two weeks. Casting and scouting are the slow parts. The shoot is fast.

**Days 1-3. Casting and scout.**

Walk Crenshaw, Leimert, and Boyle Heights at 5pm on three different days. Find 2-3 storefronts with the right palette and signage. Talk to people. The barber, the shop owner, the musician on the corner. Pitch them the trade. Goal: subject locked by day 3, with the visual hook already identified at casting (the apron, the instrument, the uniform, whatever it is). Backup subject on standby.

**Day 4. Prep and reference.**

Print the Herzog. Tape it where I'll see it before the shoot. Pull palette reference from the location scout (a few iPhone shots of the storefront I picked, at the target hour). Confirm subject and wardrobe. Decide which storefront is primary, which is backup if the light shifts. Put the 35mm in the bag and confirm I haven't accidentally packed the strobes.

**Day 5 or 6. Shoot.**

Arrive 60 minutes before target light. Walk the block. Confirm the storefront still looks how it did three days ago (LA neighborhoods change fast). Lock the framing. Subject arrives. Let them stand around for 10 minutes before I shoot. Talk. Shoot 30-50 frames. Walk.

**Days 7-8. Edit.**

Cull to one frame. The retouch is the harder discipline than the shoot on this one. Pull my normal SNIPED edit back about 30%. Don't push saturation. Don't add contrast. Don't add grain. Skin gets cleaned but not sculpted. The frame should look like the corner felt.

**Day 9. Audit gate.**

Run the 8-criteria on the strongest frame. The tests on this one:

- Emotional weight 8+ without rim or key light: lesson took.
- Color palette 7+ with restrained ambient palette (not the usual monochromatic torque): lesson took.
- Composition 8+ with the storefront geometry holding the frame independent of the subject: lesson took.
- If the frame got polished into a SNIPED-with-a-storefront-backdrop: I over-finished. Pull back and re-edit. If it still reads as my normal studio work with a warmer location, the lesson didn't take. Hold and reshoot.

**Day 10. Build carousel. Write caption.**

**Day 11 or 12. Post.**

**Days 13-14. Slack.**

If a subject falls through on day 4, the timeline shifts a week. Don't grab a model to hit the deadline. The frame collapses if the subject is fake. Same rule as the Avedon shoot.

## The audit gate

This is the frame where I deliberately remove my strongest tool. The whole point is to find out what's left. The audit before posting:

- **Pass.** Emotional weight 8+, color palette 7+, composition 8+, and no strobe-shaped light on the face. Post.
- **Hold.** Frame got polished into a regular SNIPED portrait with a Crenshaw backdrop. Lesson missed. Pull the edit back, repost the audit. If it still reads polished, hold the post and try the edit again next week.
- **Reshoot.** Frame is flat, light is under, subject didn't have the hook. The whole picture failed because the casting failed or the light shifted. Reshoot in week 3.

Better to post nothing than to post a SNIPED frame in Herzog drag. The series is supposed to show movement. A frame that looks the same as Frame 1 means I didn't move.
