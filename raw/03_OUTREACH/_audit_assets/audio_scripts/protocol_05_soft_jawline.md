# Protocol 05 · Soft Jawline

**The read:** slack mouth, jaw not engaged, lower face has no definition. Photo reads "neutral expression" but lands as undefined.

**Most common in:** photographers who direct only the smile (and the operator either smiles or doesn't, with nothing in between).

---

## Observation · ~75 sec

```
Protocol 05 · Soft Jawline. The lower half of your face has no
engagement.

This is the protocol most photographers create by accident. They
direct the smile · either tell the operator to smile, or they
don't. What they never direct is the jaw.

What happens in a photo where the smile is removed but the jaw
isn't engaged: the mouth goes slack, the lower face loses
definition, the photo reads as neutral but lands as undefined.
Not stern. Not warm. Just absent.

The jaw is what makes the difference between a serious portrait
and a flat one. A jaw with slight engagement · the teeth lightly
together, the lower lip just barely active · gives the entire
lower face structure. Without it, the face is reading as one
plane.

You can usually spot this in profile or three-quarter shots
where the jawline should be visible against the neck and isn't.
The line is there anatomically. The photo just isn't catching
it because the jaw isn't engaged.
```

## Implication · ~65 sec

```
On a press cycle, the soft jawline reads as caught off guard.
A reporter using your photo for a story wants you to look
intentional, not in between expressions. The slack jaw signals
in between.

For founder-spotlight pieces, conference speaker promo, podcast
thumbnails · the eye reads jaw definition as command. An
operator with a defined jaw is read as someone who's about to
say something. An operator with a slack jaw is read as waiting
for the question.

The smile vs no-smile decision is downstream of this. Whether
the operator smiles or doesn't, the jaw needs engagement for
the photo to land. Otherwise the smile reads forced (Protocol 07)
or the no-smile reads checked out (Protocol 10).
```

## The Fix · ~55 sec

```
The fix is small and physical. Teeth just touching · not
clenched, just contact. Lips closed but not pressed. Jaw active.

The cue I give on a shoot: "tongue to the roof of your mouth,
teeth lightly touching." It sounds clinical but the result is
immediate. The lower face activates. The jawline becomes
visible. The photo gains structure.

This works for serious portraits and for smiling portraits.
For the no-smile variant, the engaged jaw gives the face
authority. For the smile variant, the engaged jaw keeps the
smile from being all teeth · it gets contained, considered,
intentional.

Two seconds of physical adjustment. Different photo.
```
