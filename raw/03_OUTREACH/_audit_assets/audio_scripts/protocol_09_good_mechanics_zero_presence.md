# Protocol 09 · Good Mechanics, Zero Presence

**The read:** technically fine photo · posture okay, smile okay, lighting okay, framing okay · but no operator gravity. The photo reads "stock" or "competent professional" instead of "person who runs things."

**Most common in:** the diagnosis. Most VP and Director tier operators land here. Justin Fiaschetti's photo. Most defense-tier founders. The protocol is the gap.

---

## Observation · ~85 sec

```
Protocol 09 · Good Mechanics, Zero Presence. This is the most
common diagnosis at your tier, and it's the most important one
to understand.

What's happening in your photo: nothing's technically wrong.
The posture is okay. The mechanics are okay. The smile, if
there is one, is reasonable. The lighting is fine. The framing
is professional. The wardrobe is appropriate. By every
photo-quality metric, this is a good photo.

But it's not doing work.

The operator gravity isn't there. The photo reads competent
but not commanding. It reads as a person who has a VP title
rather than a person who operates at the level of a VP. The
mechanics check the boxes. The presence doesn't show up.

This is the protocol where the camera is the only thing in
the room thinking about the photo. The operator showed up,
let the photographer do their job, and the photo reflects
exactly that · the operator showing up. Not the operator at
their working register.

The hardest part of this protocol is that nobody flagged it
for you. The photographer delivered. The shoot was professional.
Friends and family said the photo looks great. And by the
standard everybody applied, it does. But the standard everybody
applied is "good professional photo," not "operator photo with
gravity." Different standard. Different result.
```

## Implication · ~80 sec

```
This is the protocol that undersells operators most at the VP
and founder tier. Specifically.

The photo gets used everywhere. Hiring page, company about,
press cycle, podcast appearances, board decks, sales side
proof, partnership announcements. On every one of those
surfaces, the photo's job is to suggest authority before the
viewer reads the bio.

Protocol 09 photos make the viewer read the bio to get the
authority. The photo doesn't lead. The photo confirms what
the bio says.

For a founder mid-launch · this is the critical gap. The press
is showing your face before they show your product. If the
face reads as "competent founder" instead of "founder
operating at the altitude the company needs to operate at,"
the press cycle undersells you. Press writers can feel the
gap even if they can't name it.

For VP hiring · candidates click through to the company about
page before they apply. They visually rank the leadership.
Protocol 09 makes the VP indistinguishable from senior IC. The
hierarchy that should be readable visually is invisible. Senior
candidates filter out before they even read the bio.

For board surfaces · the photo gets used in pitch decks, fund
updates, ops reviews. Protocol 09 reads as "this operator is
fine." That's not the read you want when fundraising or asking
for stretch trust.

This is the high-value protocol fix. The mechanics are already
working. The gap is just presence. Once that's added, every
surface the photo lands on gets the upgrade.
```

## The Fix · ~70 sec

```
The fix is presence direction, not mechanics direction.

Most photographers default to mechanics direction. Stand here.
Tilt your head. Smile. Move your shoulder. The result is good
mechanics. Protocol 09.

Presence direction is different. It targets the internal state
of the operator, not the external position. The cue isn't "stand
this way." The cue is "operate this way."

On a SNIPED shoot, I'll prompt for specific operator states.
Sometimes it's "you've just walked into a meeting where the
team is waiting for you to say what you think." Sometimes it's
"the next sentence out of your mouth is the one the whole company
needs to hear." Sometimes it's a memory · "the last hard decision
you made."

The body responds. Jaw engages. Eyes shift. Shoulders shift.
The photo catches an operator in a real internal moment, not
in a posed external position.

Same person, same wardrobe, same lighting, same composition.
Presence direction · different photo. The operator gravity is
in the eye contact, the jaw, the breathing pattern, the
shoulders. Once the internal state is right, the mechanics are
secondary.

This is the protocol the entire Direction Stack methodology was
built to fix. Most of the operators I work with come in at
Protocol 09 and leave with portraits that operate at their
actual tier.
```
