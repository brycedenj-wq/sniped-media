# Reply Scripts · 7 Common Reply Types

Last updated: 2026-05-12

These are templates for the most common reply types in Unibox. Goal: positive replies answered within 2 hours, no friction, move to call when fit.

Plain text. No em dashes. Same operator voice.

---

## 1. "yes send it" / "yes I'd take a look" (positive · audit requested)

Action: Queue the Higgsfield Loom production · target delivery within 24-48 hours. Don't promise a specific time, but stay tight.

```
Cool. Pulling your LinkedIn now, I'll send the Loom over in the next 24-48 hours.

Anything specific you want me to look at (hiring page, press cycle, conference speaking)? Optional, I'll cover the basics either way.

· BJ
```

Word count: ~35.

**Logic:** Acknowledge, set expectation, ask one optional question that lets them frame the use case (which informs how you record the Loom focus). Don't overdo it.

---

## 2. "what do you do?" / "what's this exactly?" (curious but unclear)

Action: One-paragraph explainer, redirect to the Loom offer.

```
Quick context: I run a portrait studio in DTLA called SNIPED. We do executive and founder portraits using a 10-protocol diagnostic framework I built called the Direction Stack · breaks down where a photo is working vs falling short.

The Loom is just me walking through your current photo against the 10 protocols. No pitch attached, just the read.

Want me to send it over?

· BJ
snipedmedia.com
```

Word count: ~80.

**Logic:** Explain the business in one paragraph, restate the offer, ask the same question again. The CTA is the SAME · we don't drift.

---

## 3. "send info" / "send me a brochure" / "what are your rates"

Action: Don't send pricing. Redirect to the audit-then-call path.

```
No brochure on this one · the Loom is the way I do this. 5 min of recorded walkthrough on your specific photo against the framework, you decide from there whether it's worth a longer conversation.

Want me to record it?

· BJ
```

Word count: ~50.

**Logic:** Refuse to send static info. The Loom IS the info. Methodology-first selling per Win Without Pitching · refusing to be commoditized into a brochure-and-quote flow.

If they push back: "fair · the Reset is $1,500 for 1 look and 20 final images, 5-day turnaround. But the Loom is free and tells you whether the gap is worth the spend before the conversation. Worth 5 min?"

---

## 4. "not now" / "maybe later" / "thanks but not interested right now"

Action: Don't push. Don't try to convert. Move to warm-but-not-now list.

```
All good. I'll keep you on my warm list and ping you when the public Direction Stack framework drops on my site · should be useful regardless of timing.

· BJ
```

Word count: ~30.

**Logic:** No fight. No push. The next-quarter founder converts on goodwill, not pressure.

---

## 5. "already have someone" / "we have a photographer"

Action: Acknowledge, position the diagnostic as additive not replacing.

```
Got it. The Loom isn't a sales pitch for replacing them · it's a read on what the current photo is doing on your hiring / press surfaces against the 10-protocol framework. Useful for the briefing you give whoever's currently shooting you.

If you want it for that reason, happy to record. If not, no worries.

· BJ
```

Word count: ~65.

**Logic:** Reframe from "buy from us" to "use our diagnostic to brief your current photographer." This is a NON-COMPETING offer · low-friction yes even for prospects who are loyal to their current shooter.

A non-trivial number of these will become customers later. The current photographer relationship is rarely permanent.

---

## 6. "no budget" / "can't afford it"

Action: They haven't seen pricing yet · this is preemptive defensive. Diffuse.

```
Loom is free, no spend on this one. If the diagnostic surfaces a worthwhile gap, that's the moment to talk about whether and when to do something about it · not before.

Should I record it?

· BJ
```

Word count: ~45.

**Logic:** They're objecting to a price they haven't been told. Clarify the offer is free. Reframe the entire conversation around "let's see if there's a problem first." This is Gap Selling 101 · no problem confirmed yet = no need to pitch the spend.

---

## 7. Negative / hostile / "stop emailing me" / unsubscribe-adjacent

Action: Acknowledge, suppress, never email again.

```
Got it, sorry to bother. You won't hear from me again.

· BJ
```

Word count: ~12.

**Logic:** Don't argue. Don't defend. Suppress in Instantly + Unibox. Clean exit. The list scrubs itself · every negative reply is the system telling you who NOT to keep emailing.

If they're particularly hot ("aggressive language, threats"): forward to Instantly support, suppress at the inbox level.

## Reply triage decision tree (for Ren)

```
Reply lands in Unibox
        │
        ▼
Is it positive (audit requested)?
    ├─ YES → Reply 1 (24-48hr Loom commit) · queue Higgsfield production
    └─ NO ▼
        Is it a question?
            ├─ "what do you do?" → Reply 2 (explainer + re-offer)
            └─ NO ▼
                Is it deflecting?
                    ├─ "send info" → Reply 3 (no brochure, re-offer Loom)
                    ├─ "not now" → Reply 4 (warm exit)
                    ├─ "already have someone" → Reply 5 (reframe as additive)
                    ├─ "no budget" → Reply 6 (Loom is free)
                    └─ Hostile → Reply 7 (clean exit + suppress)
```

Ren handles 1-7 directly. Anything ambiguous · escalate to BJ via Slack with the original email + draft reply.

## Reply SLA

- Positive replies: within 2 hours (target · 30 min if possible)
- Question replies: within 4 hours
- Deflection replies: within 24 hours
- Negative replies: within 24 hours

Ren checks Unibox 3x daily: 9 AM, 1 PM, 5 PM. Anything urgent slacks BJ.

---

## What we're NOT doing in replies

- No long defensive paragraphs
- No premature pricing
- No multi-step pitch sequences
- No salesy "looking forward to hearing from you!" closers
- No follow-up follow-ups · one nudge after a 3-day silence on a positive reply, then drop
- No em dashes
