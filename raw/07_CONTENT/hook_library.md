# Hook library · openers for every content surface

**Hooks are the first 8-12 words of any post. They decide whether the reader scrolls past or stops. Pull from this library; do not write hooks from scratch.**

The library is structured by content type. Pull a hook, plug into the relevant scaffold from `/07_CONTENT/caption_templates.md`, fill the rest.

Refresh quarterly. Retire hooks that have been used 3+ times within 90 days. Add new hooks when a protocol or shoot produces a strong line.

---

## Direction Stack protocol hooks (LinkedIn primary · 30+ posts can be built from these)

### Generic protocol-named openers

- Most LA founders fail at one specific layer.
- There are 10 protocols. Most photographers do not run any of them.
- The camera reads two layers. Most founders fail one or both.
- The fix is not a better photographer.
- Pricing is not the problem. Direction is.
- Headshots fail in the same 6 mechanical and 4 psychological ways.
- The 10-protocol diagnostic exists because most founders cannot self-diagnose what is wrong with their LinkedIn header.
- A founder photo failing at Protocol [N] reads to investors as [characteristic].
- The diagnostic takes 8 minutes. The corrected frame takes 45.

### Per-protocol hooks (one per of the 10 protocols, mechanical + psychological)

**Mechanical · Protocol 1 · Claw Hands**
- Most founder photos fail before the camera even fires · Claw Hands.
- If your hands are doing this in your LinkedIn header, the photo is reading as anxious.

**Protocol 2 · Locked Shoulders**
- The shoulders carry the photo. Most founders' shoulders are locked.
- Locked Shoulders is what makes a $5,000 photo read like a $300 one.

**Protocol 3 · Squared**
- Squared is the protocol that separates a press cover from a passport photo.
- If your shoulders are squared at the camera, you are losing the entire dimension of the frame.

**Protocol 4 · Pinned Arms**
- Pinned Arms is what makes a founder photo read as defensive.
- The arms are not props. Pinned Arms is the most-failed protocol on LinkedIn.

**Protocol 5 · Soft Jawline**
- Soft Jawline is correctable in 30 seconds on set. Most photographers do not correct it.
- The jaw is not a vanity protocol. It is the structure the eye reads first.

**Protocol 6 · Spinal Collapse**
- Spinal Collapse is what reads as "smaller-than-the-business" on LinkedIn.
- The spine carries the entire posture. Most founder photos fail at the spine.

**Psychological · Protocol 7 · Forced Smile**
- The forced smile is the psychological tell that the photographer did not run a methodology.
- A forced smile reads to investors as performative. The fix is not "more candid."

**Protocol 8 · Transition Freeze**
- Transition Freeze is what happens when a founder stops between poses and the photographer keeps shooting.
- Most founder photo galleries are 60% Transition Freeze frames.

**Protocol 9 · Good Mechanics Zero Presence**
- A founder can pass every mechanical protocol and still fail the photo.
- Good mechanics, zero presence, is the most expensive failure mode.

**Protocol 10 · Full Shutdown**
- Full Shutdown is the 4th-pose moment when the founder retreats inside.
- The 90-second opener exists to prevent Full Shutdown.

### Anti-pattern openers (the contrarian frame)

- Lighting is overrated. Direction is the lever.
- Equipment is not your bottleneck.
- A $300 headshot and a $5,000 portrait fail at the same protocol.
- The reason your LinkedIn photo is not converting is not the photo.
- The 6-month wait list at the famous LA studio is not buying you better photos · it is buying you the same protocol failures with better lighting.

## Hooks that are banned (never use)

- "Excited to share..." (creator-economy register)
- "Big news..." (clickbait register)
- "Let me explain..." (passive register)
- "Quick story..." (sets up something that has to deliver)
- "Did you know..." (lecture register)
- "Hot take..." (Twitter-bro register)
- "Unpopular opinion..." (Twitter-bro register)
- "Here's the truth..." (preachy register)
- Any hook starting with "I" (passive · open with the subject of the post, not yourself)

## Quarterly refresh cadence

Last Monday of every quarter:
1. Pull all hooks used in the last 90 days from Notion (or scroll LinkedIn / IG manually if Notion logging is not yet automated).
2. Mark hooks used 3+ times → retire from rotation for 90 days.
3. Add new hooks from the past quarter's protocols, shoots, conversations.
4. Total active hook bank should sit at 60-100 hooks across all categories.

The library never runs dry if quarterly refresh runs. If a quarter goes by with no refresh, the rotation stales and the audience starts hearing repetition. Calendar reminder.
