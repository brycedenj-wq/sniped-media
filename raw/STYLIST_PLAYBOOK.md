# THE STYLIST PLAYBOOK

The operational guide. Pairs with the Style Manifesto.

Manifesto = principles (fit, proportion, color, why protocol).
Playbook = process (how to style a subject in real time on a shoot).

Drop both into any chat to give Claude the foundation to style any shoot, any model, any brief.

---

## I. ROLE CLARITY

Before any shoot, know which seat you're in:

- **Solo**: photographer + stylist + sometimes director, all you. Most of your portrait and brand work
- **Lead with assist**: you're directing wardrobe, with someone pulling and steaming. Mid-tier shoots
- **Stylist as collaborator**: outside stylist owns wardrobe, you own camera. Editorial and bigger-budget brand work
- **Subject styled themselves**: client showed up with their stuff. Common in headshots and quick portraits

The role determines authority. If you're solo, you call the look. If there's a stylist on the job, you support unless something is fighting the camera.

---

## II. PRE-PRODUCTION

### The brief
Every shoot has a brief, even if it's a 10-minute call. Get answers to:

- **Purpose**: LinkedIn headshot, brand launch, banquet, portfolio, lookbook, magazine
- **End use**: web, print, billboard, square social, vertical story
- **Tone**: editorial, commercial, portrait, lifestyle, fashion, documentary
- **Audience**: who sees this and what should they think
- **Wardrobe**: who's pulling, who's paying, what's the budget
- **Schedule and location**: indoor/outdoor, time of day, multiple looks, light setup

If the client can't answer these in 5 minutes, you're filling them in.

### The mood
Build a moodboard before pulling clothes. References from Pinterest, Are.na, photographer portfolios, brand campaigns, films. Show 6-12 images. Get the client to react: "this yes, this no, why." That conversation is worth ten hours of guessing on shoot day.

### Pull / sourcing
Where the clothes come from:

- **Client's own closet**: most common for portrait and headshot. Have them send photos 48 hours out. Curate to keepers
- **Shopping with returns**: buy at retailers with strong return policies, shoot with tags tucked, return after. Common for brand work
- **Brand pulls**: PR samples for editorial. Need contacts at the brand or publication masthead
- **Rental houses**: in LA, Western Costume, Mister Mort, Costume Rentals Corporation. Day rates, deposits, cleaning fees
- **Thrift / vintage**: for character pieces, period work, textural depth retail doesn't have
- **Your own kit**: the basics every working stylist owns (white tee, cream button-down, neutral chino, classic blazer, basic accessories in standard sizes)

Pull 2-3x what you'll actually shoot. Options collapse fast in the fitting.

### Wardrobe approval
Before shoot day, get client signoff on:
- Top 3-4 looks per subject
- The "hero" look (most likely to be the lead image)
- Backup tops in case of stain, sweat, or fit failure on the day

Lock it via email or text. Avoids the on-set "I changed my mind" moment.

## III. THE ON-SET KIT

Don't show up without it.

### Fit and fix
- Wonder Clips and binder clips, range of sizes
- Safety pins, multiple sizes, gold and silver
- Topstick / hollywood tape (double-sided)
- Lint roller, two of them
- Mini sewing kit, pre-threaded needles in white, black, navy, brown
- Iron-on hem tape
- Static spray
- Clear nail polish (stops runs, seals frayed threads)
- Small sharp scissors

### Garment care
- Steamer (handheld is fine)
- Spray bottle with water
- Tide pen, OxiClean travel
- Hand wipes, dry towels
- Lint shaver / fabric razor

### Subject comfort
- Bandaids and blister patches
- Clear stick deodorant (no transfer)
- Hand sanitizer
- Tissues
- Bobby pins, hair ties, small comb
- Mints
- Bottled water

### Backup wardrobe (kit basics)
- 2-3 white tees, different fits, M/L
- 1 cream button-down, M/L
- 1 black tee, 1 navy crewneck
- Clean white socks, multiple
- A neutral belt

These save shoots when something the client brought fails.

### Tech and ops
- Cloth tape measure
- Phone with reference images saved offline
- Notebook for fit notes per look
- Garment bags for transport

## IV. SUBJECT ASSESSMENT

You have 5-10 minutes when subject arrives. Read for:

### Body proportion
- Long torso vs long legs (changes pant rise, jacket length, where to put waistline)
- Shoulder width relative to hips (changes silhouette balance)
- Frame size: delicate, average, larger (affects scale of patterns and accessories)
- Neck length (changes collar choice and layering)

You're not measuring them. You're eyeballing how the manifesto's fit rules apply to this specific body.

### Coloring
- Skin undertone: warm (golden, peachy), cool (pink, blue), or neutral
- Hair tone and contrast level against skin
- Eye color

This drives wardrobe color picks. The manifesto's color rules apply, tuned to this person's read.

### Comfort signals
- How they stand: slumped, square, locked up
- How they handle clothes: "oh I'd never wear that"
- What they walked in wearing (their natural style baseline)
- Energy: tired, anxious, excited, professional

Don't fight a subject's comfort zone hard. Stretch it 20%, not 200%. If they're stiff in a piece, the camera reads it.

### The four questions to ask in 60 seconds
1. "What do you usually feel best in?"
2. "Anything you're insecure about so I know what to avoid?"
3. "What's one thing you wish people noticed about you?"
4. "Any wardrobe must-not-wears?"

Those four save the shoot.

## V. THE FITTING

The 10-minute version of the principles.

### Setup
- Private space: bathroom, dressing area, or sectioned-off room with a clothing rack
- Full-length mirror
- Garments steamed and ready, hung in shoot order
- Subject brings own underwear and foundation pieces

### Order of operations per look
1. Bottoms first. Get rise, length, fit settled
2. Top second. Tucked or untucked decided here
3. Footwear. Confirms silhouette balance
4. Outerwear if applicable
5. Accessories last. Accent only after the base is locked

### Fit checks (every look, every time)
- Shoulder seam landing right
- No pulling, gaping, twisting
- Proportion: loose-tight balance, waist defined
- Length: hems hitting where they should
- Movement: have them sit, stand, walk, raise arms. Anything wrong shows up in motion

### Fast fixes
- **Too big in waist**: clip in back with a binder clip, hide under jacket
- **Sleeves too long**: fold and pin inside, or cuff on purpose
- **Pant too long**: pin a temporary hem from inside, or tuck into boot
- **Shirt collar gaping**: topstick to chest
- **Bra strap showing**: topstick to skin or pin to garment
- **Static cling**: spray, or rub a dryer sheet on the inside

### Escalate vs accept
- **Escalate (swap the piece)**: shoulder fit wrong, pulling at chest or hips, color clashing on skin, fabric reading cheap on monitor
- **Accept (move on)**: minor sleeve length, slight wrinkles, seat fit on a piece worn under a jacket, things the lens won't see

## VI. STYLING BY SHOOT TYPE

Different jobs, different rules.

### Headshots / professional portraits
- Tight crop. Shoulders up dominates. Neck and collar matter most
- Solid colors near the face. No busy patterns
- Skin-flattering tones. Avoid pure white shirts in flat light
- One strong color OR one strong texture, not both
- Layer for depth: blazer over knit, knit over collared shirt
- Watch / chain visible only if intentional
- Background neutral unless concept calls otherwise

### Brand / lifestyle
- Wardrobe matches brand voice. Read their existing visuals, copy the feel
- Multiple looks per subject. Plan transitions: jacket on/off, layer changes
- Color story across looks should flow. Don't bounce between palettes
- Subject should look like a real person, not a costume

### Editorial / fashion
- Concept first. Wardrobe serves the story
- Bigger swings: statement pieces, dramatic proportions, color play
- Subject's comfort zone matters less here, but still read it
- Pulls are larger, looks are more
- Hair / makeup aligns to wardrobe concept

### Event photography (banquets, galas, social events)
- Subjects are not models. They're guests in their own clothes
- Your job isn't styling, it's catching them at their best
- You can offer light direction before shutter: fix collar, button blazer, adjust posture
- For principals (host, honoree, speakers): advance a wardrobe call so you can light it well

### Personal portfolio / model work
- Highest creative latitude
- Use this to test ideas. Pull beyond your usual lanes. Build references
- Pulls can be wild. Returns or rentals only

## VII. LENS-AWARE STYLING

Different focal lengths show different things.

### 35mm and wider (full body, environmental)
- Whole silhouette reads. Proportion matters most
- Footwear in frame. Don't skip the shoe
- Shape of outfit beats details
- Loud patterns OK if cut clean

### 50mm to 85mm (mid to portrait)
- Waist-up dominant. Top half wardrobe is the headline
- Texture reads strongly. Fabric quality is visible
- Accessories show. Watch, chain, sunglasses all in frame
- Posture and styling cleanup matters: collar straight, no twisted seams

### 85mm to 135mm and up (tight portrait)
- Crop is shoulders or face only
- Color near face is everything
- Hair, skin, neckline, jewelry
- Below-crop wardrobe doesn't matter for that frame, matters for the next

### Wide aperture, shallow depth
- Background blurs. Texture in wardrobe stands out by contrast
- Subject needs detail to compete with bokeh

### Phone vs camera
- Phone compresses less. Patterns and busyness read sharper
- Long lens with shallow DOF hides minor wrinkles and fit. Phone shows them

## VIII. ON-SET WORKFLOW

How the day actually runs.

### Between shots
- Lint roll subject. Quick sweep
- Check collar, shirt tail, jacket button (rebutton if standing)
- Coordinate with hair touch-ups
- Refresh deodorant, water, mints if needed

### Between looks
- Subject changes private. Don't rush them
- Pre-stage next look on rack, ready to go
- Steam anything that wrinkled in transit
- Full fit check again. Don't assume the second look fits because the first did

### When something breaks
- **Stain**: blot first, treat second. Tide pen for small. Swap if visible
- **Rip**: pin from inside if hidden. Swap if visible
- **Sweat-through**: change shirt, dry the body, fresh deodorant
- **Subject shutdown** (overwhelmed, tired, can't perform): pause, water, ten minutes off camera. Pushing through a tired subject shows in the shots

### Continuity
- Phone photo of every approved look on the subject before shooting. Check tucks, cuffs, accessory placement
- Multi-day shoots: photograph every setup so day 2 matches day 1

## IX. WORKING WITH SUBJECTS

Most of styling is people management.

### Modesty and respect
- Ask before adjusting clothes on the subject's body. "Can I fix your collar?"
- Don't reach into anyone's clothes uninvited
- Bra straps, underwear lines, bodies: clinical, fast hand, or hand it to the subject to fix themselves

### Insecurities
- Ask early so you can avoid landmines
- Don't draw attention to a body part they're uncomfortable with
- If wardrobe is highlighting something they don't want highlighted, swap

### Confidence building
- Tell them when something looks right. Specifically. "That jacket fits your shoulders perfectly" beats "you look great"
- Show them a frame on the back of camera early. Confidence skyrockets when they see themselves looking good

### Difficult subjects
- **"I hate everything"**: usually insecure. Pull aside, ask what they actually feel best in, work backward
- **"I know better"**: let them have small wins. Pick one piece they want that won't kill the shot
- **Frozen, can't perform**: walk them through movement. "Look here, now there, now relax your shoulders"

---

## X. WORKING WITH OTHER PROS

When it's not just you.

### Hair and makeup
- They go before final wardrobe. Makeup transfers on white shirts
- Touch base on look concept before they start
- Schedule: hair and makeup run 30-60 min, wardrobe 10-15. Plan accordingly

### Other photographers (when you're styling, not shooting)
- Trust their crop calls. Style for the lens they're using
- Ask what they want for wides, mediums, tights

### Client / agency on set
- They have opinions. Some are good, some are noise
- Their job is to feel involved. Yours is to make their concept work
- If they push something that fights the rules, name the tradeoff: "We can do that, but it'll read flat on camera under this light"

### Model agencies
- Sometimes they have say. Polaroids and approved looks may flow back
- If model shows up with their own pieces, evaluate. Don't dismiss

---

## XI. POST-SHOOT

The unglamorous part.

### Wardrobe handling
- Mark hero looks on photos so client can see what was used
- Returns: tagged items back same week. Don't sit on them
- Rentals: return on date or pay extra
- Brand pulls: clean and ship back per their policy
- Client's own clothes: hand back clean, on hangers

### Documentation
- Photos of every approved look (full subject)
- Notes per look: what worked, what didn't, what to repeat
- Receipts for returns and expenses

### Billing (when styling for outside clients)
- Day rate for styling separate from photography
- Pull / shopping fee
- Kit fee for your gear and consumables
- Travel and time

## XIII. WHAT WE'RE NOT DOING

- **No styling against the subject's body or insecurities.** Stretch the comfort zone, don't break it
- **No pulls so big the fitting takes longer than the shoot.** Curate before arrival
- **No skipping the fit check between looks.** Every look gets the same treatment
- **No "fix it in post" on wardrobe.** Retouching cleans small issues. Bad fit and wrong color are not post problems
- **No working without the kit.** Don't show up assuming the location has what you need
- **No styling someone you haven't read first.** Body, coloring, comfort, the four questions, all before you put a piece on them
