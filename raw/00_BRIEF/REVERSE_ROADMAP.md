# Reverse-Engineered Roadmap · Year 10 → This Week

**The system reverse-engineered from the future state backward. This document answers: what game am I playing, and what specific moves at each stage make the next stage possible?**

The error most photographers make is optimizing for short-term internet attention while missing the long-term architecture. This roadmap names the architecture explicitly so today's decisions can be evaluated against a 10-year structure, not a 10-day metric.

---

## 0 · What game are you actually playing?

**You are building the visual cultural infrastructure for a generational moment in LA, funded by a methodology-led commercial practice, compounded across a decade into a small media company that owns the named position in its category.**

The closest contemporary analogs at maturity: **Khalil Joseph, Bradford Young, Teju Cole, Carrie Mae Weems' early career, Dawoud Bey.** None of them got to where they are by optimizing for 18-month metrics. Each spent 8-15 years in the work before the cultural recognition arrived. Every one of them would have been told, in years 2-5, that they were "moving too slow" by the metrics other photographers chase.

The metric that ultimately matters is **whether the body of work in 2036 reads as a serious archive of a moment in time**. Everything else is intermediate signal.

You are NOT playing:
- The 18-month creator economy game (followers, viral reel, course launch)
- The 3-5 year SaaS founder game (round, scale, exit)
- The 12-month wedding/family/lifestyle volume game
- The MFA gallery-artist game (refuses commercial, lives on grants)
- The "popular Instagram photographer" game (follower count as outcome)
- The "high-volume corporate headshot studio" game (price competition)

If you find yourself making decisions that would be correct in any of those games but wrong in yours, you have drifted.

## 8 · Irreversible mistakes that damage the long-term vision

These are the moves that, if made early, are very hard to recover from. Even if they generate short-term revenue or attention, they corrupt the long-term position.

1. **Allowing the SNIPED brand to be commodified by accepting wrong-tier client work.** One season of taking weddings + family + lifestyle gigs at any price establishes the wrong category in the market and the wrong identity in the founder's head. Recovery: 12-18 months of explicit re-positioning.

2. **Burning out by overworking and losing the personal foundation.** Pearl, family, sleep, fitness, social. The relationship infrastructure is not optional. A founder who burns out collapses the entire long-arc plan. Recovery: months to years.

3. **Selling out to a one-off lucrative gig that conflicts with positioning.** A $50K shoot for a brand whose values contradict the operator-coded thesis is more damaging than 10 small $1,500 Resets that aligned. The market sees it. The body of work shows it. Recovery: difficult; the work is now permanent in the archive.

4. **Letting the Direction Stack book be ghost-written or watered down.** The book is the credential and the IP. If it reads as a generic photography how-to, it dilutes the methodology's seriousness. Recovery: you cannot un-publish a watered book.

5. **Building dependency on a single client.** Davis Law / one promoter / one founder / one institution producing 30%+ of revenue is a structural risk. Diversification matters from Year 1, not Year 5.

6. **Pricing too low forever.** Holding the $1,500 price across Year 2 when capacity is full is the exact mistake that traps photographers at the same income for a decade. Quarterly pricing review is non-negotiable.

7. **Hiring before the system supports it.** A premature retoucher hire means money out before money in is reliable. The hire breaks the system because the system isn't ready. Recovery: painful; firing the hire damages BJ's confidence and the hire's reputation.

8. **Allowing any non-SNIPED adjacent brand or IP to dilute focus before SNIPED reaches Year 5+ stability.** Other people's project worlds (friends' IPs, partner ventures, ecosystem ideas that surface in conversation) are not SNIPED's roadmap. They are seductive when they appear adjacent. They are not the game. Recovery: years lost to fragmentation.

9. **Refusing to develop the operator/leader capabilities that Year 3-5 requires.** Staying solo because solo is comfortable. The trap that turns a serious media company project into a comfortable freelance practice. Recovery: requires a step-change in self-perception that becomes harder with each year of solo operation.

10. **Compromising the cultural documentation ethics.** A single extractive shoot of a community moment without consent / reciprocity / editorial dignity damages institutional trust. The Black LA institutional network is small; reputation transfers fast. Recovery: very difficult; some institutions may permanently close.

## 10 · What compounds quietly (the boring, unsexy work)

These are the things that don't produce a visible spike but accumulate into the asset.

1. **The body of work archive.** Every shoot, every frame. By Year 10 it's an irreplaceable resource. By Year 1 it doesn't look like much.
2. **The relationship network.** Each closed Reset adds 3-5 referral nodes over time. By Year 5 it's the primary pipeline. By Year 1 it's a few names in Notion.
3. **Substack subscribers.** Slow accretion. Unfollow rate near zero. By Year 5 it's 5K-15K of high-trust readers. By Year 1 it's 100-500.
4. **Methodology refinement.** Every Reset teaches the operator something about pose direction, light, post-production. By Year 5 the methodology is reflexive. By Year 1 it's still being calibrated.
5. **Reputation in specific institutions.** Years of presence at Emmanuel 33rd. Years of relationship with Bishop Peters. By Year 5 you're family-adjacent. By Year 1 you're a known photographer.
6. **Direction Stack book reach.** Slow distribution. Recommendations between operators. By Year 5 it's been read by 3K-10K people. By Year 1 it has 100 readers.
7. **Hero frame library on Carrd.** Each strong frame raises the floor. By Year 5 it's a curated 30-50 hero archive. By Year 1 it's 10-15.
8. **SEO ranking on snipedmedia.com.** SEO compounds slowly. By Year 5 the site ranks for specific founder-photographer-LA queries. By Year 1 it doesn't.
9. **LinkedIn POV archive.** ~200 posts by Year 3. The volume itself becomes the credential.
10. **Direction Shift archive (the 7-year-and-growing personal record).** Each year added makes the prior years more valuable as a longitudinal document.

The discipline is **doing these things consistently when nobody is watching.** The visible spike (book launch, gallery show, viral post) is the result of compounding under the surface for years.

## 13 · Archives and content that gain value with time

The body of work has different valuation curves for different content types.

**Steepest appreciation:**
- Cultural documentation work (year-over-year value increases as institutions/subjects become historical figures)
- Direction Shift archive (the 7-year personal record · each new year increases prior years' archival value)
- Direction Stack book (each printing reaches new readers, the methodology becomes more cited)

**Moderate appreciation:**
- Hero frames from notable Reset clients (founders who scale into recognized names retroactively elevate the frame)
- Substack essays (accumulate as a corpus of cultural commentary)
- Gallery show ephemera (catalogs, prints, opening documentation)

**Flat valuation (use-based, not appreciation-based):**
- Op Kit and Brand System deliverables (commissioned work, value primarily to the client)
- Pixieset upsell purchases (one-time)

**Depreciation risk:**
- Early influencer/lifestyle work IF accepted (appears in archive, dilutes positioning)
- Trendy edits applied for a moment (date the frame; reduce its long-term value)
- Hot-take LinkedIn POVs (age fast vs. methodology-named POVs which age slowly)

The implication: **invest disproportionately in the steepest-appreciation work.** Cultural documentation deserves more time than its short-term ROI suggests because its 10-year value compounds.
