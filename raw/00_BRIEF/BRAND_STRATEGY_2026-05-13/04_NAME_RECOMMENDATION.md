## The argument

### Why BASEPLATE (not just "any tech-y name")

The Operator Questions doc + 100Q audit + Lineage Doctrine + Operator-Coded Definition all surface the same operator identity: **a systems-architect making editorial photography as the proving ground for systems-as-creative-leverage.** BJ is not a photographer who happens to think systemically · he is a systems thinker who happens to make photographs.

BASEPLATE names what BJ actually is. Every other strong candidate (PLATE, FOLIO, MERIDIAN, CODEX) names the work or the artifact · BASEPLATE names the operating layer underneath the work. That is the genuinely accurate description.

### Why now

- **Pre-audience.** Migration cost ≈ zero.
- **LLC filed.** Zero legal step to start.
- **CH01 = prototype.** Ships tonight as SNIPED · the last SNIPED-branded artifact · then BASEPLATE opens at CH02. Clean cut-over.
- **Every chapter shipped under the wrong name is a Card you'll want to reprint.** Print the right name on Card 02.

### Why BASEPLATE as master · not BASEPLATE as parallel-only

The original mental model was:
- SNIPED = editorial photography brand
- BASEPLATE = parallel anonymous AI/content side play

The problem: maintaining two brands at pre-audience phase splits focus, dilutes any equity earned, and forces choices later about which "wins." Better to commit one master brand now and use sub-imprints to separate the AI/experimental work (BASEPLATE LAB).

The firewall BJ originally wanted between SNIPED's editorial discipline and the AI side-play still holds · it's just enforced at the sub-imprint level instead of the master-brand level. BASEPLATE EDITIONS holds the editorial discipline. BASEPLATE LAB is the experimental container. They share a parent · they don't share an editorial register.

### Why BASEPLATE EDITIONS as the chapter archive imprint

A standalone "BASEPLATE" wordmark on a chapter Card masthead reads as a tech company logo. A "BASEPLATE EDITIONS" wordmark reads as a publishing house · which is what the chapter archive actually is.

Treat "EDITIONS" like Penguin Editions, Aperture Editions, Magnum Editions · the imprint signature that publishes named work.

Card masthead reads:

```
BASEPLATE EDITIONS

VOL · II
CHAPTER 02 · MIMI · LOS ANGELES · MAY 2026
[image]
"[Cover line]"
[colophon] EDITION I · MMXXVI · CHAPTER 02 · SERIAL 01 / 01
```

The wordmark hierarchy puts BASEPLATE EDITIONS at the top in display weight · subordinate text (VOL · CHAPTER · SUBJECT) below. Same Playfair Black register as the SNIPED design · now the type carries a name that matches it.

## Why not the runners-up

### PLATE (8.5)

Strong contender. Single-syllable elegance. Photography-historical (glass plate negatives). Subset of BASEPLATE (could even read as the public-facing version of the BASEPLATE LLC).

**Loses to BASEPLATE because:**
1. PLATE has more competing meanings in everyday English (dinner plate, license plate, home plate) · creates context-confusion risk.
2. PLATE loses the engineering-systems identity layer · it reads only as photographic / culinary.
3. The LLC tie-in to BASEPLATE is zero-cost · adopting PLATE means either re-filing the LLC or maintaining a name-vs-LLC mismatch.
4. Sub-imprint extension is weaker: PLATE EDITIONS is fine · PLATE LAB sounds like a 3D printing service.

**Where PLATE wins:** if BJ decides the engineering register is too cold and wants something more elegantly photographic. PLATE is the fallback for that path.

### MERIDIAN (8.5)

Beautiful · time-coded · operator-friendly. Implies the highest point of the sun (peak craftsmanship) and the line dividing maps (defines territory).

**Loses to BASEPLATE because:**
1. Doesn't extend as cleanly into sub-imprints (MERIDIAN LAB · MERIDIAN STUDIO read fine but MERIDIAN PRESS feels heavy).
2. 3 syllables · slower in speech.
3. Less product / use-case codeable · MERIDIAN doesn't sound like a SaaS or system.
4. No LLC alignment.

**Where MERIDIAN wins:** if BJ wants the brand to feel more poetic / archival / mythic, less industrial. Worth considering as the imprint name if BASEPLATE becomes the parent (BASEPLATE · MERIDIAN EDITIONS), but the simpler architecture is BASEPLATE EDITIONS.

### Keep SNIPED

**Loses across all 10 criteria** · file 01 audit detailed this. Worth noting one last time: the operator-coded refusal of validation-seeking means the brand name should NOT chase edge / cool / attitude. SNIPED chases all three. It's the visible attempt to convince the viewer that the work itself refuses.
