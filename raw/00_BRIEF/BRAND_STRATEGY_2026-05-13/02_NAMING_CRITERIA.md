# 02 · NAMING CRITERIA · The Framework

**Date:** 2026-05-13

These 10 criteria are derived from the SNIPED OS corpus · 100Q audit, OPERATOR_QUESTIONS, CANONICAL_TRUTHS, OPERATING_LOCKS, THE_OPERATOR_CODED_DEFINITION, THE_LINEAGE_DOCTRINE, and the intel layer (Maister Trust Equation, Win Without Pitching, Berger photo theory, Perennial Seller, Hospitality, Naval leverage logic).

Every name in `03_NAMING_CANDIDATES.md` gets scored against these 10. Pass / borderline / fail.

The framework is durable · revisit it any time we're naming anything (a new imprint, a product, an offering).

---

## The 10 criteria

### 1 · Doesn't need explaining (legibility)

The name lands in 1 second. No follow-up question. No "what does that mean?" The first impression IS the meaning.

**Test:** Tell a stranger the name. Do they nod, or do they ask?

**Why:** Operator-coded refusal of marketing copy means the name carries its own weight. If it needs an explanation, the work has to do extra labor to overcome the explanation.

---

### 2 · Register match to the actual work

The emotional register of the name matches the emotional register of the photography. Both quiet, restrained, editorial, archival, operator-coded.

**Test:** Does the name feel like it belongs on a Magnum / Aperture / LIFE / Roversi book spine?

**Why:** Lock 1 + Lock 7 (v3 LUXURY · editorial restraint). The name is the cover of the book · it should match the book.

---

### 3 · Founder credibility (Forbes / VC / serious operator rooms)

The name reads as legitimate in founder / VC / institutional rooms. Not creator-economy. Not influencer. Not lifestyle.

**Test:** Could it appear on a Sequoia / a16z portfolio page without looking out of place? Could a Forbes 30 under 30 byline list it without flinching?

**Why:** 100Q Q19 lock · the buyer-world is LA tech founder class. The brand name has to clear the founder-credibility bar to convert founders.

---

### 4 · Engineering / systems-architect alignment

The name has resonance with BJ's actual identity as a systems-architect · the operator-coded core. Doesn't sound like "an artist" alone · sounds like someone building infrastructure for a body of work.

**Test:** Could it be a SaaS / platform / system / infrastructure brand?

**Why:** BJ's natural identity is the systems-architect. The brand should signal what BJ actually is, not what photographers stereotypically are.

---

### 5 · Brand architecture · extends to sub-brands

The name extends cleanly into sub-brands or imprints. `[NAME] EDITIONS`, `[NAME] PRESS`, `[NAME] STUDIO`, `[NAME] LAB` all read naturally.

**Test:** Write out 4 sub-imprint names. Do they all sound right? Are any awkward?

**Why:** The OS contains 4-5 product lines (chapter archive, services, book imprint, AI/experimental). The name has to hold all of them.

---

### 6 · Time durability (10+ years)

The name doesn't feel dated in 2036. It uses vocabulary that holds up across decades. No trend, no era-marker, no fad register.

**Test:** Imagine the name on a 2036 monograph spine. Does it still land, or does it feel like a 2020s artifact?

**Why:** Lock 7 (decade-long edit register) implies decade-long brand register. The chapter archive is meant to compound across 30 years.

---

### 7 · Ownable in search / trademark

The name is registrable as a trademark in the photography / media / publishing category, AND owns enough of the search index that someone looking for it can find it.

**Test:** Does Google return the name dominantly when you type it, or does it return something else? Is `.com` available · or at least `.co` / `.studio` / `.press`?

**Why:** Brand equity that lives at someone else's URL is fragile. Owning the search result is owning the front door.

---

### 8 · Visual identity headroom

The name supports multiple visual treatments · editorial serif (Playfair), Bauhaus sans, brass-foil colophon, industrial wordmark. Not locked to one visual register.

**Test:** Mock the wordmark in 3 different registers (editorial · industrial · minimalist). Do all 3 work, or does only one?

**Why:** Brand needs flexibility across surfaces (Card masthead · IG bio · LinkedIn · founder portrait byline · book cover · Reset deck cover).

---

### 9 · Phonetic strength (easy to say · easy to remember)

The name is easy to pronounce on first read. Memorable phonetically. Doesn't get mis-spelled. Doesn't get mis-pronounced.

**Test:** Say it once to someone in a noisy room. Do they get it · or do they ask?

**Why:** Word-of-mouth distribution requires phonetic resilience. The Lineage Doctrine, Reset, chapter system all depend on subjects / clients / collaborators saying the brand name correctly to others.

---

### 10 · Product / function / use-case codeable

The name can someday refer to a SOLUTION, not just an identity. Like STRIPE became "payment platform" · the brand becomes the use-case.

**Test:** Could "[NAME] for [user]" be a complete sentence? E.g., "BASEPLATE for founders." "STRIPE for businesses." "FIGMA for design teams."

**Why:** BJ's deeper ambition is systems / methodology / product · the photography is the proving ground. The brand should leave room to become a product line, a methodology imprint, a software offering, a network.

## Scoring system

For each candidate in file 03, I score each criterion as:

- ✓ (passes cleanly)
- ~ (borderline · works but not strongly)
- ✗ (fails · meaningful gap)

Total possible: 10 ✓
Threshold for serious consideration: 7+ ✓
Threshold for recommendation: 9+ ✓

---

## Tie-breakers (when 2+ candidates score equally)

In rank order:

1. **Already-filed LLC alignment.** If one candidate matches a filed legal entity (zero filing cost), it wins.
2. **Register match.** The more restrained / editorial-quiet, the better.
3. **Phonetic strength.** Shorter wins.
4. **Product codeability.** The one that extends into a system / methodology / platform.

---

## What this framework explicitly does NOT optimize for

- **Coolness.** A name that's "cool" to BJ today but doesn't pass criterion 6 (time durability) loses.
- **Cleverness.** Wordplay, puns, double-meanings lose to direct, durable, legible names.
- **Industry-default.** No "[Name] Photography" or "Studio [Name]" · those defaults sound like every other photographer.
- **Personal pride.** A name BJ picked at a meaningful moment but no longer fits the work doesn't win out of nostalgia. (See SNIPED audit in file 01.)

---

## How to use this framework

When evaluating a name (now or in future · for a new imprint, a product, a methodology), open file 02, score against the 10 criteria, document the scoring in writing. Don't trust gut alone · use the framework.

A name that scores 9/10 with this framework will likely outperform one BJ "just likes."
