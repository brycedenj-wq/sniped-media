# SUCCESs Message Design

**Skill ID:** 5.1  
**Category:** Communication & Messaging  
**Source Document(s):** Sticky_Communication_Idea_Design_FRAMEWORKS.docx

## Purpose

Design ideas and messages that are understood, remembered, and drive action by applying the six-principle SUCCESs checklist (Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Stories).

## When to Use

When crafting any business communication that needs to be clear, memorable, and drive action.

## Instructions

Follow this workflow precisely. Each step is grounded in the source document(s) listed above. Do not skip steps. Do not invent frameworks, models, or terminology not present in the source material. Execute each step in order, using the exact logic and decision criteria documented.

## Workflow

1. Define your audience. Who are they? What do they already know?
2. Find the Core: What is the single most important thing? Use the Commander's Intent forcing questions: 'If we do nothing else, we must ___.'
3. Check for Curse of Knowledge: Are you a tapper? What would a listener actually hear?
4. Make it Unexpected: Identify which schema (mental model) this violates. Open a knowledge gap before delivering the answer.
5. Make it Concrete: Replace every abstract word with a sensory, tangible equivalent. Two people should picture the same thing.
6. Make it Credible: Add vivid details, human-scale statistics, Sinatra Test examples ('If you can make it there...'), or testable credentials.
7. Make it Emotional: Target a specific emotion. Use individuals, not abstractions. Apply the Three Whys (ask 'why does this matter?' three times to move from features to values).
8. Wrap it in a Story: Find or create a Challenge, Connection, or Creativity plot that embodies the core.
9. Test it: Share with someone outside your expertise. If they can retell it accurately, it sticks.

## Output Format

Produce all of the following deliverables:

- A completed SUCCESs scorecard for the message
- The final sticky message or communication piece
- A before/after comparison showing which principles were strengthened
