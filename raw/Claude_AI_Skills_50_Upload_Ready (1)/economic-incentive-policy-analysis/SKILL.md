# Economic Incentive & Policy Analysis

**Skill ID:** 17.1  
**Category:** Economic Analysis  
**Source Document(s):** ECONOMIC_THINKING_FRAMEWORKS.docx

## Purpose

Evaluate any business decision, pricing policy, or market intervention by mapping incentive structures, tracing second/third-order consequences, and applying the Sowell Test (Compared to what? At what cost? What hard evidence?).

## When to Use

When validating business ideas, new features, or market opportunities. When you need real data from customers rather than opinions.

## Instructions

Follow this workflow precisely. Each step is grounded in the source document(s) listed above. Do not skip steps. Do not invent frameworks, models, or terminology not present in the source material. Execute each step in order, using the exact logic and decision criteria documented.

## Workflow

1. Define the scarcity: What scarce resource is being allocated? What are its alternative uses?
2. Identify the decision-makers: Who will actually make choices under this policy? Consumers, producers, regulators, employees?
3. Map the incentive structure: What behavior does this reward? What does it punish? What does it make more expensive, less expensive, easier, harder?
4. Trace the chain of consequences at least three levels deep: First-order (visible, intended), second-order (behavioral adjustments), third-order (long-run systemic effects).
5. Compare to the counterfactual: Not 'Will this work?' but 'Will this work better than alternatives, including doing nothing?'
6. Check for perverse incentives: Does the policy incentivize the opposite of what it intends?
7. Apply the Sowell Test: (1) Compared to what? (2) At what cost? (3) What hard evidence is there?
8. Check for fallacy of composition: What is true for one unit may not be true for the whole system.
9. Examine empirical evidence: What has actually happened when this type of policy was implemented elsewhere?

## Output Format

Produce all of the following deliverables:

- An incentive map showing who gains, who loses, and what behavioral changes are triggered
- A three-level consequence chain (first, second, third-order effects)
- Sowell Test evaluation (compared to what? at what cost? what evidence?)
- Go/No-Go recommendation with identified perverse incentive risks
