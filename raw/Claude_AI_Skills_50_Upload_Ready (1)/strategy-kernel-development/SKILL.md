# Strategy Kernel Development

**Skill ID:** 1.1  
**Category:** Strategy & Competitive Analysis  
**Source Document(s):** STRATEGIC_THINKING_FRAMEWORKS.docx

## Purpose

Develop a complete strategy using the three-element Kernel framework (Diagnosis, Guiding Policy, Coherent Action) to ensure every strategic initiative has logical structure and avoids the hallmarks of bad strategy.

## When to Use

When developing, evaluating, or stress-testing business strategy. When a team needs to move from vague ambition to concrete, coordinated action.

## Instructions

Follow this workflow precisely. Each step is grounded in the source document(s) listed above. Do not skip steps. Do not invent frameworks, models, or terminology not present in the source material. Execute each step in order, using the exact logic and decision criteria documented.

## Workflow

1. Map the situation: Gather facts about the competitive landscape, internal capabilities, and forces of change at work.
2. Identify anomalies: Find where facts don't fit received wisdom. The gap between expectation and reality reveals opportunity.
3. Generate at least three alternative diagnoses: Frame the situation multiple ways. Each diagnosis implies a different guiding policy.
4. Name the challenge: Write one or two sentences defining the critical obstacle. If you cannot, you don't understand the situation yet.
5. Choose a guiding policy: Select an overall approach to cope with the obstacles identified in the diagnosis. Exploit available sources of power (leverage, focus, design, dynamics).
6. Accept tradeoffs: A guiding policy that accommodates all interests is not a strategy. Good strategy requires saying no.
7. Design coordinated actions: Actions must reinforce each other, not conflict. Set proximate objectives for each action area.
8. Commit to execution specifics: Actions must be concrete enough to implement within 90 days. Vague directives fail.

## Output Format

Produce all of the following deliverables:

- A written Diagnosis (1-2 sentences naming the critical challenge)
- A Guiding Policy statement (the directional approach, with tradeoffs explicit)
- A Coherent Action plan (coordinated, feasible 90-day actions with proximate objectives)
- A tradeoff list (what we are choosing NOT to do)

## Example Use

User provides context about their specific situation. The skill guides them through each workflow step sequentially, producing all deliverables listed in the Output Format section. Each step builds on the previous one, and no step should be skipped.
