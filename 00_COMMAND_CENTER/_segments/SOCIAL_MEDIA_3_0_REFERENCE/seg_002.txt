The character can be you, them, or a third party. If they relate to the character and lock onto the dream outcome, they’re hooked. If the character had a $1,200 noise machine to
get there, they dismiss. Five execution patterns: About me (looking back, creator as character): “I just accomplished X using Y simple method.” Sammy: “I took a break from posting
for almost a month and still grew by 11k followers. And this is why.” 2,000 leads from one video. If I (looking forward, creator as character): “If I had to grow X, I would do Y
simple thing.” Sammy: “I’m pretty confident I could rebuild this account back to where it is now in less than 6 months. Not by doing anything crazy, just by doing this one thing
from day one.” 1,000 leads. To you (you as the character): “If you want X, use Y simple method.” Kallaway: “If you want to get better at using AI, this one simple trick will make
your life so much easier.” 21,000 leads from one video. Can you (question form, you as character): “Is it possible for you to achieve X under Y conditions?” Minnow Lee: “Is it
possible to start a business in one day and make $1,000 from it?” (5th template implied — typically third-party “she” / “he” cases — same structure with someone else as the
relatable character.) The relatability is the game. Doubling down with phrases like “in just 3 simple steps,” “without doing anything crazy,” “one simple thing” — these remove
perceived constraints and make the dream outcome feel achievable. Cassie Skuniverse routinely drives 1,500–3,500 leads per video using this format. THE 6 STORYLOCKS (RETENTION
TECHNIQUES) Six tactical techniques to lock viewers in throughout a video. Layer them. You don’t need all 6 every time. Term Branding — name your concepts. The labeling effect
creates instant anticipation when you say a term they don’t recognize. Embedded Truths — replace hedging (“if/maybe/might”) with confident framing (“when/the reason why/once you do
this”). Closes the doubt door. Thought Narration — say what they’re thinking. Builds trust and locks them in. Negative Frames — use loss aversion. “Don’t make this mistake” hooks
2x harder than “here’s how to win.” Loop Openers — re-hook every 20–30 sec (short) or 60–90 sec (long). “Here’s the thing, though…” Contrast Words — but, actually, instead, turns
out, except, yet. The bedrock under all 5 above. Contrast is the engine. 6 STORYTELLING TECHNIQUES (DEEPER) A different lens on the same material: The Dance (South Park / Trey
Parker–Matt Stone). Beats in a story should connect via “but” or “therefore,” never “and then.” “And then” is piling details. “But/therefore” forces conflict and consequence. Audit
your script: between each beat, can you write “but” or “therefore”? If not, restructure. Rhythm (Gary Provost). Vary sentence length. Short. Then medium. Then occasionally a long
sentence that builds with crescendo. If every sentence is the same length, you create monotony, the brain tunes out. Write each sentence on its own line. If the document looks like
a jagged edge looking down the right side, you have variety. Tone (Steve Jobs / Casey Neistat / Emma Chamberlain). Conversational, like you’re talking to one friend. Print a photo
of one friend, tape it under the camera lens. Write the script as if you’re texting them or sending an audio note. Breaks down the “am I being sold to?” guard. Direction
(Christopher Nolan). Start writing from the end. Figure out the last line — the “last dab” — first. Make it so memorable it could be shared on its own. In short form, the last line
sets up the first line on the loop. Then work backwards. Storylenses (Taylor Swift example). A topic isn’t enough. Your story lens — your unique angle on a topic — is what
differentiates. When Taylor Swift went to a Super Bowl, common lenses: outfit, arrival, facial reactions. Less common: predictions. Even less common: the business impact she was
driving for the NFL. The third one was Kallaway’s lens. 1M+ views. The Hook (Epic Gardening). First line should be punchy and indicative of the plot. Don’t open with “wait till you
see this” — open with the topic. Visual hooks are 10x more effective than audio-only — show what you’re talking about on screen, the eye perceives faster than the ear. THE 8-STEP
CONTENT PLAYBOOK (SYSTEM) A repeatable system for de-risking each rep. The eight steps: IVA Clarity (Ideal Viewer Avatar). Document who they are: demographics, psychographics,
platforms, pain points. Reread every time you start a video. Find Winning Creators. On a fresh account, search avatar-relevant terms. Build a list of 10–20 creators with at least
100k followers serving the same IVA. Mix sizes — small accounts (20–50k) often have unintentional bangers with low traction bias. Find Outlier Ideas. Scan each creator’s feed for
videos with 10x average views. Add to a shortlist. Tools like SortFeed sort feeds by views and date range automatically. Idea Analysis & Selection. For each shortlisted idea, break
it into the 4 buckets (idea, hook, story, visual). Find the weakest bucket — that’s the opportunity. Pick the idea with the biggest improvement gap. Idea Development. Research the
topic. Find better visuals, additional facts, missed angles. The example: Rowan made a video on Toyota’s secret AI city in Mt Fuji that got 40k views. Strong idea, weak execution:
AI avatar voice (flat intonation), slow pacing, low-energy hook, missed key visual (underground robotics). Kallaway’s remix did 2.2M views. Scriptwriting. Optimize for
comprehension above all. Every sentence: only one possible interpretation. Use Sandcastles to draft, then punch up. Editing. Match visual to spoken word. The viewer can only focus
on one spot. If their eyes are on a B-roll that doesn’t reinforce what you’re saying, the visual is hurting, not helping. Find best key visuals from YouTube, company sites, social
profiles. AI for B-roll: MidJourney for stills + Kraya / Veo3 / Flow for animation. Posting. Don’t overthink. Make the video. Post. Gut check. Iterate. Daily for short, weekly for
long. 200 short or 50 long-form reps to start working. THE ONE SECRET: COPY THEN INNOVATE The fastest growth path is to model winners — minimize the unknowns. Hold 5–6 of the 7
Lego bricks constant from a winning creator, change 1–2 (usually topic + angle, sometimes one execution element). Beginners try to invent everything. Wrong move. There are too many
simultaneous unknowns (~200 things to learn, 100 of which are unknown unknowns). Modeling reduces the surface area of unknowns to 1–2 things at a time. Once you’ve closed the gap
on basics, then you can innovate on Lego bricks. “Can I do this in a way no one’s done before? Can I borrow from another industry?” Most beginners get this backwards because
they’re embarrassed to model. Get over it. Virgil Abloh’s 3% rule. COMMENT STRATEGY (UNDERRATED VIRALITY DRIVER) Comments are another surface for entertainment. Use the script to
drive a debate question that people will fight about. Contrarian-debate cyclones in the comments boost engagement significantly. Also: respond to comments. Builds trust. Don’t
ignore them. Use them to inform the next video — what questions did people have? What did they want more of? Bonus: certain people’s likes/shares are weighted more by the
algorithm. (“Super shares.”) When a CEO of a platform or a high-following account shares your video, the algo treats it as a high-credibility signal and pushes harder. CONTENT MIX
/ PILLARS Most creators run 3–4 pillars. Within a niche. NOT across niches. Wider = within the topic, not across topics. If you make marketing content, “wider” means paid marketing
+ viral + agency + B2B — not marketing + MBA. Stay in your category, vary the sub-topics. Beware: too many pillars on one account confuses the algorithm. If you genuinely have 17
distinct sub-niches you want to cover, run 17 channels. TOP-OF-FUNNEL FOLLOWERS-AS-PROXY Followers are a proxy for “should I trust this person?” — used by uneducated viewers as a
credibility shortcut. There are minimum credibility thresholds: ~1k–2k for X, 10k+ for IG, ~10k for YouTube subs. Below those, viewers default to “this isn’t legit.” Above the
threshold, the difference between 180k and 365k followers is essentially zero. So follower count matters in a binary sense, not a continuous one — except for brand deals, where
it’s one of three factors brands price on. Engagement rate is also a signal. Big follow + low engagement = sophisticated viewers spot the fake. Small follow + high engagement
on-target = credible. CONFIDENCE = ALREADY-DECIDED OUTCOMES Confidence in delivery = framing your points as if they’re already true. Subtle but huge: - “This might help you” →
“This will help you” - “I think the best approach is” → “Here’s how to approach it” When you speak in outcomes instead of scenarios, the viewer trusts more by default. The
confidence others read in your speech is them sensing your certainty about your answer. This is the embedded truths principle in delivery rather than copy. LIKABLE EXPERT (BUILDING
TRUST + AFFINITY) Two qualities you need simultaneously to grow a personal brand: - High authority — they trust you know what you’re talking about. - High affinity — they like you
and want to keep watching. Most creators have one or the other. Top brands have both. Five tactics: Believability. Feel connected to the words you’re saying. If a ghostwriter or AI
wrote them, spend 10–20 min before recording to rephrase into your pocket. The viewer can sense phoniness instantly. HTBT — Happy To Be There. If you don’t want to record, don’t.
Walk, work out, take a cold shower, get endorphins. Then come back. The energy is infectious in either direction. No edit can fix it after. Proof-Heavy. Specific numbers, specific
stats. “I grew my account from 2k to 47k in 3 months on Instagram with these three methods” beats “I grew really fast.” Specificity builds credibility — and importantly, it makes
you trust your own claims more. Useful, not extractive. Mindset before recording: “How can I be most useful to the viewer?” — not “I want views, follows, dollars.” If you know it’s
useful, you’re doing them a favor; the pressure drops; the energy becomes the calm authority you want. Admission Lines. Counter-intuitive: openly admit struggles or failures. “I
hate recording. I get super anxious.” Builds rapport. Once you admit you struggled and then solved, the solution hits way harder. Don’t lie. SPEAKING ON CAMERA: THE 5 ANXIETY FIXES
If you don’t sit down comfortable, nothing else matters. Physical shake-out. Before recording, 30 sec of literal body shaking — like UFC fighters before a fight. Resets adrenaline.
Throwaway reps. Press record and ramble for a couple minutes. Warm up. Don’t burn the most valuable part of the video (the intro) when you’re coldest. Friend photo. Tape a photo of
a close friend who matches your IVA below the camera lens. Pull it up on phone if you don’t want it visible to others. Brain locks into conversational mode automatically when it
sees a real face. Line-by-line recording. Read one line, look down, reset, deliver next. Micro-resets between lines keep the energy fresh and reduce drop-off in volume/energy at
the end of paragraphs (which happens when going off the dome — the brain gets ahead). Don’t go off-the-dome unless you’re naturally extroverted; the trade is more prep up front for
less anxiety in delivery. “This doesn’t matter” reset. Anxiety spikes from over-pressure. Remind yourself: nobody sees the bad takes. Even if this video flops, you’ll need
thousands more. This single video is meaningless in the macro. Instantly drops anxiety to zero. RHYTHM SPEAKING (4 TACTICS) Tactical delivery improvements after anxiety is solved:
Shorter sentences. Less load, higher absorption. Cut long sentences in half. Breathers. Half-second pauses after key words. Lets the word land. The pause turns information into
focused insight. Practice with copy speech — find a video, get the transcript, play the video and read along word for word with the original creator. Internalizes their tempo.
Downward inflection. Confidence ends sentences with a downward syllable (statement). Anxiety ends with an upward syllable (sounds like a question). “Upspeak” signals uncertainty.
Most don’t realize they do this until it’s pointed out. Don’t drop phrases. Your volume shouldn’t trail off at the end of sentences. Volume can be fixed in post; energy can’t.
Line-by-line recording solves this — you don’t run out of air. AI CONTENT DETECTION & THE ZAG TO HUMAN-ONLY The bar for “can you create something” has shifted to “can you actually
make something only you can make.” Find your gap — what’s unique to you, not copyable. The contrarian gap is the easiest to start with. Take a look at your niche. Identify the
non-believers — things you disagree with. Could be people (big influencers), could be ways of doing things, could be common knowledge. Kallaway disagrees with hook lists. Hooks are
not the words on a list — they’re the alignment of visual, text, audio, the first frame, the speaker’s social proof, etc. Same words from a different speaker land differently.
(This is the “Hook Lists Suck” thesis.) People are increasingly aware of and repelled by AI-feeling content. Soft-cancellation comes for those caught using AI without disclosure.
Yet everyone wants to use AI to go faster. The dichotomy: use AI to save time on commodity tasks (research, captions), keep the creative thinking and authentic delivery human. Path
of AI replacement (easy to hard): Twitter (text-only) → static image content → short form → long form → live unscripted. Live multi-hour streams will be replaced last. 3–5 years
until AI content is indistinguishable but accessible enough to flood feeds. Maybe 2 years on short form, closer to 5 on long form. RAW AESTHETIC / ANTI-EDIT (BUT NOT LAZY) Mosseri
(head of IG) and others observe winning content has shifted from over-polished to “raw aesthetic.” Imperfections that signal authenticity. Drinking tea on camera. Different voice
from being sick. Things AI can’t easily fake. Common misunderstanding: anti-edit means less effort. Wrong. Anti-edit means intentional imperfection. Intentional authenticity. Looks
casual but everything is calibrated — the angle, the tonality, the rhythm, the way it makes the viewer feel. Lazy raw content underperforms. Calibrated raw content overperforms.

PODCAST CLIPS ARE HARD VS REGULAR SHORTS Podcast clips lack motion (two people sitting still talking). To make them perform, you need: - High color (the wardrobe, the set) - High
contrast - Subtle push-in motion or lens flare added artificially - Or a uniquely visually different set Most podcast clips fail because they look the same — wooden slats with a
tree in the corner that every podcast has. KALLAWAY’S TECH CHANNEL WAS NOT OPTIMIZED His own admission: his tech channel has 3 disconnected pillars (sports tech, GenAI tools,

trained them to expect free entertainment, not a buying decision. The conversion rate is low because the audience is too wide. The creator either: - Stays in the media game (caps
at $30-50k/year) - Has to build a second narrow audience from scratch around the offer Kallaway is in this situation himself with his tech audience. EXAMPLES OF STRONG CONFIDENCE
LANGUAGE “When you do this” not “if you do this” “The reason this works” not “this might work because” “Once you see it, you can’t unsee it” not “you might notice this” “Most
creators are making this mistake” not “some creators might be” THE DELAY DIAGNOSIS If your hook starts with phrases like: - “Guys, this is one of the craziest things…” - “You’re
never going to believe this…” - “Wait till you see this…” You’re failing on Speed-To-Value. The viewer has zero context to decide if this is for them. The brain bounces by ~2
seconds on the steep early-retention drop-off. THE “LAST DAB” TECHNIQUE The last line of a script should be so memorable that if it’s all someone heard, they’d share it with a
friend. In short form, that last line also seeds the loop opener for the first line on replay. Write last line first. Then first line. Then fill the middle. THE 4-PHASE WATCHING
FUNNEL For long form, the viewer’s journey through a video: 1. Click confirmation (first 5–10 sec) — confirm the title’s promise immediately 2. First dopamine hit (within 30–60
sec) — first major insight that justifies the time 3. Hypnosis state (2 min onward) — they’ve forgotten the title and are just watching 4. CTA / native embed (when contextually
relevant) — without breaking hypnosis If you break hypnosis with a non-native ad before 8 min (the average AVD), you’ll cause a retention cliff. THE BRAND NEW ACCOUNT TRICK FOR
RESEARCH Use a fresh email and account when researching what’s working in your niche. Otherwise the algorithm has biased you on your existing watch history. Fresh account = clean
signal of what’s actually performing for the average viewer. FORGET TIME UNDER ATTENTION ON SHORTS Short-form content has a half-life of 72 hours to 5 days. Don’t put long-term
faith in any single short. The whole point of shorts is feed and forget — make many, learn fast, accept the perishability. THE ICONIC AND OPEN RESIDENCY POSITION OpenResidency runs
