Every piece of content maps to one of them. You should never post without knowing which angle you are running. Angle 1 — The Failure Angle "Why portraits fall apart even when your
lighting is good." The lens. The session. The hands. The freeze. The dead smile. Show the failure first. Then show the system. Angle 2 — The Anti-Posing-Guide Angle "Posing
references are not recovery systems." Your product is not what they think it is. This angle separates you from every other photography product in the market. Angle 3 — The Operator
Angle "I documented real failure modes from real sets." Show your notes. Your protocol names. Your markups. The system looks invented because it was. That is the differentiator.
Angle 4 — The Transformation Angle "What changes when you stop guessing and start diagnosing." Not aspirational improvement. Specific operational change. From "I don't know what to
say" to "I have the exact cue." Angle 5 — The Identity Angle "For photographers who are past the gear phase but still not fully in control of the frame." This is the exact ICP.
They know who they are. Mirror it back at them. Angle 6 — The Proof Angle Screenshots. Comments. Buyer reactions. Marked-up protocol previews. The verbal cues in action on set.
Real evidence that the system works. SECTION 6: CHANNEL ROLES Ranked by Impact 1. Launch team outreach: Most important thing you do. One week of direct personal messages produces
the first 10-20 sales and triggers Gumroad Discover algorithm. 2. Jasmine Collins: One post to 49.6K photographers is worth three months of your own posting. Protect this

reviews exist. Channel Separation — Critical The Direction Stack is a photography education product. It belongs on photography-focused channels only. Do not market it on a
Baseplate Studio account targeting D2C founders. The algorithm will punish you and you will confuse both audiences. Keep the feeds separate from day one and forever. SECTION 7:
