Van Der Zee James Van Der Zee, a seminal figure in the Harlem Renaissance, captured the vibrancy and complexity of African American life through his lens, becoming one of the most
revered photographers of the 20th century. Operating a studio in Harlem, Van Der Zee documented a critical period of Black cultural flourishing, from the 1920s through the 1960s,
with a portfolio that includes iconic portraits, cultural events, and everyday life that highlight the dignity and resilience of his subjects. His work, rich in detail and
historical significance, offers a window into the Harlem community, celebrating its achievements against the backdrop of societal challenges. Van Der Zee’s photographs not only
serve as a vital record of Black history but also as a testament to the power of photography as a form of social commentary, leaving a lasting legacy that continues to influence
and inspire. Fashion and Glamour While fashion photography trends seem to shift daily, there are always those classic and timeless photography styles that photographers will always
look to for inspiration. Helmut Newton Helmut Newton was an Australian fashion photographer who became infamous for his striking, often racy, black-and-white fashion photographs.
Newton was heavily influenced by surrealism in photography, and he strived to push the boundaries in the world of fashion photography. Today, photographers still try to mimic his
style. Annie Leibovitz Considered to be one of the best American photographers, Annie Leibovitz became famous in the world of fashion photography during her tenure at Rolling
Stone. Her work was defined by bold colors and interesting poses. War and Conflict Photography The advent photography and the sheer power of documentary photography uncovered the
critical importance of conflict photojournalism. Photographers willing to go into war zones and capture images can help craft authentic and truthful narratives of those
experiences. Robert Capa Perhaps one of the most well-known war photographers is Robert Capa, who famously embedded himself into some of the most brutal battles during the Spanish
Civil War and World War II. Most people would instantly recognize the images that he captured during the 1944 Normandy Invasion, which were some of the most powerful and moving
images of the war. Steve McCurry Conflict photojournalism is anything but safe, and Steve McCurry proved that sometimes you must take significant risks to provide others with
truthful insight. McCurry was smuggled over the border into Pakistan and was able to capture some horrific images of the conflict taking place there. He was only able to get those
images back by hiding his film in his clothing. Lee Miller Lee Miller, a figure whose photography career spanned fashion, surrealism, and war journalism, carved a unique path
through the 20th-century art world. Initially celebrated as a fashion model and muse to the surrealists in Paris, Miller transitioned behind the camera to become a formidable
photographer in her own right, blending the boundaries between art and documentary. Her work during World War II, as one of the first female war correspondents, captured haunting
images of the London Blitz, the liberation of Paris, and the horrors of concentration camps, revealing the stark realities of conflict and human suffering. Miller’s ability to
convey profound emotion and narrative depth in her photographs, whether capturing the whimsy of surrealism or the devastation of war, cements her legacy as a pioneering artist who
used her lens to bear witness to the beauty and brutality of the 20th century. Conceptual and Art Photography Defined by Iberdrola as a creative approach to photography that
strives to convey a particular message through an abstract image, conceptual photography requires artists to develop interesting, thought-provoking images. Conceptual photography
artists differentiate themselves from other photographers. Rather than capturing their subject as-is, they work to meticulously craft a scene that will emphasize the meaning of the
photo. Leaving plenty of room for interpretation, conceptual photographs are meant to be considered, discussed and analyzed. Man Ray Considered to be an avant-garde artist, Man Ray
was influenced by surrealism in photography. Rather than sticking with the status quo, Ray worked to experiment with different types of photography. He even invented camera-less
images that he named Rayographs. Cindy Sherman While other photographers try to find subjects in nature and among people in their communities, Cindy Sherman became famous for being
a photographer who primarily used herself as the subject. Her goal with her images was to draw attention to and raise awareness of the issues that were most important to her.
Landscape and Nature Photography With landscape and nature photography, the environment is the subject. Many of these sweeping photos are used in fine art photography, giving
people the opportunity to invest in stunning images that can bring the outdoors inside their own homes and galleries. Large-format photography is widely used in landscape and
nature photography, thanks to its ability to craft incredibly detailed, high-resolution images. Almost from the beginning of the history of photography, artists have been compelled
to document the landscapes and natural beauty that surrounds them. Ansel Adams Ansel Adams was more than just a landscape photographer; he was also an environmentalist who was
truly passionate about preserving the natural world. Throughout the 1930s, Adams spent much of his time capturing images around Yosemite National Park, and the U.S. Government took
notice. He was soon hired to take photographs of national parks around the country so that the government could use them in advertising materials to promote the parks. In 1980,
Ansel Adams was awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom. Galen Rowell Considered to be the pioneer of participatory photography, Galen Rowell was more than just an observer who
attempted to capture images. He was an active participant in the experience and strove to incorporate his own perspective and experience into each shot. Today, he is one of the
most famous landscape photographers of all time. In addition to being a photographer, Rowell was an avid mountain climber, and most of his images capture the majesty and mystic
beauty of the mountains. Modern Innovators While there continue to be endless debates about analog vs. digital photography, it’s clear that modern innovators in photography are
willing to push the boundaries and develop new styles that will continue to influence photographers for years to come. Not only do modern innovators in photography strive to
capture unique and authentic images, but they also work to incorporate new technology into their art to expand the depth and breadth of photographs. Andreas Gursky Considered a
specialist in large-format photography, Andreas Gursky has proven that sometimes an artist must push the boundaries to get that coveted shot. Over the course of his career, he has
gone to great heights (quite literally) to capture the images that were important to him. At different points, Gursky shot images from helicopters and on top of cranes, allowing
him to capture many built landscape images. By combining large-format photography techniques with digital manipulation, he can develop striking images that have redefined what it
means to be a landscape photographer in modern times. His work proves that you sometimes must think outside the box to set yourself apart in the world of photography. Vivian Maier
Vivian Maier is now known as one of the leading street photography innovators. However, she was not truly recognized for her work until after she passed away in 2009. Maier took
many of her photographs while walking down the streets of Chicago and New York City during the middle of the 20th century. She would stand in front of storefronts and windows, and
capture self-portraits that also highlighted images of daily life during the 1950s and 1960s. While many of her images lack the polish of other more accomplished photographers, she
had a unique ability to capture beauty among the ordinary. Renee Cox Renée Cox, a provocative and pioneering contemporary artist, uses her photography to challenge societal norms
and explore themes of race, gender, and sexuality. Her work, characterized by its boldness and often incorporating her own body as a central subject, serves as a powerful critique
of historical and cultural prejudices. Cox’s art not only confronts the viewer with the necessity of reevaluating cultural stereotypes and biases but also celebrates the strength
and beauty of African American women. Through her inventive use of digital imagery and photography, she crafts visually stunning pieces that transcend traditional boundaries,
inviting dialogue and reflection on identity, empowerment, and liberation. Cox’s contributions to contemporary art and photography continue to resonate, making her a significant
figure in discussions around the role of art in social activism and the ongoing quest for equality and justice. Carrie Mae Weems Carrie Mae Weems, an influential contemporary
American artist, utilizes photography, video, and installation art to explore complex societal issues concerning race, family, gender, and class. Renowned for her powerful visual
narratives that traverse personal and political landscapes, Weems’ work often examines the African American experience, particularly focusing on the representation of Black women.
Through her thought-provoking series, such as “The Kitchen Table Series,” Weems invites viewers into intimate spaces to confront the realities and constructs that shape identity
and cultural perception. Her art not only acts as a mirror reflecting social injustices and personal narratives but also as a lens through which the broader implications of history
and power can be examined. Weems’ contributions to the field have firmly established her as a critical voice in contemporary art, blending the personal with the political to
illuminate the shared aspects of human experience. Explore Your Photography Style at Rocky Mountain College of Art + Design If you want to explore both the technical aspects of
