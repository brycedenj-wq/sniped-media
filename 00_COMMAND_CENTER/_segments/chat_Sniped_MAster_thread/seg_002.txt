Breaking Into Commercial & Advertising Photography in Los Angeles LA is one of the top three markets in the world for this (alongside NYC and London), which means the ceiling is
high — but so is the competition. Here's a ground-level breakdown of exactly how to approach it. 1. UNDERSTAND THE ECOSYSTEM FIRST Before making a single move, your brother needs
to understand who actually hires commercial photographers in LA, because it's almost never the brand directly at first. The key players are: • Advertising Agencies — They concept
and produce campaigns for brands. The Art Buyer or Art Producer is the person who sources photographers. Major LA agencies include 72andSunny, TBWA\Chiat\Day, David&Goliath, RPA,
and Deutsch LA. • Production Companies — They execute the shoot once an agency has a concept. Many have their own photographer rosters. • In-House Creative Teams — Larger brands
(Nike, Apple, Hulu, etc.) have internal studios that hire directly, especially for ongoing content needs. • Brands Directly — Smaller or mid-size brands without an agency
relationship will hire photographers directly, usually through referrals or portfolio discovery. • Photo Representatives (Reps) — These are agents who pitch photographers to
agencies and negotiate deals. Getting repped is a major accelerant. Your brother needs to know which door he's knocking on at every stage of his career. 2. BUILD A PORTFOLIO THAT
SPEAKS THE LANGUAGE Commercial buyers are not looking for pretty pictures. They're looking for problem-solving imagery — photos that sell a feeling, a lifestyle, or a product. The
portfolio has to communicate that he understands brand communication, not just photography. Specifics: • Niche down hard. Automotive, beauty, fashion, food & beverage, tech,
lifestyle, sports — pick one or two and go deep. LA has massive demand in automotive (tons of car brands headquartered or marketed there), entertainment/streaming (Netflix, Hulu,
Disney+, Amazon), and beauty/fashion. • Shoot spec work aggressively. Spec work means creating fake campaigns for real brands — shoot a mock Nike campaign, a fake Glossier
editorial, a Porsche lifestyle series. This is standard practice and completely acceptable. Buyers want to see that you can think like an ad. • Show a range within your niche. If
you're going beauty, show product, model, and lifestyle within that world. Buyers want to see versatility within your lane. • Every image should have a "client" in mind. Before
shooting, ask: what brand would run this? If you can't answer that, the photo probably doesn't belong in a commercial book. • 12–20 images max in your primary portfolio. Quality
over quantity. Buyers flip through dozens of books — yours needs to hit hard fast. 3. BUILD YOUR DIGITAL PRESENCE THE RIGHT WAY Personal Website: • Use a platform built for
photographers — Format, Squarespace, or a custom-coded site. Keep it clean, fast, and brand-forward. • Your URL should be your name or studio name. No "smugmug" or "pixieset" links
for a commercial book. • Include a "Clients" section even if early clients are small — it signals professionalism. • Have a clear, downloadable or viewable tear sheet (a one-page
PDF showcasing your best work and contact info). Instagram: • This is still a primary discovery tool for art buyers and creative directors in LA. Maintain a consistent aesthetic
that reflects your niche. • Post behind-the-scenes content, lighting setups, and production context — buyers want to see how you think on set. • Use strategic hashtags:
#commercialphotography #advertisingphotography #[yourniche]photography plus LA-specific tags. • Tag brands in relevant spec work. Sometimes this leads to organic inbound. LinkedIn:
• Set up a strong profile framed around commercial photography specifically. Many in-house creative directors and brand managers discover photographers here. • Connect with Art
Directors, Creative Directors, Art Buyers, and Brand Managers in LA. 4. CREATE A PROMO STRATEGY (THIS IS WHAT SEPARATES PROS) In commercial photography, promos are how you stay top
of mind with buyers. Most working commercial photographers treat this like a marketing budget. Direct Mail: • Physical mailers still have a disproportionate impact because almost
nobody does them anymore. A well-designed 4x6 or 5x7 card with one powerful image and your contact info, sent to a curated list of art buyers and creative directors, can generate
real callbacks. • Print in small batches (50–100) through Newspaper Club, Moo, or Momento Pro and mail quarterly. Email Promos: • Build a list of art buyers and creative directors
at LA agencies (more on how to build this list below). Send a simple, image-forward email every 6–8 weeks. Do NOT send walls of text. One image, one line, your name, your URL. •
Use Mailchimp or Flodesk to track open rates. • Subject lines matter — keep them short and curiosity-driven. The List: Building your promo list is an ongoing project. Sources
include: • Agency websites — Most agencies list their team. Look for Art Buyers, Art Producers, and Senior Art Directors. • LinkedIn — Search "Art Buyer Los Angeles" or "Art
Producer Los Angeles." • Workbook and Agency Access — Industry directories specifically built for photographers to find and contact buyers. Agency Access is a paid subscription
