THE RIGHT PEOPLE IN LA Portfolio Reviews: • APA (Advertising Photographers of America) has a strong LA chapter and regularly hosts portfolio review events where working art buyers
and creative directors give direct feedback. This is invaluable and often leads to real relationships. • ASMP (American Society of Media Photographers) also hosts events and has a
Southern California chapter. • The Photo District (PDN) events — PDN Portfolio reviews, PhotoPlus Expo, etc. In-Person Agency Visits: • This is old school but still works in LA.
Call or email ahead, ask if you can do a portfolio drop or a short in-person review. Smaller boutique agencies are more receptive to this than big holding company shops. • Bring a
printed book. Seeing physical prints is still a differentiating experience. Assistanting as a Strategic Move: • If your brother is newer to the commercial world, assisting
established commercial photographers in LA is one of the fastest paths into the industry. You learn set dynamics, client communication, how to run a crew, and most importantly —
you build relationships with Art Directors and Producers who are already working. • Key assistants eventually get recommended by the photographers they work under. It's a direct
pipeline. • Find assisting opportunities through APA, word of mouth, or by reaching out directly to established photographers whose work you admire. 6. UNDERSTAND BIDDING,
LICENSING & THE BUSINESS SIDE This is where most talented photographers leave money on the table or lose jobs entirely. Commercial photography is a business-to-business transaction
and buyers expect photographers to understand the business. Usage Licensing: • Your brother needs to understand that in commercial work, you charge for two things: the creative fee
(your time, talent, and production) and the usage license (how the client can use the image — where, for how long, in what media). • A photo shot for a regional print campaign has
a very different license value than a photo used on a national TV commercial or a Times Square billboard. • Resources: fotoQuote software and BlinkBid help calculate
industry-standard licensing fees. Estimates & Bidding: • Commercial jobs are bid competitively. The agency sends a "Request for Estimate" (RFE) and often three photographers bid on
the same job. • Bids include: creative/day fees, usage licensing, production costs (crew, equipment, location, talent, post-production), and a markup on production (typically
15–20%). • BlinkBid or Quickbooks are standard for generating professional estimates. • Learning to bid is a skill — starting too low devalues the market and kills your negotiating
position. APA offers workshops specifically on bidding. Contracts: • Always work with a contract. ASMP provides industry-standard contract templates for members. • Never shoot
without a signed estimate/agreement, especially for larger commercial jobs. 7. GET REPPED (THE ACCELERATOR) A photo rep is an agent who pitches your work to agencies, handles
negotiations, and manages your business relationships in exchange for a commission (typically 25–30% of the creative fee, not production). Getting repped by a strong LA rep is a
massive accelerator. Reps have existing relationships with art buyers and get calls that unrepresented photographers simply never hear about. How to approach getting repped: • You
generally need a strong, focused portfolio and some initial commercial credits before a rep will take you seriously. • Research reps who represent photographers in your niche.
Agency Access and Workbook both list reps. Instagram is also a discovery tool — many reps are active there. • Send a concise, professional inquiry with a link to your portfolio and
a brief note on why you think you're aligned with their roster. Don't pitch multiple reps at the same agency. • Some photographers work without reps their entire career — it's not
required, especially early on, but it's a major ceiling-breaker once you're ready. 8. TARGET LA-SPECIFIC OPPORTUNITIES Beyond the general commercial market, LA has specific
high-volume sectors your brother should target based on his niche: • Entertainment Marketing — Key art (movie posters, streaming show artwork), behind-the-scenes unit photography,
and promotional campaigns for studios. Netflix, Disney+, HBO, Hulu, Amazon, and Apple TV+ all have massive production footprints in LA. • Automotive — LA is the automotive
advertising hub of the US. Brands like Toyota, Honda, Hyundai, Kia, and Genesis all have North American HQs in the LA area. This is one of the highest-paying niches in commercial
photography. • Beauty & Skincare — LA has a dense cluster of beauty brands (e.g., Kylie Cosmetics, Revolve, Anastasia Beverly Hills). Strong demand for product and campaign
photography. • Fashion & E-Commerce — DTLA's Fashion District and the broader LA streetwear/lifestyle scene generates constant demand for lookbook, campaign, and e-commerce
photography. • Tech & Streaming — Silicon Beach (Playa Vista/Venice/Santa Monica) is home to Google, YouTube, Snap, Hulu, and dozens of tech companies with active content needs. 9.
