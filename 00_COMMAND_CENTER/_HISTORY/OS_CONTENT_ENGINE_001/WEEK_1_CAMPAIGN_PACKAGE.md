## 1. Weekly cadence

## 2. The theme (one category, not random)
**The everyday-carry "desk object."** The small thing you keep within arm's reach all day. Faceless (object-on-white, no person ever), employer-safe (generic archetypes + the operator's own concept only), trademark-free (taste taught by the subtraction principle and generic forms, never by riding a brand), in the operator's taste, and a clean PHYSICAL product-surface candidate.

## 3. The hero (the owned concept that could become a product)
**THE READYMADE DROP No.001: "THE ONE".** A single shallow machined tray: one continuous radius, no dividers, no lid, no labels. Subtraction applied to the desk-organizer category: instead of twelve molded slots, one undivided surface that forces you to carry less. The organization IS the rule: if it does not fit flat in one layer, it does not come with you. It is the operator's own concept (a form plus a rule of use), not a teardown of any existing product. Ownable because the form is a generic primitive (a shallow tray) and the owned part is the specific intervention (delete all dividers, turn it into a rule, plus a signature proportion and the naming system "THE ONE"). **Product surface tested:** the OBJECTS surface (Surface 1), a signal-priced limited PHYSICAL drop. No store, no price, no link.

## 4. How the support connects to the hero
- **Teardown A (Mon, FORM):** teaches the subtraction principle (absence can be the design). Pre-loads the exact logic THE ONE is built on, 48h early, so Wednesday's empty tray reads as considered, not as "a tray with a part missing."
- **Teardown B (Tue, CONSTRAINT):** teaches systems-as-creative (a designed rule is a product, not a deprivation). Justifies the hero's premise so the rule-of-use card reads as the whole product.
- **System Autopsy (Thu, AUDIENCE):** names who buys subtraction (the editor: founder/creative/coach/operator who owns too many tools) and why now (decision fatigue, everyday-carry as a status surface, the swing to fewer better things). Lands the market case on the people who just saw the drop.

- 7.0-12.0s: 3-up variant grid on white, three radii / three depths, labels "r4 · r8 · r12", cursor circles the middle. Type: "Three tries. One proportion is right." (process legibility: designed, not found)
- 12.0-15.0s: chosen final zooms to fill, others fade, slow quarter-rotate (object stays pixel-exact). Type: "THE ONE. One tray. One layer."
- 15.0-18.0s: RULE-OF-USE card, placard style: "If it does not fit flat in one layer, you do not carry it." Hold in near-silence. (you are buying the rule, not the slots)
- 18.0-21.0s: dual-legibility card. Top line for the scroll: "A desk organizer that organizes by deleting the compartments." Bottom line for the operator: "Carry less. Decide less. Edit your life down to one layer."
- 21.0-22.0s: LOOP, the empty tray slides off and the twelve-slot tray slides back for one frame as "THE ONE" re-stamps, so the replay reads as a punchline. Burn-in: "Reply DROP if you'd keep this on your desk."

### TEARDOWN A (MON) · FORM · IG Reel + Threads text cut (~18s)

### TEARDOWN B (TUE) · CONSTRAINT · TikTok + Pinterest 2:3 Pin (~18s)

### SYSTEM AUTOPSY (THU) · AUDIENCE/MARKET · LinkedIn POV (text, ~250 words)
Hook line: "Nobody buys a desk organizer to store more things. They buy it to make fewer decisions."
- Put the noun in quotes: "Organization is sold as storage. It is actually decision-removal. The real product is the relief of not choosing what to carry every morning. The slots were never the point."
- The costly-flex mechanic: "An edited desk is a legible signal. It says 'I have already decided.' That is a costly flex: it is expensive to subtract because it means owning fewer, better things on purpose. Addition is cheap. Subtraction reads as taste."
- Who this is for: "This object is not for the person who needs more slots. It is for the editor: the founder, the creative, the operator who already owns too many tools and is tired of managing them. The one who would rather carry three things well than twelve things badly."
- Why now: "Decision fatigue is at an all-time high, everyday-carry and desk-setup culture has made the small object a status surface, and there is a quiet swing back toward fewer, better physical things against infinite digital sprawl. The market for 'less, but considered' is widening."
- Connect to the drop without selling: "On Wednesday I posted an object built entirely on that thesis: one tray, one layer, the rule is the product. I am not selling it. I am asking who it is for."
- Close (the 4-way self-ID probe): "If this is you, tell me which one you are: founder, creative, coach, or operator. And name the ONE object you refuse to leave behind. I am reading every reply."
Caption: same as the post body opener. Connects: lands the market case the day after the drop on the highest-intent pool, converting "I want this" into a self-identified segment the ledger reads. Signal: which archetype gathers (audience_tag) + hero durability. CTA: "Tell me which one you are: founder, creative, coach, or operator. And name the ONE object you refuse to leave behind." Self-ID + named object = strong signal + a logged audience_tag.

### E. Paid amplification rules (ads allowed later, never a crutch)
- **No boosting until organic clears a threshold.** A post qualifies for a small boost ONLY after it organically clears either: >=8% named-object DROP replies of reached accounts, OR top-quartile save-to-reach for the account, OR >=3 inbound "make this real" DMs. Views and likes never qualify a post.
- **Micro-test budget:** small and operator-set (proposed default ~$20-50 per qualifying post), one platform at a time. Treated as a measurement cost, not growth spend.
- **Split test:** boost 2-3 HOOK or first-frame variants of the SAME proven post, never new untested posts. Hold the offer/probe constant; vary only the hook.
- **When to kill:** kill a boost if it does not lift strong-signal replies/DMs/waitlist adds above the organic baseline within the micro-test window (judge on product signal, not CPM or views).
- **When to scale:** scale only if the boost drives strong product signal (named replies, DMs, waitlist), and only by repeating the micro-test on the next proven post; no large budgets until a product hypothesis has graduated (section F).
- **Avoid vanity:** never boost for reach/views/likes. The only thing worth amplifying is a post already producing product signal.
