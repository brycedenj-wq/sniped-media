01

The 10-second stranger experience

A stranger is scrolling TikTok at speed. This is the first frame and the first three seconds.

I deleted every compartment.
the most over-built object on your desk

Why they stop

First framePure white. A familiar twelve-slot desk tray sits dead center, alone, no hand, no room, no logo. It looks like a product photo, which is a pattern-break on a feed of faces and noise.
Motion0.0s hard cut in (no fade). At ~2s the slotted tray slides off-frame left and one empty shallow tray slides in to replace it. The motion is the argument: subtraction happening live.
TextOne line, sans, high contrast: "I deleted every compartment." A contradiction (an organizer with nothing to organize into) that opens a loop the brain wants closed.
SoundNear silence. A single soft mechanical click on the swap. No trending audio. The quiet is the tell that this is considered, not content-mill.
Why it worksIt attacks a category everyone owns, withholds the why, and the visual is so clean it reads as a brand before they know it is one. They stop because it looks expensive and says something.

The Week 1 hero drop experience

Hero image direction

Top-down, seamless white, one shallow undivided tray dead center, one continuous radius, no dividers, no lid, no labels. Soft single-source light, a long quiet shadow. Slow quarter-rotate. The object is the only event in the frame.

Sequencetitle card → rough sketch (one stroke) → 3-variant grid (r4 · r8 · r12) → chosen final → rule-of-use placard.
Caption"THE READYMADE DROP No.001. THE ONE. One shallow tray. No compartments. The organization is the rule: if it does not fit flat in one layer, it does not come with you. I designed this. It does not exist yet."
CTA"Reply DROP if you would actually keep this on your desk, and tell me the ONE thing you would refuse to leave behind."
Comment promptThe second clause forces a specific, costly answer (a named object), which is the real instrument.
What gets testedDoes the OBJECTS category pull real intent, and is demand for a physical object or a digital plan. Threshold: named-object DROP replies of reached accounts at or above 8 percent reads as real pull. Bare "DROP" is weak signal only.

06

The backend experience

The part nobody sees. Every reaction becomes a logged signal, and signal is the only thing allowed to graduate into a product.

Demand Signal Ledger (one row per platform cut)

date · week_id · theme · piece_id · format · platform · role_in_funnel · category_tag · audience_tag · signal_probe_text · reach · weak_signal · strong_signal_count · save_to_reach_ratio · reply_author_type · inbound_dm_intent · physical_vs_digital_pull · repeat_engager · hero_durability · crossed_threshold · next_action

DM workflow
  • Personal reply (not canned): thank, ask the use-case, tag interest level.
  • Drafted by the OS, sent by hand.
  • Every DM logged: inbound_dm_intent + audience_tag + physical_vs_digital_pull.
Email / waitlist tags
  • category (objects), archetype (founder/creative/coach/operator)
  • form pull (physical vs digital), intent (weak / strong / inbound-ask)
  • the owned, un-deplatformable list, grown every week
Weekly signal review → graduation
  • Read the ledger against the 7 signals + thresholds, write one next_action.
  • Graduates only when, across 2+ weeks: at or above 8 percent named-reply pull AND 10+ inbound "make this real" DMs AND 20+ repeat-engagers.
  • Graduation triggers a separate operator-approved build, payment follows proof. It is permission to scope an offer, not a crown.
07

The money path

No product crowned. Each route is inbound-gated and payment follows proof. This is how money could realistically appear.

01

Paid concept commission

A brand or founder asks "design X like this." Scope a one-off. Triggered by inbound asks, never outreach.

02

Limited physical drop

If OBJECTS pulls and physical demand is proven: a capped, numbered, made-to-order run. Waitlist first, no inventory risk.

03

Brand / license inquiry

A brand wants the aesthetic or the object licensed. A license or retainer, on their inbound ask.

04

Waitlist for production

The cheapest money signal. A refundable deposit only after strong, repeated proof. Still gated.

05

Sponsor / partner inquiry

A tool or brand sponsors the teardown or drop format, once the format has an audience.

06

Later product line

A retainer or a standing line, never pitched, accepted only when inbound demand is undeniable and recurring.

Across all six: no rails until a real yes, no price before demand, the hypothesis clears the graduation bar first.

The life path

Why this is worth building, beyond a feed. What it compounds toward if it works.

More control

Owned IP and an owned audience, not a client queue. The brand and the list are yours and un-deplatformable.

LA house and family life

A faceless, location-free engine that earns from taste and an audience, so income is not tied to where you stand or whose set you are on.

Less employer dependence

A clean, employer-safe lane with its own name and revenue logic, built to fund a runway, never in conflict with the day job.

Creative leverage

One authored object a week compounds into a body of work, a reputation for taste, and a portfolio brands come to, instead of one-off labor.

Global market

Objects and taste travel. A faceless design world sells to anyone with a screen, not just a local booking radius.

AI-proof taste and ownership

AI makes images cheap and taste scarce. The moat is the point of view and the owned brand, which a model cannot hand to the next person.

09

Visual direction board

The actual look. Quiet luxury editorial restraint, object on white, near monochrome, exactly one disciplined accent.

color palette
paper #FBF8F2
bone #F1EBDF
ink #1B1916
soft #6E675C
brass #9A7430 · the one accent
white #FFFFFF · the stage

Near monochrome. The brass appears only on the mark, the chosen variant, and the one live CTA. Never teal/orange, never neon, never red.

typography
Made World
editorial serif display · Didot/Baskerville feel
SANS LABELS · WIDE TRACKING · QUIET

Serif for the voice and the object names. Sans, small, wide-tracked, for labels and placards. One masthead, one line, one mark. No filler type.

image style
  • Object on seamless white, top-down or eye-level, one object per frame.
  • Soft single-source light, one long quiet shadow.
  • Generous negative space. Museum-placard captions.
  • No people, no hands, no clutter, no brand logos, no AI gradient sheen.
motion style
  • Hard cuts, not fades. Objects slide in and out of frame.
  • Slow quarter-rotate on the final. One soft mechanical click.
  • Loop that re-asks the opening so the replay reads as a punchline.
website style
  • Editorial, lots of white, thin rules, small-caps labels.
  • One accent, one CTA per screen. No store chrome.
  • The page feels like a quiet gallery, not a shop.
packaging / product style
  • Bone box, debossed mark, a numbered edition seal in brass foil.
  • One printed rule-of-use card per object, placard typeset.
  • The object arrives looking like it was always meant to exist.
what it feels like at its best

Like walking into a quiet room where one object sits under a single light, and you understand, before anyone explains it, that someone decided every part of it on purpose.

Higgsfield generation plan

Spend approved in principle for the hero plate only. Nothing is generated here. This is the plan and the approval checkpoint.

The exact 5 hero images worth generating first
#imageprompt direction
1THE ONE, final, heroTop-down, seamless pure-white sweep. One shallow undivided machined tray, single continuous r8 radius, no dividers, no lid, no labels, matte aluminum or bone resin. Soft single-source light from upper left, one long low-contrast shadow. Museum product still. No logo, no text, no hands, no reflection-noise. Square + 9:16 crops.
2The 3-variant gridThree identical trays side by side on white, only the corner radius differs (tight r4, chosen r8, soft r12). Even spacing, identical light, top-down. The middle one a hair more present. Clean catalogue grid.
3The before/after swap pairTwo frames: a generic twelve-slot molded organizer (busy, additive), and the single empty tray (considered). Same white, same scale, same light, so the only variable is the subtraction. For the teardown-to-hero motion.
4The rule-of-use placard stillThe tray on white with a small typeset placard card beside it reading the rule. Editorial, calm, lots of negative space, placard in wide-tracked sans. The status-object beauty shot.
5The edition / packaging stillThe bone box, debossed mark, brass-foil numbered seal, the tray half-revealed. Quiet luxury unboxing-as-object. One light, one shadow, no hands. The "it could be real" proof frame.
budget cap

Hard weekly cap, operator-set. Proposed default ~20-30 credits (about 2cr per still per the ledger, the 5 plates plus a re-roll allowance). No generation beyond the cap without fresh approval.

approval checkpoint

Tuesday before hero day, the operator approves the exact prompt + the credit estimate. Nothing generates without that explicit go. No go means the hero ships as an on-white spec render at zero spend.

QA checklist before posting
  • Object on white only, no person, no face
  • No logo, brand color, or real-org mark
  • One-accent discipline holds, mark consistent
  • Reads at thumbnail, no AI tells (warp, sheen, artifacts)
  • Passes the External Visual Proof Gate (a human signs off; Claude is not the final visual authority)
