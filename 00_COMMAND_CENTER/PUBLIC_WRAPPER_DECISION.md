**Public wrapper for the 30-day test: BASEPLATE.** Founder-led, with **Bryceden Jones** named as the operator behind it, and **The Capability Dossier** as the offer.

- Wordmark / brand: **BASEPLATE**

## Why BASEPLATE, decisively (not a default, not caution)

1. **The name was already chosen on merit, for exactly this reason.** The historical brand work (`raw/00_BRIEF/BRAND_STRATEGY_2026-05-13/`) scored 28 names against 10 criteria and landed on BASEPLATE at **9.5/10**, explicitly because it is infrastructure/engineering-coded: "names the operating layer underneath the work," "sounds like Vercel / Stripe / Substack," "engineering match: literal, BJ's actual identity," "time durability: engineering vocabulary holds decades." That scoring was done for a photography thesis. The name fits the **current operator-infrastructure thesis even better** than it fit the old one. A "baseplate" is literally the structural foundation a machine or column sits on. It is the buyer's own vocabulary.
2. **The wrapper infrastructure already exists and is paid for.** BASEPLATE Media, LLC is filed; the domain and handles are claimed. BJ is starting from zero on brand equity for this B2B play, and with debt and a need for fast, consistent cash, the correct move is to deploy the serious wrapper he already owns rather than invent and fund a new one. Zero migration cost (pre-audience for this market).
3. **It avoids the photography reduction.** "SNIPED Media" carries "media," which signals content/photography/agency to a critical-facilities owner. That is the exact reduction the thesis forbids. BASEPLATE carries none of it.
4. **It is serious, premium, and scalable, with no fake platform claim.** BASEPLATE presents as a founder-led operator practice, not a claimed platform or registry. It can grow into the eventual infrastructure company if the thesis earns it, without claiming anything now.
5. **Founder-led is what actually converts at zero equity.** The offer's entire differentiation is insider credibility, which lives in a person, not a logo. Naming Bryceden Jones as the operator behind BASEPLATE removes the "who are these guys" friction a brand-new studio name creates, and costs nothing to establish. Person = trust now; BASEPLATE = the serious shell that scales.

## Why the rejected options lose

- **SNIPED Media:** "media" reduces it to photography/content to this buyer. Rejected.
- **SNIPED (alone):** still tied to the photography company and an edgy register that fits IG mythology, not procurement trust. Rejected.
- **BJ personal name alone:** strong on trust, but gives no shell to grow into the infrastructure company and reads as a freelancer; better to name BJ *inside* BASEPLATE than as the whole brand. Used as the operator anchor, not the wrapper.
- **The Capability Dossier as standalone:** an offer is not a wrapper; it cannot expand beyond itself and reads narrow. It is the offer under BASEPLATE, not the brand.
- **A new temporary operator studio wrapper:** inventing and funding a brand-new name at zero equity, with debt and a need for fast cash, is the wrong move when a stronger, infra-coded, already-owned name (BASEPLATE) exists.
