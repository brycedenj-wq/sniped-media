## 1. Purpose and scope
A reusable persuasion canon so every outbound surface (subject line, email, DM, call, proposal, price, objection, follow-up) is built on the same trust logic, not improvised and not generic cold-email tricks. It encodes the best source on each lever (Maister on trust, Enns on selling and pricing, Cialdini on influence, de Botton/Simler-Hanson on status, Thompson on exposure) so the output is unfairly better than generic outreach. Apply it to every conversation; it is the "why" behind the playbook's "what."

## 2. Master lever: lower self-orientation
Maister: **T = (C + R + I) / S.** Self-orientation (S) is the divisor, so doubling self-focus halves trust regardless of how high credibility goes. A cold message's default read is maximum S: "this person wants my money." **Every touch exists to drive S down.** Concretely: lead with their problem, not your offer; give before you ask; one line about yourself, not a resume; an easy out instead of pressure; "I will tell you honestly if I can't help." This is the thread through every section below. If a line raises self-orientation, cut it.

## 3. Trust equation applied to outbound
- **Credibility (C):** one line of insider authority ("I came up on the operator side, stood on the floor") + the named methodology (The Capability Dossier). Enough to be believed, not a performance.
- **Reliability (R):** do exactly what you say (15 minutes is 15 minutes; the sample is ready; the proposal lands in 24 hours). Reliability is earned in micro-promises kept across the sequence.
- **Intimacy (I):** make it safe to engage. The security-safe promise ("nothing sensitive, you approve everything") is the intimacy lever; it lowers the buyer's risk of sharing.
- **Self-orientation (S):** the killer. Minimized by problem-first framing, the free teardown, the easy out, and refusing to perform "let me prove I'm worth it." The asset and the diagnosis do the talking.

## 4. Subject-line principles
Hit Makers MAYA (Most Advanced Yet Acceptable) + Exposure. Bold but instantly comprehensible, in their vocabulary: `your reqs`, `your techs`, `your bid`, `commissioning`. Lowercase, 2 to 3 words. A sliver of curiosity, never clever, never salesy (clever reads as marketing = high S). The subject's only job is to feel like a real 1-to-1 note from someone who knows their world.

## 5. E1 opener logic
- **Authority + Story (Cialdini + Hit Makers):** one sentence of insider authority that also plants the operator archetype. More than one sentence about yourself spikes S.
- **Specialize + Frame (Enns #1 + Maister "Frame"):** immediately their world, re-articulate their problem before proposing anything ("your recruiters are good; to a candidate weighing three offers you look like one more logo").
- **Diagnose, do not prescribe (Enns #3):** name the gap, do not dump the product.
- **The ask is tiny** (15 minutes) and the value is their outcome (win the hire/bid). No deliverable list in E1.

## 6. E2 free-teardown logic
Reciprocity (Cialdini) balanced against Enns #8 (do not solve before paid). The free "candidate-view teardown" (how a top tech reads your firm right now) gives value first, which triggers reciprocity and drops S. But it is a 60-second observation/diagnosis, a taste that proves you see what they can't, NOT free consulting and NOT the dossier. Give the insight, not the work.

## 7. E3 close logic
Enns selectivity (#6) and head-held-high (#12) + Cialdini commitment. "Worth 15 minutes, or is this not a priority right now?" The easy out is the mechanism: it reads as selective and unbothered, lowers S, and paradoxically lifts replies. The 15-minute call is the small yes that sets up the bigger yes (commitment/consistency).

## 8. Follow-up principles
Hit Makers Exposure, not nagging. 3 touches, 48 to 72 hours apart, each adding a NEW angle, never "just bumping this." Repeated viewing in a trusted context compounds; repetition without new value is spam. The break-up ("I'll leave the door open; when a hard bid or a stuck req hits, that's the moment this earns its keep") leaves goodwill and is pure low-self-orientation.

## 9. LinkedIn DM principles
Liking (Cialdini) + cluster logic (Hit Makers #5). Lead with one specific observation about their firm (attention/similarity builds liking), then the operator line, then the small ask. Treat the critical-facilities world as a cluster, not a market: each contact is a node, referrals inside the cluster compound, so even a no is worth leaving warm. DMs beat posts for this buyer; no public posting required to run this.

## 10. Objection-handling psychology
- **"We have marketing"** to Frame as additive, not competitive: "This isn't marketing, it's proof built insider-to-insider; your marketing person can use all of it." Lowers S (you threaten no one).
- **"Security/NDA"** to the Intimacy lever: "Nothing sensitive, you approve everything." Raises I and lowers S at once; makes engaging safe.
- **"Send info"** to the assumptive "five minutes on a call beats a doc," offered once, selectively (Enns), not chased.
- **"We win on relationships/price"** to Frame: "This is what makes the relationship transferable to the people who haven't met you yet." Reframes the objection into the need.

## 11. Discovery-call psychology
Run the WEDGE_OFFER_SPEC discovery script consciously as Maister's 5 behaviors: **Engage** (the 90-second operator opener) to **Listen** (ask their hardest open role/bid, then shut up) to **Frame** (re-articulate the real problem) to **Envision** (paint the won hire/bid) to **Commit** (agree on the Pilot). The opener "I'll tell you honestly if I can't help" is the self-orientation reducer; the diagnosis does the selling, the way VIB lets the asset talk.

## 12. Pricing psychology
Enns Pricing Creativity + premium-as-insurance.
- **Price the client, not the job:** anchor to their outcome ($20-45K placement, six-figure bid), never to your cost.
- **Say the price before you show it (#4):** verbal on the call; the one-page proposal (#6) confirms, it is not a presentation.
- **Premium = insurance:** name the exact risk being insured ("the cost of looking generic in a bidding war is far higher than the pilot").
- **Anchor high:** mention the higher tiers exist so the Pilot reads as the easy entry; sell the Pilot for a clean first proof signal.
- **Do not discount by default:** the price itself is part of the value signal (see section 13).

## 13. Status / hidden-motive layer
Status psychology (de Botton) + hidden motives (Simler/Hanson). The owner's stated motive is "win the hire/bid." The actual motive is to signal that the firm is a serious, credible operator, to candidates, clients, and peers. So the copy taps the status competition: "the firm that looks the most credible wins the signature." The price is part of the signal (de Botton's snobbery filter): a $2,500 proof asset signals seriousness in a way $300 content never can. Sell to the part of the buyer that wants the firm seen and respected, not just "photos."

## 14. Outbound voice rules
Direct, observant, peer-to-peer operator, not corporate and not creator. Lowercase subjects, plain text, human cadence, under 80 characters per sentence, 7th-grade clarity. One specific observation beats a paragraph of pitch. Confident but unbothered (head held high). Never hype, never clever-for-clever's-sake, never desperate. The voice itself is a self-orientation signal: a calm operator who does not need the sale reads as more trustworthy than an eager vendor.

## 15. Why the email works
It wins because it inverts the default. A cold email usually screams self-orientation (your product, your ask, your urgency); this one opens with their problem, proves insider authority in one line, gives a free insight, and asks for a small, easy-to-decline yes. Low S + real C + safe I = trust forms before the pitch. The buyer feels diagnosed, not sold. That is the whole mechanism.

## 16. Reply-handling psychology
- **Positive:** book within 2 hours (Reliability); keep the calm operator tone, do not over-thank or over-sell (that spikes S).
- **Objection:** treat it as a request for safety or framing, not a no; answer with section 10, offer the sample, stay selective.
- **Neutral/"not now":** accept gracefully, set a follow-up, leave goodwill (cluster logic, the no is a future node).
- **Negative/unsubscribe:** remove immediately, never re-contact; protect the cluster's regard for you.
Every reply is logged to PROOF_LOOPS the same day (real interest = money/access committed; "looks great, send info" = Yellow, not proof).

## 17. What not to do
- No fake social proof (none exists yet; lean on authority + specificity until real testimonials/clients exist; never fabricate).
- No fake urgency or invented scarcity.
- No neediness, no chasing, no "just following up again" (raises S, lowers regard).
- No spam language or spam-trigger words.
- No creator/photographer/"content"/"shoot"/"media kit" language (collapses the offer).
- No platform/registry/network or AI-company overclaim.
- No discounting as the default move (the price carries the signal).
- No begging the buyer or performing worth.
