## 1. Purpose and scope
Govern the moment a stranger becomes a conversation: how BJ diagnoses, qualifies, decides whether to pitch The Capability Dossier, handles objections, introduces price, and logs proof. Scope is the reply, the 15-minute call, and the immediate next step. The proposal/invoice mechanics live in Artifact #4 (not built yet). BJ runs every call; admin never touches it.

## 2. When this playbook activates
The instant a prospect replies to outbound (any channel) or books a Calendly call. From that point it is BJ's, non-delegable (per the outbound playbook handoff rule). Admin's job is already done; this is the human conversation.

## 3. Discovery philosophy
- **Diagnose before prescribe (Enns #3):** understand their situation before proposing anything. The call is a diagnosis, not a demo.
- **Lower self-orientation (Maister, the master lever):** the call is about their problem, not your offer. The diagnosis does the selling; you do not perform worth.
- **Selective posture (Enns #6, #12):** you are deciding if you can help them, not begging them to buy. Head held high. You can say no.
- **Proof over praise:** "this is great" is not a yes. A named role/bid + money committed + access granted is a yes. Treat praise as Yellow.
- **No needy selling:** no chasing, no discounting to close, no over-explaining. Calm operator who does not need the sale reads as the most trustworthy person in the call.

## 4. Call outcomes (decide one by the end)
- **Qualified Pilot:** decision-maker + a named role/bid + urgency + budget + a non-secure angle. Pitch the Pilot, move to proposal.
- **Nurture (Yellow):** real interest but no specific role/bid yet, or timing is later. Set a follow-up trigger, leave warm, log Yellow.
- **Disqualify (Red):** no decision-maker, price-only, nothing showable, treats it as headshots. End cleanly, leave goodwill (cluster node).
- **AI-Ops / system pull signal:** they ask for more than the artifact (recruiting/bid/documentation/reporting systems). Note it, do not chase it yet (later layer); log the signal.
- **C4 / founder fallback signal:** the energy and fit point to founders/operators rather than infra procurement. Note it; it informs the 30-day infra-vs-C4 gate in PROOF_LOOPS.

## 5. Pre-call prep (5 minutes, from the admin row)
- Review the lead row: company, decision-maker, segment, urgency signal, notes.
- Review their public surface: careers page, capability statement, LinkedIn, recent posts (public only; no employer data).
- Identify the likely role or bid pain (the specific thing that hurts).
- Prepare ONE hypothesis, not a pitch: e.g., "I bet a top commissioning tech can't tell why they beat the next shop." You will test it, not assert it. (Specificity feeds the liking/specialize levers.)

## 6. 15-minute call structure (Maister 5 behaviors)
1. **Open (1-2 min) [Engage]:** who you are in one line, why you keep it short, and the honest frame ("I'll tell you if I can't help").
2. **Context (3 min) [Listen]:** what they do, where their critical-facilities work sits, demand vs competition.
3. **Pain (3 min) [Listen/Frame]:** what decided the last lost hire or bid; how candidates/clients form a first impression today.
4. **Current proof surface (2 min):** what they use now (careers page, capability statement, referrals) and who handles it.
5. **Cost of generic appearance (1 min) [Frame]:** what a stuck role or a lost bid costs per week / in dollars.
6. **Security / approval constraints (1 min) [Intimacy]:** what can and can't be shown; you design around it by default.
7. **Decision process (1 min):** who decides, what the approval path is.
8. **Fit judgment (1 min) [Envision]:** say honestly whether you can help and paint the won hire/bid.
9. **Next step (1-2 min) [Commit]:** if qualified, propose the Pilot on the specific role/bid and set the start.

## 7. Exact call script (BJ's plain voice)
- **Open:** "Thanks for the time, I keep these short. I came up on the operator side of critical facilities, field and data-center work. I build proof assets that move a hire or a bid. I want to understand your situation first, then tell you straight if I can even help."
- **Context:** "What does your shop do, and where's most of your data-center or mission-critical work sitting right now? Is the buildout giving you more demand than you can staff, or more competition on bids?"
- **Pain:** "When you lost a hire or a bid you should've won, what usually decided it?" then "How does a candidate or a client form their first impression of you today?"
- **Current proof:** "What are you using now for that, careers page, capability statement, referrals? Who handles it, and are you happy with it?"
- **Cost:** (staffing) "What's a hard role open right now, and what's it costing you a week it sits?" (subs) "What's a bid coming up you really want, and what's it worth?"
- **Security:** "What can and can't be shown given your clients' security and NDAs? I build around that by default, nothing sensitive, you approve everything before it exists."
- **Decision:** "If this is a fit, who else weighs in, and how do you usually green-light something like this?"
- **Fit + next step:** "Based on what you said, the Pilot Capability Dossier fits, it's built around [that role/bid], you approve every asset, you own it, one to two weeks. Want to run one on [the specific thing]? I can start this week." (If not a fit: "Honestly, I don't think I'm the right spend for you right now, and I'd rather tell you that than sell you something.")

## 8. Qualification criteria (the minimum viable buyer)
All of the following:
- A **named role or named bid** (not "we hire sometimes").
- **Owner / decision-maker access** (on the call or a clear path to them).
- **Urgency** (a stuck req, an imminent bid, a poaching competitor).
- **Economic value** quantifiable (a placement = $20-45K; a bid = six figures).
- **A non-secure angle** they can show (people/work in controlled settings).
- **An approval path** (they can say yes and sign off on assets).
- **Willingness to pay** for proof, not just praise it.

## 9. Green / Yellow / Red scoring
- **Green:** decision-maker on the call + a specific named role/bid + quantified cost + discretionary budget + a non-secure angle + frustration with how they look on paper. Pitch the Pilot now.
- **Yellow:** interested but no specific role/bid, or "send info," or a marketing person is involved but not the owner, or timing is "next quarter." Nurture, set a trigger, log Yellow.
- **Red:** no decision-maker, "we win on price only," can't show anything (all hard-NDA with no non-secure angle), wants free/cheap content, treats it as headshots, no real role or bid. Disqualify cleanly.

## 10. Disqualification rules
Disqualify (politely, leaving goodwill) when: no decision-maker and no path to one; nothing non-secure can ever be shown; the buyer wants commodity content/headshots; price-only with no value conversation possible; no real role or bid exists. Saying no early protects your time and your selectivity (Enns #6), and a clean no keeps the cluster node warm for later.

## 11. Objection handling (from the persuasion canon section 10)
- **"We already have marketing":** "Good, this isn't marketing, your marketing person can use everything I deliver. This is a capability proof asset built by someone who's stood on the floor, tied to a hire or a bid." (Additive, not competitive, lowers self-orientation.)
- **"We can't show facility details":** "That's the default assumption, not a problem. Nothing sensitive, no secure floors, no equipment layouts, no client names. We work with your people and your work in non-secure settings you control, and you approve every asset." (Intimacy lever; makes it safe.)
- **"Send me info":** "Happy to, but info rarely lands without context. Give me five minutes on the call to show one sample and you'll know immediately if it's worth it." (Offered once, selectively; if still no, send the 60-second sample + one proposal, set a follow-up, log Yellow.)
- **"Too expensive":** "Compared to what? One filled role is worth 20 to 45K to you; the Pilot is a fraction of one outcome. If a single hire or bid moves because of this, it pays for itself many times over." (Price the client, premium-as-insurance; do not discount.)
- **"Not now":** "Fair. The moment this earns its keep is a hard bid or a stuck req. When that hits, that's the call to make. I'll leave the door open." (Selective, goodwill, log Yellow with a trigger.)
- **"Sounds like photography/content":** "It's not. Photography is one instrument. The deliverable is a capability dossier, the document a candidate or a client actually believes. It moves a hire or a bid, it's not a gallery." (Kills the reduction.)
- **"We need to think about it":** "Of course. What specifically do you want to be sure of? If it's whether it'll land for [the role/bid], I can show you the sample and we decide together." (Surface the real hesitation; do not chase.)

## 12. When to pitch The Capability Dossier
Pitch the Pilot only when the call is Green: a decision-maker with a named role/bid, urgency, budget, and a non-secure angle. Pitch it as the Pilot tied to that specific outcome, scoped, owned by them, 1 to 2 weeks. Diagnose first, then prescribe; never lead with the offer.

## 13. When NOT to pitch
Do not pitch when: it's Yellow (no named role/bid yet, nurture instead); it's Red (disqualify); the decision-maker isn't present (book the person who can say yes); or you genuinely can't help (say so, Enns selectivity). Pitching a weak fit lowers your credibility for the cluster.

## 14. How to introduce price (Pricing Creativity)
- **Say the price before you show it (Enns #4):** verbal on the call, the one-page proposal confirms.
- **Price the client, not the job:** anchor to their outcome ($20-45K placement, six-figure bid), never to your cost.
- **Pilot from $2,500**, scoped to one role/bid, 1 to 2 weeks, they own it.
- **Anchor high:** mention the higher tiers exist (Standard, Premium) so the Pilot reads as the easy entry; sell the Pilot for the clean first proof signal.
- **Premium = insurance:** "the cost of looking generic in a bidding war is far higher than the pilot."
- **No discount default:** the price is part of the value signal (status/snobbery filter). Hold it; do not perform "let me prove I'm worth it."

## 15. How to identify AI-Ops / system pull
Listen for the buyer asking for MORE than the artifact: "can you also help us with our recruiting process / our bid documentation / our reporting / our intake." That is the systems-layer pull (the AI-Ops upsell, a later layer). When you hear it: acknowledge, do not chase or build it on the spot, note it in the proof log as a system-pull signal. It is evidence the wedge-to-AI-Ops path is real, to be acted on after proof, and only with the employment/IP attorney check before any data-center-operations tooling.

## 16. How to identify named-author media / intelligence signal
Listen for: "I follow the buildout," "where do you post," "can you put this where candidates see it," interest in your perspective on the industry, or them sharing their own content struggles. That signals the distribution/media layer (BJ as named author) has pull. Note it; it informs when to start the media seed. Do not pivot the call to it.

## 17. Post-call follow-up
- **Green:** send the one-page proposal + Pilot invoice within 24 hours (Reliability). Reference the specific role/bid discussed.
- **Yellow:** send the 60-second sample + a short note, set the follow-up trigger, log.
- **Red:** a clean, warm close ("not the right spend for you now, I'll leave the door open"), no chasing.
Every follow-up keeps the calm operator tone; no over-thanking, no pressure (both spike self-orientation).

## 18. Proposal handoff
On a Green call, hand off to the Proposal + Invoice Playbook (Artifact #4, to be built): one-page proposal, Pilot scope tied to the named outcome, deposit invoice via Pixieset Studio Manager. This playbook ends at "yes, send me the proposal"; #4 turns it into money committed.

## 19. Proof-log update rules (PROOF_LOOPS + WEDGE §12)
Log every call the same day as a Proof entry under H1/H2 + "infra-buyer pull (staffing)/(subs)": fields = price quoted, paid y/n, the named role/bid, room-opened y/n, access granted y/n, system-pull y/n. Real proof = money committed or access granted + a named role/bid. Praise with no money and no access = Yellow, not proof. Move H1/H2 from UNTESTED to TESTING on the first real call; toward KEEP/KILL/ITERATE as signals land. Run the T1/T2/T3 gates.

## 20. BJ self-check after each call (60 seconds)
- Did I diagnose or did I pitch too early?
- Did I keep self-orientation low (their problem, not my offer)?
- Was I selective, or did I get needy?
- Did I get a named role/bid, or just praise?
- Did I hold the price, or did I flinch toward a discount?
- Is this Green, Yellow, or Red, and did I log it honestly?

## 21. What not to do
No needy selling, no chasing, no discounting to close. No guaranteeing a hire or a bid (you build proof, you do not promise outcomes). No pitching before diagnosing. No leading with the offer. No fabricated social proof or fake clients. No facility-sensitive talk or imagery. No employer data or targeting employer clients/vendors/employees. No reducing it to photography/content/headshots. No promising the AI-Ops/systems layer on the spot.

## 22. Guardrails
No fake proof. No guaranteed hires/bids. No platform/registry/network claim. No photography-only reduction (the dossier is the deliverable; media is the instrument). No employer/confidential information. No targeting the employer's customers/vendors/employees via inside knowledge. No facility-sensitive imagery. No needy selling. No over-discounting. No automation before proof. The AI-Ops layer needs the employment/IP attorney check before anything touching data-center operations. Old SNIPED/BASEPLATE material is historical evidence, not law.
