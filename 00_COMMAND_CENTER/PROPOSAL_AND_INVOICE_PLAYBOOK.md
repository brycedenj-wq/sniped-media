## 1. Purpose and scope
Turn a qualified (Green) discovery call into a clean paid Pilot, without needy selling, fake proof, over-discounting, or scope drift. Scope is the proposal, the price conversation, the invoice, payment terms, and the close. The build/delivery of the dossier itself lives in Artifact #5 (Sample/Delivery, not built yet). BJ runs this; admin never touches a proposal or a price.

## 2. When this playbook activates
The moment a discovery call lands Green and the prospect says "yes, send me the proposal" (the handoff point from Artifact #3). Not before: a Yellow call gets nurtured, a Red call gets disqualified. No proposal goes to a non-decision-maker.

## 3. Close philosophy
- **Price the client, not the labor (Enns Pricing Creativity):** anchor to their outcome ($20-45K placement, six-figure bid), never to hours.
- **One-page proposal (Enns #6):** the proposal confirms an agreement, it is not a pitch deck.
- **Verbal price before written (Enns #4):** the price was said on the call; the proposal documents it. No surprises in the PDF.
- **No needy selling (Maister, low self-orientation):** you are confirming a fit, not begging. Calm operator.
- **No discount as default:** the price is part of the value signal. Hold it.
- **Proof over praise:** the close is money committed + a named role/bid, not "looks great."

## 4. Inputs required from discovery (must have all before a proposal)
Named role or bid; decision-maker (the person who can say yes); urgency (stuck req / imminent bid); economic value (quantified); approval path (who signs off); security constraints (what can/can't be shown); scope fit (a real non-secure angle). If any is missing, it is not proposal-ready, go back to discovery or nurture.

## 5. Proposal qualification gate
- **Send the proposal when:** the call was Green, all section 4 inputs are present, and the decision-maker asked for it or agreed to the Pilot scope verbally.
- **Do NOT send when:** no decision-maker; no named role/bid; "just send me something to look at" with no commitment (that is a brochure request, not a buyer); price-only shopping; nothing non-secure to work with. Sending a proposal into a non-qualified call trains the prospect to treat you as a vendor and lowers your selectivity.

## 6. Offer structure
- **The Capability Dossier**, Pilot tier.
- **Pilot from $2,500**, scoped to ONE role or ONE bid.
- **1 to 2 week delivery.**
- **Client owns the final asset** (keeps and reuses everything).
- **Approval before anything is public:** the firm signs off on every image and line before delivery; nothing publishes without written approval.
- Higher tiers exist (Standard, Premium per WEDGE_OFFER_SPEC) as the anchor, but the first sale is the Pilot for a clean proof signal.

## 7. One-page proposal structure
1. **Context:** one line naming their firm and the specific role/bid discussed.
2. **Problem:** the gap, in their words from the call ("strong crew, generic on paper, losing the candidate/bid on perception").
3. **Outcome:** what the dossier moves (win the hire / win the bid). Outcome-first, not deliverable-first.
4. **Scope:** the Pilot deliverables (proof narrative, approved non-secure imagery of people/work, a reusable image set, hiring/bid copy blocks, a one-screen capability profile, private delivery). Tied to the one role/bid.
5. **Timeline:** 1 to 2 weeks from approved scope.
6. **Investment:** the price (verbal-first, now confirmed). Anchored to the outcome.
7. **Approval / next step:** what happens on yes (deposit invoice, scope sign-off, start date).
8. **Decision window:** a real, low-pressure window ("this proposal holds for 14 days; if a hard bid or req is driving it, we can start this week"). No fake countdown, no manufactured scarcity.
One page. No logo wall, no fake testimonials, no platform language.

## 8. Proposal copy blocks (reusable, BJ plain voice)
- **Context block:** "For [Firm], built around [the open commissioning role / the upcoming data-center bid] we discussed."
- **Problem block:** "Your crew is excellent. On paper, to a candidate weighing three offers (or a client comparing bidders), you look like everyone else. That perception is deciding hires and bids you should win."
- **Outcome block:** "A capability dossier that makes [Firm] the obvious yes to a top tech (or the credible bidder a client believes), built by someone who's stood on the floor. Not marketing. Proof."
- **Scope block:** "Pilot, scoped to [the one role/bid]: a tight capability narrative, approved non-secure imagery of your people and work, a reusable image set you own, hiring/bid copy blocks, and a one-screen capability profile, delivered privately. You approve every asset before anything is public."
- **Investment block:** "Investment: $2,500, scoped to this one outcome. One filled role is worth 20 to 45K to you; one won bid is six figures. This is a fraction of one result."
- **Next-step block:** "On yes: I send a deposit invoice, we lock the scope (what's shown, what's off-limits), and I start within [X] days. You own everything I deliver."

## 9. Pricing rules
- **Pilot starts at $2,500.** This is the floor for the entry tier.
- **Anchor to placement/bid value**, never to hours or "days of work."
- **Do not price by hours.** Value-based only.
- **No cheap packages**, no "starter" discount tier, no headshot-priced option.
- **No default discounting.** If a buyer pushes, hold the price and reframe the value (premium = insurance); do not flinch.
- **When to quote above $2,500:** a named bid worth six figures, multi-role/multi-crew scope, a tight deadline, or a buyer whose outcome value is clearly large, quote the Standard ($6-10K) or Premium ($15-25K) tier per WEDGE_OFFER_SPEC, anchored high, with the Pilot as the entry option.

## 10. Payment terms
- **Deposit upfront starts the work.** Recommended: 50% deposit on signed scope, 50% on delivery, OR 100% upfront for the Pilot (simplest for a $2,500 entry, fewer moving parts).
- **What starts work:** a paid deposit (or full Pilot payment) AND a signed scope. Both, not one.
- **Invoice timing:** send the deposit invoice within 24 hours of the verbal yes (Reliability lever).
- **What NOT to begin before payment:** no shooting, no production, no asset creation, no "I'll just get started." Nothing begins until the deposit clears and scope is signed. (Enns #10: refuse to work at a loss / on spec.)

## 11. Invoice requirements
- **Legal name: Baseplate, LLC** (the filed entity, not "BASEPLATE Media, LLC").
- **Offer name:** The Capability Dossier, Pilot.
- **Scope:** one line naming the role/bid and the deliverables.
- **Price:** the agreed amount.
- **Payment due date:** clear (e.g., due on receipt to start, or net-7).
- **Notes / terms:** deposit vs balance split, ownership ("client owns final assets"), approval clause ("client approves all assets before delivery"), what starts work. Run invoicing through Pixieset Studio Manager for v1 (one tool).

## 12. Scope boundaries
- **Included:** the section 6 Pilot deliverables, tied to the one named role/bid; one round of reasonable revisions on the copy/selects.
- **Not included:** additional roles/bids, extra shoot days, ongoing content/retainer, a live website build, anything inside a secure facility, additional revision rounds beyond the agreed one.
- **Extra revisions / added scope:** quoted separately as an add-on or the next tier, never absorbed for free (no scope drift).
- **Travel / shoot / production notes:** non-secure settings the firm controls; any travel beyond a set radius is quoted; the firm provides access to its own people/shop/yard.

## 13. Security and approval language
Standard clause on every proposal and invoice: "Security-safe by design. Nothing sensitive is shown, no secure floors, no equipment layouts, no client names, nothing under NDA. We work only in non-secure settings you control, and you approve every image and every line in writing before anything is delivered or made public." This is the Intimacy lever and the WEDGE_OFFER_SPEC §5 hard rules; it is non-negotiable and it protects both sides.

## 14. Objection handling after proposal
- **"Too expensive":** "Compared to what? One filled role is 20 to 45K, one won bid is six figures. The Pilot is a fraction of one outcome. If it moves a single result, it pays for itself many times over." (Premium = insurance; no discount.)
- **"Need to think":** "Of course. What specifically do you want to be sure of? If it's whether it lands for [the role/bid], I can walk you through the sample and we decide together." (Surface the real hesitation; do not chase.)
- **"Send me examples":** "Here's the sample on a quick call so you see it in context; I'd rather show you one good one than email a folder." (The sample, not a dump; offered once.)
- **"Need approval":** "Makes sense. Who else weighs in, and what would they need to see? I can give you a one-pager to forward, or join a quick call with them." (Move toward the real decision-maker.)
- **"Can we do cheaper":** "I keep one tier at the entry for exactly this, the Pilot at $2,500. Below that it stops being the asset that actually moves a hire or a bid, and I won't sell you something that won't work." (Hold price, Enns selectivity.)
- **"Can you just do photos/content":** "That's not what this is. Photography is one instrument; the deliverable is a capability proof asset a candidate or client actually believes. If you only want photos, I'm not the right spend." (Kills the reduction; selective.)

## 15. Follow-up cadence
After the proposal: one follow-up at 48 to 72 hours (add a new angle, not "just checking in"), one at the decision-window mark, then a clean break-up ("I'll leave the door open; when the bid or req is live, that's the call"). Max 2 to 3 touches. No needy follow-up, no daily pinging. Goodwill preserved (cluster node).

## 16. Close / no-close decision rules
- **Close:** verbal yes + deposit invoice sent + scope signed + payment initiated. That is a real close (log it).
- **No-close, nurture:** interested but no commitment after the cadence, set a trigger (next bid/req), log Yellow.
- **No-close, walk:** price-only, wants free/cheap, no decision-maker, can't show anything. Disqualify cleanly.
A close is money committed, not a verbal "we're in" with no invoice paid.

## 17. What gets logged in PROOF_LOOPS
A Proof entry per proposal under H1/H2 + "infra-buyer pull (staffing)/(subs)": fields = tier quoted, price, proposal sent y/n, deposit paid y/n, named role/bid, scope signed y/n, room/access granted y/n. Real proof = deposit paid (money committed) + a named role/bid. A sent proposal with no payment is Yellow, not proof. This feeds the T1/T2 gates (a paid Pilot or a paid LOI = pass).

## 18. When AI-Ops / system pull gets logged
If, during the proposal or close, the buyer asks for more than the dossier (recruiting/bid/documentation/reporting systems), log it as a system-pull signal in PROOF_LOOPS. Do not scope or sell it now (later layer), and never anything touching data-center operations without the employment/IP attorney check. It is evidence the wedge-to-AI-Ops path is real, recorded for the 30-day gate.

## 19. When to walk away
Walk when: the buyer will only pay below the Pilot floor; demands free/spec work; has no decision authority and no path to it; can show nothing non-secure; or treats it as commodity content after the reframe. Walking protects the price, the positioning, and your time (Enns #6, #10). A clean walk keeps the cluster node warm.

## 20. What not to do
No discounting to close. No starting work before deposit + signed scope. No vague unlimited scope. No guaranteeing a hire or a bid. No fabricated proof, testimonials, or client names. No facility-sensitive content. No employer data or targeting employer clients/vendors/employees. No multi-page proposal-as-deck. No needy follow-up. No reducing it to photography/content.

## 21. Guardrails
No fake proof. No guaranteed hires/bids. No platform/registry/network public claim. No photography-only reduction (the dossier is the deliverable; media is the instrument). No employer/confidential information. No targeting the employer's customers/vendors/employees via inside knowledge. No facility-sensitive imagery. No work begins before agreed payment terms (deposit + signed scope). No vague unlimited scope. No needy follow-up. No default discounting. No automation before proof. Invoicing under the correct legal entity, Baseplate, LLC. Old SNIPED/BASEPLATE material is historical evidence, not law. Bible held.
