## 3. WHAT IT SELLS + THE INNOVATIVE MODEL

### The product (Layer 2)
A tight, ruthlessly edited line. Overproduce in the render stage, then cut to the signature (Dior: fewer, better things):
- **Entry POD pieces:** heavyweight tee, long-sleeve carrying the chapter's hero graphic. $48-58.
- **Core drop pieces:** the silhouette of the chapter, batch-cut after pre-order. $70-95.
- **Scarce heavyweight / outerwear:** the flagship of the chapter, numbered, batch-cut. $120-180.
- **Founding numbered editions:** 1/100 on the first drop, status-carrying.

But the product the customer *feels* they are buying is the chapter: the world, the register, the lineage. The cotton carries the floor; the world carries the margin (Agins: image is the form).

### The innovative model: WORLD-AS-PRODUCT. Render before you produce. (Layers 2 + 3)

**The core inversion, made concrete:** Afterlight renders the *entire editorial campaign* before a single unit is produced. For each proposed drop, the engine produces the hero frame, 3 to 5 supporting frames, a teaser film, the garment mockup on a body, and the pre-order landing block, all from the AI atmosphere tools, plus, for graduating worlds, a real Canon shoot. That full campaign goes to a cheap response test. Only the worlds that pull response graduate to production. Losers die in the render stage at near-zero cost.

This is POD/inventory logic applied to *creative direction itself*, which is the genuinely new move. A normal POD brand still guesses which graphic to make. Afterlight never guesses. It renders ten chapter-worlds, tests them, and prints only the world the market already reacted to.

**Drops are chapters, not products.** Each drop is a chapter in an ongoing body of work (perennial logic + the Sculptural Gallery archive). Chapter 1 leads to Chapter 2; the buyer collects chapters. This converts one-time buyers into a returning roster, and lets the imagery sell the dream while the drop sells the product (Kapferer: advertising builds the aura, the drop activates the sale).

**Concentrate the bet** (blockbuster, Layer 1): one hero drop per chapter with disproportionate resources and one recognizable wearer in the cluster, then chain the next chapter off that proof. Never a diffuse 10-SKU spread.

## 4. HOW IT MAKES MONEY (real numbers)

### Zero-inventory economics (Layer 2)
The customer funds production. Every release opens as a **pre-order DROP with a hard close date.** Cash collected up front, COGS spent after. Two fulfillment lanes:
- **Lane 1, POD** (Printful/Printify): true zero inventory, evergreen catalog, long-tail.
- **Lane 2, batch-cut after pre-order:** once the pre-order count is known, place one finite batch order at a better unit cost, higher price point, zero overstock because quantity = confirmed orders. This is the scarcity lane.

### Margin per unit, the honest math
**POD heavyweight tee, $78 retail:**
- Blank + DTG print (Printful heavyweight): ~$18 to 22.
- Stripe/processing (~3% + $0.30): ~$2.65.
- Gross contribution per unit: **~$53 to 57** (~68-73%).
- Design cost: $0 (rendered). Model cost: $0 (consenting brand-face / real wearer). Photographer cost: $0 (Bryce's own camera). Sample shoot: $0 (rendered mockup). These are the four line items that normally kill a small apparel brand. Afterlight zeroes all four.

**Batch-cut heavyweight, $95 retail, 100-unit run:**
- Premium blank + screen print at volume: ~$14 to 18.
- Processing: ~$3.15.
- Gross contribution per unit: **~$74 to 78** (~78-82%).

**Founding numbered edition, $145, run of 100, hand-numbered hangtag:**
- Premium garment + print + hangtag + mailer ritual: ~$26 to 32.
- Processing: ~$4.65.
- Gross contribution per unit: **~$108 to 114** (~75-79%).
- 100 units sold through = **~$10,800 to 11,400 contribution** off one rendered chapter, zero inventory risk, near-zero creative spend.

Near-100% of gross survives to contribution because the expensive parts of apparel were always the people, not the cotton, and the people are the part Afterlight rendered or already had.

### Pricing posture (Layers 1 + 2)
Priced by the Value Equation, where the dream outcome is identity and belonging, so **price signals rank and can lead demand** (the luxury inversion). One price per piece, never itemized, never discounted, never justified in copy. A discount is a status violation that re-categorizes you into the dying middle (status-psychology). Hold the price cold.

### Pre-order / drop mechanic that gets first dollars with NO audience (Layer 2, THCPP)
Each drop is a 4 to 8 week **TEASE → HYPE → COLLECT → PUSH → PULL** campaign, not a one-day event:
- **TEASE** (3-4 wks out): AI mood content. Hint the world, never reveal the graphic.
- **HYPE** (2-3 wks out): the lifestyle, the tribe, the world.
- **COLLECT** (ongoing): capture email/SMS to the owned list. **Non-negotiable. Skipping it is the #1 conversion killer.**
- **PUSH** (launch + 48h): drive the owned list to the pre-order storefront.
- **PULL** (Day 3 to Week 4): retarget with the creative that already won *organically.* Free traffic pre-scores the paid creative, so spend only amplifies proven winners.

**The first-dollar mechanic, stated plainly:** a pure pre-order shadow-test with a hard unit threshold and a hard close date. No audience required, because the COLLECT list is built *during* the tease from the one seeded cluster, and the pre-order page collects real card authorizations before a single garment exists. Hit the threshold, you batch-cut and fulfill. Miss it, you auto-refund every pre-order and lose nothing but render time. First dollars come from the founding roster, not from a back catalog and not from ad spend.

### Distribution from zero (Layers 2 + 4)
1. **Follow the free:** give away the un-copyable world layer (the lookbook film, the chapter wallpapers, the "world" content) to earn attention. Monetize the garment downstream.
2. **Cluster, not broadcast:** seed ONE LA subculture deep. Cluster engagement is the win metric. Broad reach is a vanity stat. Refuse "try more platforms / go viral."
3. **AI content velocity as the unfair edge:** Higgsfield Marketing Studio + Virality Predictor pre-score every hook *before* posting, so a brand with zero followers still ships only top-scoring creative at a volume no manual operator could match.

### Honest about the clock (Layer 5, non-negotiable)
This brand monetizes on **audience and brand equity that do not yet exist.** POD + AI removed the capital, inventory, and design-cost objections that parked the clothing lane. They did **not** remove the audience requirement. The B2B cash engine (the same-day site/dossier/MONEY_PLAYS work that produces a dollar in *days*) stays the cash spine and funds this. Afterlight is built on **compounding, batched effort:** one AI render batch feeds weeks of drops. Set it up cheap and clean now, let it compound, do not bet the rent on it.

**Honest timeline:**
- **Week 1:** render Chapter 1 in full (Stages 0-2, 5, 8). Wordmark, world, mockups, teaser, landing block. Zero shoot day spent.
- **Weeks 2-3:** seed the cluster, run TEASE + HYPE + COLLECT, open the pre-order shadow-test.
- **Day of close (end of Week 3 / 7-day window):** read the threshold. Hit = batch-cut + schedule Proof Day. Miss = auto-refund, iterate, re-render.
- **Weeks 4-6:** Proof Day (real Canon shoot of founding wearers), fulfill, ship the chapter book, open Chapter 2 tease.
- **Months 2-6:** run reps. One chapter roughly every 4 to 6 weeks. The asset being built is the repeat roster, not any single drop.
- **Honest floor:** expect the first 1 to 3 chapters to build the list and the proof, not the profit. The money compounds at the roster level across chapters, at near-zero CAC. Anyone promising profit on Drop 1 with no audience is lying.

## 5. THE AI-NATIVE PRODUCTION PIPELINE

Connected-first per the 7-question standard (Layer 3). Two registers that never cross: **ATMOSPHERE** is fully AI-native; **IDENTITY/PROOF** is real-shoot-only on the Canon R6 Mark II (Layer 4).

**STAGE 0: BRAND SPINE (Figma).** Lock the wordmark, type system, palette, and garment-mockup layouts (tee/long-sleeve/outerwear/founding-card). Locked once; every downstream asset inherits it.

**STAGE 1: GRAPHICS & PRINT (Adobe).** Chapter graphics, wordmark vectorization, print-ready files, hangtags, the analog aftercard ritual touchpoint. Firefly gen → `image_vectorize` → Photoshop assembly.

**STAGE 2: WORLD-BUILD (Higgsfield).** One environment per chapter from the locked 7-rotation, never mixed. Render the hero world + supporting frames. `generate_image`.

**STAGE 3: IDENTITY ANCHOR (Higgsfield Soul, one-time, consenting real subject only).** Train a Soul Character on a *consenting real brand-face* so atmosphere content stays identity-faithful across the chapter. **HARD GATE: Soul is never trained on a fabricated person and never used to imply a real result. Real wearers stay in Stage 4.**

**STAGE 4: REAL PROOF SHOOT (Canon R6 Mark II, Bryce's reps).** The only source of real wearer portraits and on-body proof-of-presence frames. Real subject + real direction + real capture + real light, FIRST. Identity-integrity rule holds: face, features, skin texture (pores at 100%), body proportions never altered. AI owns the assembly *around* the subject, never the human inside it. Develop in v3 LUXURY EDITORIAL (Lightroom); Evoto for skin, texture preserved.

**STAGE 5: GARMENT MOCKUPS & STILL-LIFES (Higgsfield + Adobe).** The garment as hero object, on-body lifestyle frames using the consenting Soul face, founding-card renders. Reframe to every platform spec. `product-photoshoot` + Adobe `image_remove_background` / `generative_expand` / `fill_area`.

**STAGE 6: LOOKBOOK / CHAPTER BOOK (Adobe).** Assemble Stages 2-5 into the branded editorial chapter PDF, plus a linesheet for any wholesale or collab motion. `document_render_layout` → `document_convert_pdf`.

**STAGE 7: STORE / FUNNEL SHIP (Vercel → Shopify).** Deploy the drop landing page live; redeploy offer swaps in-session. At physical retail, Shopify + POD/3PL handles zero-inventory fulfillment, printing only the SKUs the rendered campaign already won.

**STAGE 8: DROP TEASE & ADS (Higgsfield).** Teaser films and ad worlds rendered from the chapter's environment. Virality Predictor scores hooks BEFORE spend; only top-scoring teasers go live. Marketing Studio builds hook/product/UGC-register ads using the consenting Soul face, never a fabricated person.

**STAGE 9: OUTBOUND & MEASURE (Instantly + Gmail + Airtable/Notion).** Instantly (hello@snipedmedia.com) runs wholesale/collab outreach (LA stylists, boutiques, scene nodes). Gmail drafts warm follow-ups. Airtable or Notion is the **drop ledger:** every rendered world logged with its response score, graduate-or-kill decision, and revenue attribution, so the next chapter renders smarter.

### Run-cadence (the office, not the tool list)
**Render Day** (Stages 0-2, 5, 8: full AI world + scored teaser) → **Test Window** (teaser + offer to owned list + small boost, Virality Predictor + real response logged) → **Graduate-or-Kill gate** → for winners, **Proof Day** (Stage 4 real Canon shoot) + **Ship** (Stages 6-7: chapter book + live landing + pre-order push) → **Measure** (Stage 9). One environment per drop. The engine runs reps; it does not reinvent the look per chapter.

## 7. STRUCTURE

**Recommendation (one line, with the reason): Afterlight is a standalone house brand with its own name and its own `.com`, legally owned by Baseplate, LLC, because the holding parent is already filed and a house brand bolts on with zero new entity, zero public-brand confusion, and full consumer-surface freedom.**

It is **NOT** BASEPLATE-the-public-brand, and it is **NOT** SNIPED-linked.

**Why owned-by Baseplate, LLC.** Baseplate, LLC is already the filed CA holding parent, designed so everything else is an expression underneath it. A house brand bolts on without a new entity filing, inheriting clean parent-level banking, IP assignment, and ownership, while keeping its own unconstrained name, domain, and aesthetic surface.

**Why NOT BASEPLATE-the-public-brand.** The BASEPLATE public wordmark is locked as the trusted infra/operator layer of the physical-AI buildout, with a hard anti-reduction guardrail. Pointing a clothing brand at the BASEPLATE public surface would confuse the procurement buyer and erode the trust that dossier sells. Zero crossover on the public layer; the only shared layer is the LLC parent on the back end.

**Why NOT SNIPED-linked.** SNIPED is the parked, photography-coded creative track: a different ICP and a different distribution motion. Linking the clothing brand to SNIPED inherits the photography reduction and muddies both. Instead, **SNIPED supplies the creative engine** (the v3 LUXURY direction, the 7-environment rotation, the Higgsfield/Seedream stack) to Afterlight, monetizing the parked SNIPED assets without re-triggering the reduction on either surface.

**Sequencing (the honest discipline).** Stand Afterlight up now as a parked-but-unblocked house brand: cheap, clean, optionality preserved. Build it on compounding, batched effort. Keep the B2B cash engine as the proof loop and the founder's scarce hours. Activate Afterlight as audience and brand equity accrue. It runs alongside the cash spine; it does not replace it. Give it its own `.com` from day one, never a subdomain or path under the infra domain.
