## 1. One-sentence statement
BASEPLATE is an operator practice that builds capability proof for the firms that build and staff critical infrastructure.

## 2. One-paragraph statement
BASEPLATE makes critical-facilities firms look as credible as they actually are, so they win the hire and win the bid. It serves owner-led firms in the AI-infrastructure supply chain (critical-facilities staffing firms first, specialty subcontractors second) who win on trust but look generic on paper. It exists because in a market where every strong tech and every serious bid is being fought over, the firm that looks the most credible wins, and that decision is currently being made on thin careers pages and identical capability statements. What it sells first is The Capability Dossier: a premium, security-safe proof asset, built insider-to-insider by an operator who has stood on the floor.

## 3. Buyer-facing statement (10-second understanding)
"I build the proof that makes your firm the obvious choice to a candidate or a client. Not marketing, not a photo shoot. A capability dossier, security-safe, priced against one hire or one bid."

## 4. BJ-facing statement (why this is not "just photography")
I am using a sharp, paid service to get inside the physical-AI-infrastructure world and earn the right to build something much bigger there. Photography is one instrument; the deliverable is a proof asset that moves a hire or a bid. The moat is not the camera, the AI, or the books. It is my operator layer: field and data-center experience, taste, relationships, and the proof I generate. The Dossier is the wedge that opens the rooms; the larger play (operator/proof/trust infrastructure) is earned through it, not announced.

## 5. Investor-level statement (wedge to thesis, no overclaim)
BASEPLATE enters the AI-infrastructure supply chain through a high-trust, premium proof service with real, urgent demand (a talent shortage and a bidding war). The service is the beachhead. As it repeats, it has a credible (unproven) path up the value chain: from one-off proof assets to recurring presence/retainers, to documentation and ops systems for the same firms, to a proprietary record of who can verifiably do the physical work. The infrastructure/data layer is an earned option, not the plan. The plan is: get paid, repeat, watch for the systems pull. Most service businesses never make that leap; this one has an unusually good bridge because the founder is a genuine insider to the physical-AI buildout.

## 6. The current claim (what we are allowed to say now)
- We build capability proof / presence assets for critical-facilities firms.
- Built insider-to-insider by an operator from the field/data-center world.
- Security-safe by design: nothing sensitive shown, you approve everything.
- Outcome-first: built to win a hire or a bid.
- Pilot from $2,500, scoped to one role or one bid; delivered in 1 to 2 weeks; you own it.

## 7. The parked claim (what we may NOT say yet)
- "Platform." "Registry." "Verified operator graph." "Network."
- "AI company." "Trust infrastructure" as a public positioning.
- "Billion-dollar company." Any scale or category claim.
- Any client name, testimonial, case study, or "trusted by." (No proof yet; fabricating it would destroy trust with a procurement buyer faster than anything.)
These are the empire kept private. Sell the dossier and the outcome.

## 8. The proof path (statement to reality)
1. **First paid Pilot** ($2,500-4,000) from a staffing firm or sub. Proves the offer is real, not just liked.
2. **First room opened** (a non-secure shoot granted, an intro, a bid-or-role-use commitment). Proves access, the real gate.
3. **Repeatable demand** (2-3 pilots, one referred). Proves it is not a fluke.
4. **Systems pull** (a buyer asks for more than the artifact: ops, documentation, recruiting systems). Proves the wedge-to-platform path.
5. **Data layer possibility** (a verifiable capability record accrues as a byproduct). The earned option. Only here does the bigger thesis get reconsidered.

## 9. Why BASEPLATE
The name was scored 9.5/10 in the historical brand work specifically because it is infrastructure/engineering-coded: it "names the operating layer underneath the work," reads like Stripe/Vercel/Substack, and a baseplate is literally the structural foundation a machine sits on. That fits the operator-infrastructure thesis better than it ever fit photography. The LLC is filed and the domain/handles are claimed, so it is the strongest *available* wrapper at zero cost. **But it is still only a 30-day test wrapper, not a permanent lock.** The buyer does not care about the name; permanence is decided by the proof, not by this statement. SNIPED/SNIPED Media is retired for this wrapper because "Media" reduces it to photography.

## 10. Why The Capability Dossier
It is the fastest paid path into the infrastructure world using only skills BJ already has. It comes before an app (months of build, no buyer), before content (no audience in this pool yet), before automation (nothing to automate at zero volume), and before a platform (unearned). It solves a concrete, urgent pain: firms that win on trust look generic on paper and lose hires and bids on perception, right when the buildout makes that most expensive. It also generates the first relationships, case studies, and data everything bigger depends on. Honest caveat: mechanically it is a productized proof/media service; "capability proof" is the positioning, and whether it lands at a premium is the single biggest thing the test proves.

## 11. Why this buyer
- **Staffing firms first:** an owner with discretion, real budget, acute urgency (talent shortage), reachable on LinkedIn/email, and a clear dollar-per-outcome (one placement = $20-45K, so the Pilot is a rounding error).
- **Specialty subcontractors second:** owner-controlled budget, bid-driven urgency (a differentiated capability statement can swing a six-figure award), but slower and more relationship-bound.
- Not founders (that is the C4 fallback where BJ's taste-edge may actually fit better; the test will tell), not schools/events (thin budget, slow), not consumers (wrong economics).

## 12. What would make the statement wrong (kill criteria)
- Infra owners won't pay a premium for "proof," only praise it (most likely kill).
- It reads as content/photography to the buyer despite the framing.
- Security/NDA reality blocks getting any usable non-secure material.
- BJ's edge converts founders (C4), not procurement infra buyers, so the buyer in the statement is wrong.
- The employer NDA/non-compete forbids selling into the adjacent supply chain (unresolved; must be checked before the first paid client).
- It never converts past one-off media gigs (then it's a lifestyle agency, and the "infrastructure" framing was aspirational).
If 2+ of these hold after ~15 real conversations, the statement gets revised (likely toward founders/C4 or a different value framing).
