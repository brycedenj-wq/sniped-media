## Judge panel (3 lenses)

- Winner pick: **LAUNCHDROP (house name: THE DROP ROOM)** , LAUNCHDROP wins on the only axis that matters most under a Hormozi lens: it attaches to the single most price-insensitive moment a funded company ever has. The launch date is externally fixed and unrepeatable (embargo lifts, demo day happens, round closes), so the 72-hour clock is genuine urgency rather than a manufactured constraint, and the cost of looking amateur on the one day everyone is watching dwarfs a $6.5k-$20k fee. That is a textbook grand-slam: high pain, hard deadline, low price sensitivity, premium framed as insurance. It also ships actual money plumbing (live landing page + booking/waitlist + n8n go-live trigger), so the buyer sees an outcome (a launch that lands) not a capability (AI video). The Seal gives it an ownable, trademarkable, reusable spine across film, deck, and site that the other two match only on aesthetic, not on transactional function. Faceless and fully synthetic by construction keeps the operator's identity protected and the model scalable. ONLYWN is more distinctive strategically but has no forcing function to pay now and rests its entire premium on one fragile strategic sentence produced in 72h. FIXED HOUSE sells cleanly against a hated incumbent but its 'fixed price productized agency sprint' positioning is crowded, copyable, and self-caps the premium. LAUNCHDROP is the most sellable, most premium, and most defensible.
  - graft: From ONLYWN, graft the Category Claim as a paid Hour-0 deliverable inside LAUNCHDROP's Launch Bible: do not just write positioning, deliver a one-sentence strategic verdict that renames the market the founder is launching into. ## The 3 candidates (full)

### LAUNCHDROP (house name: THE DROP ROOM) , KILLED
- **Tagline:** Your launch deserves a premiere, not a post.
- **Premise:** A 72-hour AI-native campaign house that turns a funded founder's product or raise into a fully produced launch event: identity, film, deck, and live landing page, shipped as one synchronized drop.
- **Angle:** Reframe the launch from "ship an announcement" to "stage a premiere." Founders already spend on the product and the round but launch week with a Notion doc, a Loom, and a tweet. The wedge is treating launch day as a timed cinematic event with a countdown, an embargo, and a single signature artifact (the Seal) that makes the moment feel inevitable and pre-validated. Velocity is the proof: 72 hours signals the founder is moving at funded-startup speed, and the polish signals the round was deserved.
- **Buyer:** Seed-to-Series-A founders (raised $500k to $15M in the last 90 days) and solo technical founders launching a flagship product or announcing a round. They pay now because launch timing is fixed and unrepeatable: the embargo lifts, the demo day happens, the round closes, and a weak launch wastes the single largest attention spike the company will get this year. Low price sensitivity in the 48 hours before a launch; they will pay a premium to not look amateur the one day everyone is watching. They have capital, no in-house brand team, and a hard deadline.
- **Productizes:** A fixed 72-hour deliverable set, time-boxed and dated. Hour 0-12: Launch Bible (positioning, narrative spine, one-line, the Seal designed). Hour 12-36: visual identity system (logotype, type, palette, the signature world rendered) plus 12 to 18 stills and key art. Hour 36-54: a 60 to 90 second launch film (synthetic world, faceless, scored via ElevenLabs, cut in Premiere/AE) plus a 6 to 10 frame vertical teaser cutdown. Hour 54-66: investor-grade pitch/launch deck (12 to 16 slides) and a live single-page launch site with countdown, embargo gate, and a booking/waitlist CTA wired in. Hour 66-72: the Drop Kit: scheduled launch-day sequence, embargo email to press/investors, social drop pack, and an n8n-automated countdown-to-go-live trigger. Handoff includes the Seal as a reusable brand mark and all source files.
- **Price:** Premiere $6,500 (film + identity + deck + site, single product/raise). Headline Act $12,000 (adds full social drop pack, press embargo ops, two cutdowns, founder narrative coaching). Opening Night $20,000+ (rush under 72h, multi-channel launch-day live ops, exclusivity in their category for 90 days).
- **Visual world:** "Embargo Modernism": the aesthetic of a sealed press kit the night before a major reveal. Deep ink navy and near-black grounds, a single warm signal color (molten brass/amber) used only for live/active states and the countdown, cold white type. Motifs: wax-seal geometry, countdown timecode, redaction bars that resolve into clarity, vault doors and slow reveals, embargo stamps, ticket-stub framing. References: Apple keynote teaser black, A24 title-card restraint, Aesop product gravity, financial-terminal monospace meeting fashion-house serif. Texture of paper and metal under glass. Faceless by design: people read as silhouette, hands, crowd-at-a-premiere energy, never a recognizable face. Calm, expensive, withheld until the moment it isn't.
- **Hero artifact:** The Seal: a circular wax-and-brass embargo seal, custom-struck per client, that locks the launch until go-live and then "breaks" on the drop. It appears on the landing page countdown, stamps the film's title card, closes the deck, and ships as the client's reusable launch mark. One ownable object that means "this launch is real, dated, and authorized."
- **Why better than AXIS-style demo:** A generic character demo proves the tool can make a pretty avatar. This proves the operator can run a funded company's single most important day end to end, on a deadline, with revenue ops attached. It sells an outcome (a launch that lands) not a capability (AI video). It is faceless and synthetic by construction, so it scales without the operator's identity. The Seal gives it an ownable trademarkable spine a character demo never has. And it ships a working landing page and booking flow, so the buyer sees money plumbing, not just a reel.
- **Why not old SNIPED:** This is the ACTIVE Prime Mover campaign-house lane, not the OLD SNIPED fallback. It does not photograph a real person, does not deliver a Pixieset portrait gallery, and does not touch the $1,500 Reset floor. The buyer is a funded founder buying a launch system, not a local subject buying portraits. People-imagery is fully synthetic/world-built and faceless, so the operator's real identity is never exposed. It productizes the one-person AI production stack (Higgsfield, Premiere, AE, Blender, Adobe, Figma, ElevenLabs, n8n) into a premium dated deliverable, which is exactly the campaign-house thesis, not the photography proving ground.
- **Sellability:** A funded founder with a fixed launch date and no brand team says yes because the cost of looking amateur on launch day dwarfs $6.5k to $20k, the 72-hour clock matches their urgency, and the deliverable is a complete event they can point to (film + deck + live site + drop ops) rather than assets they still have to assemble. Premium reads as insurance on an unrepeatable attention spike. The Seal and the countdown make the offer concrete and time-bound, which closes deals fast.
- **Risks:** 1) Scope blowout: 72h is tight; founder indecision on positioning can eat the clock. Mitigate with a locked Hour-0 intake and a no-revision-during-window rule. 2) Trust gap on synthetic/faceless film for an investor audience that may expect "real." Mitigate by leaning into premium abstract brand-film convention (A24/Apple teaser), not fake testimonials. 3) Deliverability risk on the live site + n8n automation under deadline; keep a pre-built launch-site template so build is configuration not from-scratch. 4) Category exclusivity promise (Opening Night) is hard to honor at volume; cap it. 5) Buyer timing is spiky and non-recurring, so pipeline must be continuously fed; no retainer by default. 6) Name/.com and Seal trademark availability still need verification per the name-availability gate.

### ONLYWN (pronounced "only one") , LOCKED WINNER
- **Tagline:** We do not make you better. We make you the only one.
- **Premise:** A 72-hour repositioning house that takes an established-but-invisible small business and rebuilds it as a category of one: new mythology, cinematic brand world, and a manifesto film, shipped as a complete launch system in three days.
- **Angle:** The wedge is category design, not branding. Most repositioning sells a logo refresh or a "fresh look." ONLYWN sells a category escape: it does not enter the buyer's market more attractively, it renames the market so the buyer is the only legitimate occupant. The deliverable is built around one strategic act, the Category Claim, a sentence that reframes what business the client is actually in. Everything else (world, film, deck, site) exists to make that claim feel inevitable and expensive. This is the Win Without Pitching / 7 Powers / Trading Up corner of the corpus operationalized: the strongest moat is being the only one, and the second-strongest is being perceived as the only one before the market notices. Faceless by construction, the client is repositioned through a built world and a manifesto, never through a founder's face or a personal-brand performance.
- **Buyer:** An established small business doing $500K to $5M that is competent, profitable, and indistinguishable: a law firm, a medspa, a boutique build/contractor, a regional financial advisor, a specialty agency. They look like every competitor on the same template, compete on price, and are quietly terrified of commoditization (now sharpened by AI flattening their category). They pay now because a single bad quarter of "we picked the cheaper option" makes the repositioning fee look trivial. Low price sensitivity at the top of the ladder: this buyer has revenue but no distinct story, and the pain is status, not cash. The economic buyer is the owner-operator who personally feels invisible. Why now: AI just made "good enough" infinite and free, so the only defensible position is being uncopyable, a category of one.
- **Productizes:** A fixed 72-hour sprint producing a Repositioning System, delivered as one sealed package: (1) The Category Claim, a one-sentence renaming of the business plus a one-page Positioning Doctrine (who they are the only one for, the enemy they reject, the new category name). (2) A cinematic Brand World bible: 12 to 16 world-built stills in a single signature visual world, color system, type system, and motif kit, all synthetic/world-built imagery, zero exposed real faces. (3) A 60 to 90 second Manifesto Film: scripted, AI-voiced (ElevenLabs), scored, graded in Premiere/After Effects, built from Higgsfield world plates and Blender hero-object renders. (4) A Repositioning Deck (the "Category Brief") that an owner can hand to a board, a banker, or a buyer. (5) A single-page launch Landing built in Figma and shipped live (Vercel), carrying the claim, the film, and one call to action. (6) An Offer and booking layer: the rewritten premium offer copy plus an n8n booking/intake flow wired to the landing. Everything is one world, one claim, one symbol, shipped in 72 hours.
- **Price:** Standard repositioning sprint $7,500 (claim + world bible + manifesto film + deck + landing). Signature $14,000 (adds extended film cut, full offer rewrite, n8n booking automation, 90-day world-asset pack for ongoing content). Sovereign $25,000+ (named category audit of the whole market, exclusivity-in-category guarantee so ONLYWN will not reposition a direct competitor, quarterly world refresh retainer).
- **Visual world:** A single ownable world called The Vault Room: a vast, dim, monolithic interior somewhere between a private bank vault, a brutalist cathedral, and a museum after hours. Palette is ink black, bone white, and a single struck metal: ONLYWN brass (warm, aged, restrained), with cold concrete grey as the connective neutral. No teal/orange, no SaaS gradients, no "modern minimal" startup white. Motifs: a shaft of hard top-light hitting one object on a plinth, long shadows, polished stone floors, embossed not printed surfaces, vault-door circular geometry, archive drawers, redaction bars used as a graphic device. Type is a high-contrast didone display (think engraved nameplate) paired with a quiet grotesque for body. References: Roversi and Meisel restraint, the lobby of a Swiss private bank, Aman hotel materiality, the title sequence of a heist film, Calvin Klein 90s campaign austerity, museum vitrine lighting. Everything looks acquired, archived, and singular: this client is the one object under the light.
- **Hero artifact:** The Singular Seal: a struck brass medallion engraved with the numeral 1 rendered as a single unbroken vertical stroke, set inside a circular vault-door ring. It is the "category of one" made physical, the only one, sealed. It appears embossed on the deck cover, struck into the final frame of the manifesto film as the sign-off, watermarked into every world still, and (in the Sovereign tier) shipped as an actual machined brass object the owner keeps on their desk. One symbol, one numeral, one client.
- **Why better than AXIS-style demo:** A generic character-engine demo proves the operator can make a pretty AI person. It sells nothing because it answers no business question and has no buyer. ONLYWN inverts that: the AI stack is invisible plumbing, and the product is a strategic outcome (becoming uncopyable) that an owner already lies awake about. It is faceless-safe by design, which removes the single biggest objection to AI-built brand work. It ships a decision (the Category Claim) and a board-ready artifact, not a vibe. And it is repeatable: same world system, same hero seal, same 72-hour rails, different claim per client, so it compounds into a recognizable house style instead of a one-off flex.
- **Why not old SNIPED:** This is not Reset and not photography. There is no shoot, no real subject, no $1,500 portrait floor, no client face on a deliverable. The unit sold is a repositioning system priced 5x to 16x the old floor, and the entire imagery layer is world-built and synthetic, which is exactly why it stays inside the active campaign-house lane and never touches the identity-exposed photography fallback. The operator's face and the client's face are both absent by construction.
- **Sellability:** A buyer says yes because ONLYWN reframes the purchase from "marketing expense" to "category insurance." The owner is not buying assets, they are buying the end of competing on price. The Category Claim is delivered as a strategic verdict, the kind of sentence they will repeat in every sales conversation for years, and the manifesto film plus Vault Room world make that claim feel already true and already expensive, so adopting it feels like claiming territory they were always owed rather than gambling on a rebrand. The 72-hour clock makes it a decision, not a project, removing the long-engagement dread. The exclusivity-in-category guarantee at the top tier converts the fee into a competitive weapon (your rival cannot buy this from us next). Premium price is the proof: a category-of-one cannot credibly be cheap.
- **Risks:** 1) Strategy quality is the load-bearing element: a weak or generic Category Claim collapses the whole premium, so the claim must clear a hard positioning gate (7 Powers / WWP) before any world gets built. 2) 72 hours is a real production constraint across stills, film, deck, and live site for one operator; the sprint must be pre-rigged with locked world templates and the hero seal so only the claim and copy vary. 3) Faceless world-imagery can read generic if the Vault Room is not held strictly, the single signature world is the moat, so visual drift is the main failure mode. 4) Buyer must trust an AI-built world for a serious repositioning, so proof artifacts (a self-applied ONLYWN reposition, 2 to 3 spec case worlds) are required before outbound. 5) Repositioning that overshoots the client's actual delivery capacity creates a credibility gap, so the claim must be true-able, not just dramatic. 6) Category education cost: "category design" is unfamiliar to small-business owners, so the manifesto/landing must teach the value in the first 30 seconds or the price reads as arbitrary.

### FIXED HOUSE , KILLED
- **Tagline:** The agency brand sprint, fixed. One price. Three days. No revisions roulette.
- **Premise:** FIXED HOUSE takes a founder's idea or unlaunched brand and ships a complete premium launch system (world, identity, stills, motion, deck, landing page, offer architecture, booking ops) in 72 hours for one fixed price, run by one operator on an AI-native production stack instead of a 6-week, $50k agency engagement.
- **Angle:** The wedge is not "AI is cheaper." The wedge is the elimination of agency dread: open-ended timelines, scope creep, revision loops, account-manager telephone, and the $50k invoice for a brand you still have to launch yourself. FIXED HOUSE productizes the entire sprint into a closed system with a published, fixed scope and a literal three-day clock. The whole brand reads like an operating system being installed, not a creative service being negotiated. The buyer is purchasing certainty and finishedness, not creativity-as-mystery.
- **Buyer:** Funded or self-funded founders (pre-seed to Series A), solo SaaS/DTC/services operators, and agency refugees who have already been burned once by a slow expensive brand engagement. They are launch-blocked: idea is ready, the brand is not, and a real agency quotes 6 weeks and $40-60k. Why now: they have a launch date, a demo day, a raise, or a product drop with a hard deadline, and an agency timeline will miss it. Price sensitivity is moderate-to-low at the top because they are comparing against a $50k agency number and their own opportunity cost of not launching, not against a Fiverr gig. They pay a premium specifically to remove uncertainty.
- **Productizes:** A fixed 72-hour deliverable set shipped as one installable "brand OS": (1) Brand World Bible: positioning, naming check, voice, signature visual world spec, palette, type system. (2) Identity Kit: logo/wordmark, the signature hero mark, usage rules, exported assets via Figma. (3) Campaign Stills: 12-18 finished synthetic, faceless-safe hero images built in Higgsfield/Adobe/Blender (world-built people, never the operator's face). (4) Motion: one 15-30s anthem film plus 3 cutdowns (Premiere/After Effects, ElevenLabs score/VO). (5) Launch Deck: investor/sales-ready PDF. (6) Landing Page: deployed live (Vercel) with copy and booking embed. (7) Offer Architecture: a 3-tier price ladder and the core sales script. (8) Booking + Intake Ops: an n8n automation that routes inbound, books calls, and fires the welcome sequence. Delivered as a single timestamped handoff with a published change-log, so the buyer sees the whole system at once, not a drip of deliverables.
- **Price:** Tier 1 SPRINT (single-product launch system, 72 hours): $7,500. Tier 2 HOUSE (full brand OS plus motion anthem and live landing page, the flagship): $14,500. Tier 3 INSTALL (HOUSE plus 30-day post-launch content engine, n8n automation buildout, and one re-cut): $24,000. Every tier is fixed-price and fixed-scope, published openly. The frame against the room: one third the price and one fifteenth the time of a $50k six-week agency sprint.
- **Visual world:** The aesthetic is "control-room minimalism meets architectural blueprint." Not generic modern minimal: think a precision instrument panel rendered in matte ink. Palette is bone white and graphite black with a single signal color, a cold reactor cyan (#00E0D2-ish) used only for live-status and the clock. Motifs: monospace data type (think technical drawing labels), thin hairline rules and registration marks, a persistent 72:00:00 countdown timer rendered like mission-control telemetry, and isometric "exploded view" diagrams that show the brand as components being assembled. Everything is gridded, measured, dimensioned, and stamped. References: Swiss technical manuals, Teenage Engineering product design, Braun/Rams instrument restraint, NASA flight-deck typography, architectural blueprint linework. The feeling on contact is "this is a machine that finishes things," the opposite of an agency moodboard.
- **Hero artifact:** THE INSTALL STAMP: a single circular registration-style seal, a thin double-ring with a precision crosshair/target at center and the text "FIXED HOUSE · INSTALL COMPLETE · 72:00:00" arced around it, struck in graphite with one cyan tick. It functions as the certificate of completion: it gets stamped onto the final handoff cover, the deck's last page, and the deployed landing page footer, marking the brand as shipped. It is ownable, reproducible, and becomes the buyer's proof-of-launch badge.
- **Why better than AXIS-style demo:** A generic character demo proves the operator can make a pretty avatar. FIXED HOUSE proves the operator can take a real business from idea to launched-and-bookable in three days for a fixed fee. It sells an outcome (a launched brand) against a named, hated incumbent (the slow expensive agency), not a capability. The productized fixed scope and the 72-hour clock are the product, which makes it buyable, comparable, and referable in a way a character reel never is. It also showcases the full stack working as one pipeline, not a single toy.
- **Why not old SNIPED:** This is not the Reset $1,500 portrait offer and never touches it. There is no photographer, no real subject, no client face, no studio session. People-imagery is entirely synthetic and world-built, so the operator's identity is never exposed and there is no shoot to staff. It lives in the ACTIVE Prime Mover campaign-house lane: world-build plus identity system plus AI campaign package sold as a productized business system, faceless-safe, drafts-and-systems only. Old SNIPED is explicitly the fallback lane and is not invoked here.
- **Sellability:** A burned founder facing a $50k, six-week agency quote and a hard launch date says yes because FIXED HOUSE removes every reason agencies feel like a gamble: the price is published and fixed, the scope is published and closed, the timeline is a literal three-day clock, and the deliverable is a complete launched system they can use on day four, not a brand book they still have to execute. The comparison is brutal in FIXED HOUSE's favor, and the INSTALL STAMP gives them a finished, ownable proof-of-launch they can show their team and investors. They are buying certainty, speed, and finishedness, which is exactly what the incumbent cannot promise.
- **Risks:** (1) Trust gap: a fixed 72-hour promise can read as too-good, so early sales need 2-3 real before/after case installs and a public scope doc to be believed. (2) Scope discipline: the model dies if the operator lets fixed scope flex into agency-style revision loops; a hard published change-policy and one-recut-only rule must be enforced. (3) Synthetic-imagery fit: some buyers in regulated or human-trust categories will want real faces, which is a clean disqualify, not a fight. (4) Capacity: 72-hour sprints are intense for one operator, so the calendar must gate concurrent installs (one HOUSE at a time) to protect quality and the clock promise. (5) Quality-bar risk: every deliverable must clear the OS max-readiness gate, because one weak landing page or muddy anthem film breaks the "machine that finishes things" promise the whole brand rests on.
