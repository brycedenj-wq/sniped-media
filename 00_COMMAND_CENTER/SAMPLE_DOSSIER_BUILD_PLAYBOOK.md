## 1. Purpose and scope
Define what the Capability Dossier is, what the sample is for, how both are built, what is security-safe, what is never fabricated, and how the asset connects to discovery, proposal, delivery, and proof logging. The sample makes the offer real internally before any outreach. BJ builds it; admin organizes assets only.

## 2. What The Capability Dossier is
A premium, security-safe proof asset that makes a critical-facilities firm look as credible as it actually is, built insider-to-insider by an operator who has stood on the floor. Not marketing, not headshots, not a gallery. It captures who the firm and crew are, the caliber of their work, and why a top operator or a client should choose them, in language a critical-facilities professional respects, tied to a hire or a bid. Media is the instrument; the dossier is the deliverable.

## 3. What the sample dossier is for
- To make the offer concrete on a discovery call (the thing BJ shows so the buyer "knows in five minutes").
- To prove to BJ, internally, that the offer is real and buildable before outreach.
- To set the visual and structural standard every client dossier follows.
- It is a clearly-labeled SAMPLE, a mock built around a realistic but invented or anonymized firm, never presented as real client work.

## 4. What the sample dossier is NOT
Not a real client case study. Not proof that a real firm hired BASEPLATE. Not a photography portfolio. Not a brand/marketing deck. Not a public asset. Not evidence (it is an illustration of the format). Never labeled or implied as a real engagement.

## 5. Buyer use cases (the dossier is built to move one of these)
- **Staffing firm hiring a role:** makes the firm the obvious yes to a top critical-facilities tech weighing multiple offers.
- **Specialty subcontractor on a bid:** makes the firm the credible bidder a client believes, beyond an identical-looking PDF.
- **Owner/founder credibility:** establishes the firm and its operator as serious and capable to candidates, clients, and peers (the status/hidden-motive layer).
- **AI-Ops / system-pull signal:** if a buyer, seeing the dossier, asks for recruiting/bid/documentation/reporting systems, that is a later-layer signal; log it, do not build it here.

## 6. Core dossier structure
1. **Cover / positioning:** firm name + a one-line capability positioning ("Critical-facilities commissioning, mission-critical standards"). Restrained, premium.
2. **Firm capability story:** a tight, credible narrative of who they are, what they deliver, the standard they hold, in operator language.
3. **Proof blocks:** the concrete capability points (scope handled, standards met, the work itself), shown generically and security-safe.
4. **Team/operator credibility:** the people and the operator behind the work (with release), the caliber of the crew.
5. **Role or bid context:** the specific outcome this dossier serves (the named role or bid), tying the asset to a decision.
6. **Security-safe visual proof:** approved non-secure imagery of people and work in controlled settings.
7. **Candidate/client decision path:** why this firm is the obvious choice, framed for the person making the hire or the award.
8. **Call-to-action / next step:** how a candidate applies or a client engages (the firm's own path, not BASEPLATE's).

## 7. Minimum sample dossier
The smallest version that makes the offer real on a call: cover/positioning + a short capability story + 2-3 proof blocks + a small approved/representative image set + one role-or-bid context page + the one-screen capability profile. Clearly labeled SAMPLE, built around an anonymized/invented firm. Enough to show "this is the thing," not a full production.

## 8. Full client dossier
The paid Pilot deliverable (per WEDGE_OFFER_SPEC §4): the full proof narrative, approved non-secure imagery of the firm's people and work, a reusable ownable image set, hiring/bid copy blocks, a one-screen capability profile, and private branded delivery, all tied to the client's named role/bid, all client-approved. Standard/Premium tiers add fuller coverage, a combined recruiting+bid copy set, and a one-page capability/careers landing asset.

## 9. Inputs required (for a client dossier)
Discovery notes (the call); the named role or bid; firm details (scope, standards, history); approved non-sensitive images (or a planned non-secure shoot); team/operator details + written releases; real proof points (capabilities, standards, generically stated); approval constraints (what can/can't be shown). No input proceeds without firm sign-off on scope.

## 10. Security-safe rules (WEDGE_OFFER_SPEC §5, non-negotiable)
- **Can show:** people (with release), tools/PPE/vehicles, exterior/yard/shop, training rooms, mockups, office, controlled/neutral settings, generic non-identifying work the firm approves.
- **Cannot show:** secure data-center floors, cage/rack/equipment layouts, security systems, badges/access, client logos/names under NDA, capacities, anything customer-confidential, PII without consent.
- **Anonymize:** client identities, locations, project specifics. Capability is shown generically ("we commission mission-critical cooling"), never by naming a protected site.
- **Approval workflow gates everything:** nothing exists or publishes without the firm's written approval of every asset and line.

## 11. What must be real
The firm's actual capabilities, crew, standards, and work. The operator's real experience. The images (real people, real work, in non-secure settings). The proof points (true, even if stated generically). The narrative (accurate, not embellished beyond what the firm can stand behind). If it is in a client dossier, it happened.

## 12. What must never be fabricated
No fake crews, no fake facilities, no fake client proof, no fake testimonials, no fake case studies, no invented outcomes, no borrowed/stock imagery passed as the firm's, no claimed clients that do not exist, no metrics that were not measured. Fabrication destroys a procurement buyer's trust faster than anything and breaks the entire thesis. The sample is the only invented artifact, and it is labeled SAMPLE.

## 13. AI / tool use rules (the v2 disciplined-AI rule)
- **Allowed:** structure and layout support, copy drafting and tightening, internal concepting and storyboarding, design assistance, brand atmosphere. AI helps BUILD the asset.
- **Never allowed:** AI-generated fake crews, fake facilities, fake client proof, fake testimonials, fake case studies, or any AI image presented as real evidence of a client's people/work/outcomes.
- **The line:** AI may assist the craft and the words; AI may never be the evidence. The proof in a client dossier is always real, photographed, client-approved. Higgsfield/Kling are for internal concept/atmosphere only (see the concept-film doc), never for client proof.

## 14. Visual direction
Premium technical-manual / data-center-schematic register, operator-coded, restrained. The BASEPLATE brand-bible palette: onyx `#0F0F0F`, white `#FFFFFF`, concrete grey `#8C8C8C`, blueprint-blue accent `#0055FF`; system/industrial type; architectural, structural, quiet. No stock-photo feel, no teal-and-orange, no creator/photographer/"studio" vibe, no marketing gloss. It should read like a serious capability document an engineer respects, not a brand brochure.

## 15. Figma / design workflow
Build the dossier layout in Figma using the brand-bible palette and type. Create reusable components (cover, capability-story block, proof block, team block, role/bid context page, one-screen profile) so each client dossier is a fast, consistent assembly, not a from-scratch design. The sample establishes the master components. Export to a private, branded delivery format (PDF + the Pixieset gallery). Do NOT build the design file as part of writing this playbook; that is the execution step BJ runs.

## 16. Copy workflow
Draft in BJ's operator register (plain, credible, non-marketing), pulling structure from WEDGE_OFFER_SPEC and tone from the persuasion canon (low self-orientation, outcome-first, status-aware). AI may draft and tighten; BJ edits for truth and voice. Every line is firm-approved before delivery. No fabricated claims, no fluff, no "passionate/creative/professional" filler.

## 17. Review / approval workflow
Brief to BJ proposes shot/copy plan to firm approves scope (confirms nothing sensitive) to produce to firm reviews and approves every asset and line in writing to deliver. Nothing publishes without written firm approval. For the SAMPLE, the "firm" is anonymized/invented, so the gate is BJ's own truth check (no real-client implication, security-safe format).

## 18. Delivery workflow
Private, branded delivery via a password-protected Pixieset gallery + a PDF the client owns. Client keeps and reuses everything (careers page, proposals, outreach). No public posting by BASEPLATE without written client approval. (Full delivery mechanics will live in the Delivery + Approval Playbook, the next build-order artifact after the minimum-viable set.)

## 19. Sample dossier build steps
1. Pick an anonymized/invented but realistic firm + a representative role or bid.
2. Draft the capability story + 2-3 proof blocks in operator voice.
3. Source security-safe representative imagery (own/permissioned, or clearly-marked placeholder), never real-client or stock-as-real.
4. Build the master Figma components in the brand-bible register.
5. Assemble the minimum sample (section 7), label it SAMPLE throughout.
6. BJ truth-check: nothing implies a real client; security-safe; on-brand; reads operator-grade.
7. Host privately for the discovery call; keep it ready to show on screen.

## 20. Client dossier build steps
1. Pull discovery notes + the named role/bid + the section 9 inputs.
2. Firm approves scope (what is shown, what is off-limits).
3. Produce: capture/assemble approved non-secure people and work.
4. Draft copy blocks tied to the outcome.
5. Assemble in the master Figma components.
6. Firm reviews and approves every asset and line in writing.
7. Deliver privately; client owns it. Log the engagement in PROOF_LOOPS.

## 21. Quality checklist (per dossier)
- [ ] Tied to a named role or bid (not generic).
- [ ] Operator-grade copy, no marketing fluff.
- [ ] Security-safe (nothing in the section 10 "cannot show" list).
- [ ] Everything real (client dossier) or clearly SAMPLE (sample).
- [ ] No fabricated crews/facilities/clients/testimonials/metrics.
- [ ] On the brand-bible palette and register; no stock/creator vibe.
- [ ] Firm-approved every asset and line (client dossier).
- [ ] Client owns the final asset.
- [ ] No platform/registry claim, no guaranteed-outcome language.

## 22. How this supports the proposal
The sample is what makes the proposal believable: it shows the buyer exactly what the Pilot produces before they pay. The proposal's scope block (PROPOSAL_AND_INVOICE_PLAYBOOK §6-8) maps directly to the dossier structure here, so what is promised and what is delivered are the same thing.

## 23. How this supports outreach / discovery
The free "candidate-view teardown" (the outbound/discovery micro-offer) is a 60-second taste that points at the full dossier. On the discovery call, the sample is the thing BJ shows during the "fit judgment" step so the buyer understands the format and the standard. The dossier is the proof behind the persuasion.

## 24. How this supports proof logging
A delivered client dossier is the artifact behind a paid Pilot (a T1/T2 proof entry). The proof log records: which role/bid it served, paid y/n, access granted y/n, and whether it moved the outcome (a hire/bid result, when known). The dossier is how "proof" becomes concrete in PROOF_LOOPS, not a claim but a delivered, client-approved asset.

## 25. When to revise the offer based on dossier feedback
If buyers consistently say the dossier is not what moves their decision, or they want something materially different (e.g., only the copy, only a landing page, or a fuller systems engagement), log the pattern and revisit WEDGE_OFFER_SPEC, do not quietly drift the deliverable. Revision is a deliberate, logged decision after real reps, per the proof-loop keep/kill/iterate rules. The sample format holds until evidence says otherwise.

## 26. What not to do
No fabricated anything. No stock or AI imagery passed as a client's real people/work. No real-client implication on the sample. No facility-sensitive content. No employer data or targeting employer clients/vendors/employees. No public use without written approval. No guaranteed-hire/bid language. No platform/registry claim. No reducing it to "photos." No building the design file or generating images as part of writing this playbook.

## 27. Guardrails
No fake proof, testimonials, case studies, crews, facilities, or metrics. No fabricated/facility-sensitive imagery. No AI-generated evidence (AI assists the craft, never the proof). No employer/confidential information; no targeting employer customers/vendors/employees via inside knowledge. No platform/registry/network public claim. No photography-only reduction (the dossier is the deliverable; media is the instrument). No unapproved public use. No over-promising hires/bids (you build proof, you do not guarantee outcomes). No automation before proof. Higgsfield/Kling internal-concept only, never client evidence. Old SNIPED/BASEPLATE material is historical evidence, not law. Bible held.
