## H1 · Succession commissioning for trades businesses
**Bet:** retiring owner-operators will pay a fixed fee to have their undocumented business turned into a transferable, sellable asset.
1. **Market category names:** business succession planning; exit readiness / exit planning; sell-side preparation; business process documentation; owner transition services; sellability assessment; CIM (confidential information memorandum) preparation; SOP documentation services.
2. **Buyer types:** retiring owner-operators (trades/services, $500k-$5M revenue); buyers/searchers (ETA, search funds) on the diligence side; business brokers as channel; SBA lenders as forcing function.
3. **Existing providers:** exit-planning consultants (CEPA designation holders), broker in-house prep, Value Builder System licensees, SOP-documentation freelancers, M&A advisors downmarket.
4. **Pricing signals:** sell-side prep / exit-readiness engagement pricing pages; CEPA consultant rates; Value Builder licensing fees; broker commission structures (8-12%) and whether prep is bundled; SOP-documentation freelance rates.
5. **Public pain evidence:** BizBuySell/BizQuest listing-to-sale conversion and time-on-market for owner-dependent businesses; Searchfunder threads on diligence failure; r/sweatystartup + r/smallbusiness + r/EntrepreneurRideAlong threads on "owner is the business," failed sales, retirement with no buyer; broker blog complaints about unprepared sellers.
6. **Existing buying behavior to confirm:** do owners actually PAY for prep pre-sale (vs brokers eating it, vs nobody doing it)? Are search funds paying third parties for operational diligence?
7. **Already bundled/free:** broker CIM prep; SBA lender checklists; YouTube/SCORE free exit guides. Map exactly what they cover and what they skip (live process verification is the suspected gap).
8. **Underserved gap hypothesis:** verified, transferable OPERATIONS documentation (not financials: brokers cover financials): the commissioning layer that proves the business runs without the owner.
9. **Handoff feasibility:** who maintains documentation after the engagement: the buyer? a successor manager? Is one-time truly one-time?
10. **One-time shape:** does the market accept fixed-scope, fixed-fee documentation engagements? Evidence either way.
11. **Babysitting risk:** does prep work drag into brokerage-adjacent ongoing advisory? How do existing providers exit engagements?
12. **Right-to-win questions:** does trades cultural fluency + AI-leveraged documentation speed actually beat CEPA incumbents on cost/quality? Is there evidence incumbents are expensive BECAUSE documentation is slow by hand?
13. **PROVES IT:** named providers selling sell-side ops-prep at real prices + public owner/broker complaints that it's scarce or unaffordable + evidence SBA/buyers demand it.
14. **KILLS IT:** owners systematically refuse pre-sale spend; broker prep already covers operations adequately; engagements structurally become advisory retainers.

## H2 · AI workflow commissioning with a verification certificate
**Bet:** small professional firms will pay for an AI workflow build that ends with a signed acceptance-test report, because the certificate (not the agent) de-risks them.
1. **Market category names:** AI implementation / AI integration consulting; AI readiness assessment; AI governance; AI audit; LLM evals / acceptance testing; AI risk & compliance for professional services; AI workflow audit.
2. **Buyer types:** managing partners at small law/accounting/advisory firms; medical-adjacent admin (fence-check first); insurance-independent professional firms; ops leads at 5-50 person firms.
3. **Existing providers:** AI implementation boutiques; fractional AI officers; Big-4 AI governance (enterprise-only?); eval-tooling vendors (Braintrust/LangSmith class: tooling, not service); bar/CPA association vendors.
4. **Pricing signals:** boutique implementation engagement pricing; fractional AI-ops rates and job postings; AI-readiness assessment fees; association CE/webinar vendor pricing.
5. **Public pain evidence:** "failed AI pilot" discourse density (search: AI pilot failure rate, MIT/RAND pilot-failure studies and who cites them); law/accounting forum threads on hallucination liability; state-bar AI guidance documents; malpractice-insurer AI bulletins.
6. **Existing buying behavior:** are firms currently paying ANYONE post-pilot to fix/verify? Or freezing adoption instead (which is also a buy signal for certification)?
7. **Already bundled/free:** vendor-built eval suites; platform compliance attestations; free bar-association guidelines. Does any of it produce a firm-specific acceptance test? (Suspected: no.)
8. **Underserved gap hypothesis:** NOBODY downmarket sells independent acceptance testing with a signed verification artifact; implementation shops grade their own homework (self-preferential bias as a market gap).
9. **Handoff feasibility:** who re-runs the acceptance tests after model updates: the firm's ops person? Annual re-certification (acceptable if productized) vs continuous monitoring (babysitting: fail)?
10. **One-time shape:** commission -> certify -> hand off, with optional discrete re-certifications. Confirm market precedent for inspection-shaped (not retainer-shaped) engagements: home inspection, SOC2 audit, commissioning agents as analogues.
11. **Babysitting risk:** highest of the three: research how inspection/audit industries keep engagements bounded.
12. **Right-to-win questions:** is a commissioning-engineer frame legible/credible to professional-firm buyers? Does his lack of law/accounting domain pedigree block trust, or does independence beat pedigree?
13. **PROVES IT:** documented pilot-failure pain with budgets attached + verified absence of independent acceptance-testing providers downmarket + insurer/bar pressure trending toward verification requirements.
14. **KILLS IT:** platform vendors shipping built-in certification; firms demonstrably only buying from credentialed insiders; engagements unscopeable to a close.

2. **Buyer types:** pastors/church boards; HBCU alumni chapter and national-body officers; small 501(c)(3) EDs; fraternal/civic org officers; tournament/event committees (Kingdom = one known instance, evidence only).
3. **Existing providers:** denominational admin offices; AMCs (association management companies); nonprofit-compliance services (Foundation Group, BryteBridge class); church bookkeeping services; CPA firms doing 990s.
4. **Pricing signals:** 501(c)(3) reinstatement service pricing; church admin consulting rates; AMC minimum contracts; grant-writing/reporting service fees; nonprofit bookkeeping monthlies.
5. **Public pain evidence:** IRS auto-revocation statistics (annual counts, share of small orgs); reinstatement volume; denominational reports on admin burden; nonprofit-forum threads (r/nonprofit) on grant-reporting drowning; news coverage of small-org compliance failures.
6. **Existing buying behavior:** do small Black institutions PAY for this (reinstatement services have customers: confirm scale) or rely on volunteers until failure?
7. **Already bundled/free:** denominational templates; state nonprofit-association toolkits; free IRS resources. What do they NOT do (installation + training + verification: suspected gap).
8. **Underserved gap hypothesis:** install + steward-training + verification as a one-time engagement, channeled through ONE trusted national body instead of door-to-door.
9. **Handoff feasibility:** the steward question: does each org have a person who can run the installed system? (Mirror of the universal handoff fragility: this hypothesis tests it structurally.)
10. **One-time shape:** install/train/certify then annual check (discrete). Confirm whether existing providers manage to stay bounded or get dragged into ongoing admin.
11. **Babysitting risk:** high if the steward doesn't exist; the channel-body model mitigates (the body fields ongoing questions, not BJ).
12. **Right-to-win questions:** does insider trust + cross-register fluency actually convert at the CHANNEL level (a denomination/national body blessing it) and what do channel gatekeepers require?
13. **PROVES IT:** auto-revocation/compliance pain at scale + existing paid reinstatement/admin services (proving budgets exist) + at least one plausible national channel with vendor relationships.
14. **KILLS IT:** per-org budgets reliably under $2-3k; channels only endorsing member-owned or brand-name vendors; engagements collapsing into volunteer-replacement admin work.
