## PART 3 · PROOF BOTTLENECK RESOLVED: THE OPERATOR PROOF-SPRINT

The operator path: start from a blank that already carries a testable shrink spec, run a real accelerated wash test in 2 to 3 days, turn the test into the hero content. The receipt is real, the timeline is days, nobody lies.

### THE FRAME FIX FIRST (the most important finding)

Cotton does almost all of its shrinking in the first 1 to 3 wash-and-dry cycles, then the curve flattens hard, and the dryer heat, not the wash, is the main driver. The industry standard for "dimensional change after home laundering" is **AATCC Test Method 135, which runs 5 wash-and-dry cycles** and computes shrinkage as ((B − A) / A) × 100.

Three consequences:
1. A 5-cycle hot-wash hot-dry test is a near-perfect, audited proxy for a 20-wash life. You are not faking 20 washes; you are running the test the industry runs and reporting it honestly.
2. Run it on HARD mode: hot wash plus hot tumble dry, the worst case a real customer inflicts. Surviving that is a stronger claim than any competitor's "wash cold, hang dry."
3. Keep "20 washes" as brand poetry only if the receipt behind it is the real 5-cycle worst-case number. Publish "measured before and after 5 hot wash-and-dry cycles, the standard for repeated home laundering." Do not print "20 washes guaranteed" next to a 5-cycle test unless you actually run 20. You can run a literal 20 later as slow-burn content; you do not need it to ship the guarantee.

### (A) SUPPLIER SPECS AS INTERIM PROOF (citable tonight)

Garment-dyeing dyes the sewn garment in hot water, which pre-shrinks the body. That is why garment-dyed blanks are the right base for a dimensional-stability claim. Buy all three; it is roughly $30 of blanks, and the test decides the hero with data, not preference.

| Blank | Weight | Published spec you can cite honestly | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Comfort Colors 1717** | 6.1 oz / 207 GSM, garment-dyed | The only one with a real published number: "pre-shrunk during garment-dye, expect about 2 to 3% shrinkage at high heat." | The benchmark your tee must beat. No-minimum singles available. |
| **LA Apparel 1801 GD** | 6.5 oz / 220 GSM, garment-dyed, made in LA | "Pre-shrunk, virtually shrink-free from garment dyeing," but no published %. | The brand story (made in LA) plus your own measured number. No published %, so your test IS the spec and you own it. |
| **AS Colour 5082 Heavy Faded** | 240 GSM, garment-dyed | "Preshrunk," full published size chart with ±2.5 cm tolerance. | The opacity champion (heaviest, best vs the #1 see-through complaint). Confirm boxy fit matches the "square not cropped" lane. |

Interim likelihood proof you can post tonight: "Garment-dyed and pre-shrunk. The shrink is taken out at the mill, in hot water, before the shirt ever reaches you. Independent testing on this class of blank reports 2 to 3% worst-case at high heat. We are about to measure ours, in public." Real, citable, no overclaim, while the test bakes.

### (B) THE ACCELERATED REAL WASH TEST (72 hours, honest, not faked)

**Method A, the 5-cycle AATCC proxy (recommended, 1 day).** Five consecutive hot-wash plus hot-tumble-dry cycles, back to back. A home washer/dryer does a cycle in roughly 1.5 to 2 hours; five cycles is one focused day at home, or 2 to 3 hours running parallel machines at a laundromat. This produces the number that backs the guarantee.

**Method B, the literal 20-cycle sprint (2 to 3 days, optional, for footage).** One shirt through 20 sequential cycles at a laundromat where each is roughly 50 minutes is about 16 to 17 machine-hours: two laundromat sessions across two days. Use this only if you want the literal "washed it 20 times" footage. The guarantee number comes from Method A.

**The measurement protocol (this IS the proof, do it right):**
1. New tee from the bag. Lay flat, no tension. Measure body length (high point shoulder to hem) and chest width (pit to pit). Photograph with a ruler in frame. This is A.
2. Mark the exact measurement points with a fabric pen or stitch so you measure identical points every time.
3. Run the cycles, hot/hot.
4. Lay flat again, same points, same ruler, same framing. This is B.
5. Compute ((B − A) / A) × 100 for length and width.
6. Repeat across all three blanks. Lowest length-loss wins and becomes the hero. The guarantee tolerance is the measured number, never a number you have not measured.

What "real" requires: same ruler, same flat surface, no tension, identical framing, shot on the R6. This is the single most important asset in the brand and the protocol is what makes it un-fakeable.

### (C) SAMPLE SOURCING AND SAMPLING LEAD TIMES

Neck prints can run as low as 36 pieces.

Assembled: maximize the dream (end the hunt) and the likelihood (the measurement makes it believable); minimize the time (we already did the washes, here is a real ship date) and the effort (one tee, normal laundry, zero buying risk).
