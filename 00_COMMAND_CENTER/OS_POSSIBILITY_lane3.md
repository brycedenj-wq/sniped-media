## 1. What the lane actually is

The Kingdom of the Sun (KOTS) is the 53rd Annual (2026) invitation-only national high school holiday basketball tournament, December 28-31, Vanguard High School, Ocala FL. Founded 1974 by Jim Haley. 51-plus years of history. 79 State Champions, 215 different Florida schools, 140 out-of-state teams from 25+ states plus Bahamas and Canada. NBA alumni: Joel Embiid (2023 MVP), Dwight Howard (HOF 2025), Kwame Brown, Christian Laettner, Jamal Mashburn, plus coaches Joe Mazzulla and Dan Hurley. NFHS Network already streams every game via Pixellot camera installed in the Vanguard gym.

BJ's dad (Coach Eric Jones) holds the Vanguard head coaching job AND the lead on the tournament committee. BJ's role (Lane B) is the complete media production operation: official photography, video packages, the tournament website, sponsor media deliverables, and the permanent archive. The site is already live at thekingdomofthesun.com. The Crown / Sun Rays visual identity system is built and QA-passed at v1.2. All governance structures (sponsor system, coach pipeline, booster club framework, revenue-share proposal for the principal, shot list, gallery delivery, game-week calendar) are built internally.

What KOTS is as a lane: B2B2C institutional sports media. Committee (B2B) pays the floor. Families, players, and sponsors (B2C) pay the upside. Neither bucket competes with the other.

## 10. What could go wrong

**Primary risks (verified in OS):**

1. **Dad's authority is softer than stated.** If the committee vote goes against the production fee, or if a committee member with competing interests blocks the sponsor money, the floor does not get paid and the trip is not financially viable. This is the single highest-risk scenario. Mitigation: confirm the committee structure before booking anything.

2. **Eric Jones gets overwhelmed (the firehose spiral).** Dad-handling doctrine is explicit: never firehose, one move at a time. If BJ sends the full system docs, the media plan, the sponsor proposal, the governance proposal, and the committee ask in the same conversation, dad shuts down. The OS has a standing rule to frame everything as load reduction, one item only.

3. **Lane A and Lane B blur.** If BJ pitches sponsor outreach himself without dad's delegation, the authority dynamic breaks. If BJ's SNIPED brand enters the KOTS public-facing materials, the institutional framing is damaged. The lane separation rule is a binary gate, not a preference.

4. **NFHS owns more broadcast inventory than assumed.** The sponsor system explicitly gates broadcast placement on confirmation that the tournament can sell NFHS stream ad inventory. If NFHS owns all of it, that sponsor asset disappears. Mitigation: confirm before any sponsor is pitched on stream visibility.

5. **Family photo sales are not approved.** Dad (or the committee) may not greenlit photo/video sales to families. This removes the largest variable-upside bucket. Mitigation: explicitly confirm in the one-liner conversation. It is listed as OPEN LOOPS item 6.

6. **BJ's physical presence is not financially cleared in time.** If the committee vote is delayed until November, airfare to Orlando for the holiday week escalates significantly. Mitigation: committee ask by August at the latest.

7. **Institutional reframe fails.** If the principal conversation never happens and the school committee structure is not formed, KOTS remains an Eric Jones personal event rather than a Vanguard institution. Revenue-share, school account routing, and multi-year infrastructure are all contingent on that structure existing. This is the long-term moat risk.

8. **KOTS is not transferable.** The six-line model is designed to be replicable to other schools and tournaments. But if the Year 1 execution depends entirely on the kinship relationship with dad, and no external institutional relationship proves the model independently, the vertical claim does not hold. Year 2 proof requires a second touchpoint that does not rely on family access.
