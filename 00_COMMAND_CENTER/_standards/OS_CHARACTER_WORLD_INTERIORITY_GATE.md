# OS Character World Interiority Gate

Date: 2026-06-20
Status: active standard
Use with: os-world-bible, sniped-crs-builder, cinema-worldbuilder, brand_campaign, film, photo/composite
Source inspiration: operator-provided character journal photos, stripped and rebuilt as SNIPED doctrine

## Core Rule

A world is not just a place, palette, camera, outfit, and lighting system.

A world becomes believable when every recurring character has interior pressure: what they hide, what they want, how their body betrays them, who they orbit, what they believe, what they cannot say, and what changes when they enter a room.

No serious new world, recurring character, campaign character, brand film, or cinematic sequence should move into prompts from only surface descriptors.

## When This Gate Triggers

Use this gate when the task involves:

- new fictional world
- recurring character
- character sheet
- brand world
- campaign concept
- film scene
- Seedance/Kling/Higgsfield scene with character continuity
- AI influencer / fictional presenter
- narrative photo/composite world
- any world where the character must feel alive across more than one output

Do not force this for:

- one-off background extras
- simple product-only frames
- pure environment plates with no human logic
- quick casual drafts clearly marked as throwaway

## The 12-Lock Interior Card

Before a serious character/world build, create a compact Interior Card. One to three lines per lock is enough. The point is not biography bloat. The point is behavioral control.

1. **Want:** what they are chasing on the surface.
2. **Need:** what would actually free them, whether they know it or not.
3. **Secret:** what they would not want the room to know.
4. **Wound:** what old pressure still shapes their choices.
5. **Mask:** how they try to be seen.
6. **Body Tell:** how fear, anger, joy, shame, or attraction shows physically.
7. **Relationship Orbit:** who they seek, avoid, admire, resent, protect, or compete with.
8. **Loneliness Trigger:** when and where they feel most alone.
9. **Object Anchor:** one object that feels like home, proof, status, safety, or loss.
10. **Belief:** what they think the world owes, punishes, rewards, or misunderstands.
11. **Rule Broken:** one rule they have broken or would break under pressure.
12. **Pressure Response:** what they do when cornered, embarrassed, desired, challenged, or watched.

## World Interiority Questions

Ask these before building the visual bible if the world is narrative or campaign-facing:

- What kind of behavior does this world reward?
- What kind of person gets punished here?
- What does status look like here?
- What does safety look like here?
- What objects do people carry, hide, trade, worship, or keep?
- What sounds, smells, textures, or rituals make the world feel specific?
- What does the world make people lie about?
- What does the world make people proud of?
- What kind of loneliness exists here?
- What does the world believe is beautiful?

## Prompt Translation Rule

Do not dump the Interior Card into the generation prompt as exposition.

Translate interiority into:

- action
- posture
- gesture
- blocking
- gaze
- prop
- wardrobe wear
- object placement
- environmental pressure
- relationship distance
- lighting contrast
- camera distance

Bad:

`She is secretly lonely and afraid of being abandoned.`

Better:

`She stands half a step outside the group, thumb rubbing the chipped lighter in her pocket, laughing a beat late after everyone else.`

Bad:

`He believes intelligence means control.`

Better:

`He answers before the question ends, straightens the glass by one centimeter, and never sits with his back to the door.`

## Scene Gate

Every serious scene with a recurring character must answer:

- What does the character want in this exact moment?
- What are they hiding in this exact moment?
- What physical tell leaks through?
- What object, wardrobe detail, or spatial choice carries the subtext?
- What changes by the end of the shot?

If the scene does not change anything, it is a portrait, not a scene.

## Campaign Gate

For brand/campaign worlds, the Interior Card should produce a usable commercial insight:

- what the audience recognizes in the character
- what status fantasy the character carries
- what anxiety the product/world relieves
- what object or ritual makes the brand feel lived-in
- what behavior the campaign teaches the audience to imitate

If the character is only attractive, styled, or expensive-looking, the campaign is not deep enough.

## Continuity Rules

- Visual identity can change outfits, hair, makeup, and setting, but the interior pressure should still read.
- If the character's body tells, relationship orbit, or pressure response changes without story reason, flag it as drift.
- If a world has no status system, no rituals, and no forbidden behavior, it is not a world yet.
- Do not over-explain the character in final prompts. Show the truth through physical and environmental choices.

## Completion Gate

Status stays draft/internal until:

- the Interior Card exists for any recurring character
- the world has at least one status rule, one safety rule, one ritual, and one forbidden behavior
- every scene prompt translates interiority into physical behavior
- the usual visual proof gate still passes for final/client-facing output

## One-Line Law

Surface makes the frame look good. Interiority makes the world survive the next frame.
