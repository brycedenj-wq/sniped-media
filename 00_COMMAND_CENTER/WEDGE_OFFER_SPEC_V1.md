## 3. Ideal customer profile

**A. Critical-facilities staffing / recruiting firms**
- Buyer title: owner/founder, VP of recruiting, or head of marketing/employer brand.
- Company size: ~10-150 people; placing critical-facilities/data-center techs, commissioning agents, MEP roles.
- Budget signal: a single placement is worth ~$20-45K (20-25% of a $100-180K salary); they spend on job boards, sponsorships, LinkedIn Recruiter. Marketing is discretionary.
- Pain: severe talent shortage; candidates have multiple offers; their careers page and employer brand are generic; candidates can't tell why this firm beats the next.
- Trigger event: a hard-to-fill req open 60+ days; a new data-center client contract requiring rapid staffing; a competitor poaching.
- What they use now: LinkedIn posts, job boards, recruiters' own selfies/stock, a thin careers page.
- Why they care: one filled role pays for the dossier 10x; faster time-to-fill is money.
- Why they say no: "we already have a marketing person," "candidates come from referrals," "this is just content," "budget is for job boards."

**B. Specialty subcontractors (electrical / mechanical / commissioning / cooling / networking / field service)**
- Buyer title: owner/president, VP of business development/preconstruction, or estimator/proposal lead.
- Company size: ~15-300 people; bidding into data-center, mission-critical, and large commercial projects.
- Budget signal: bids are $500K-$10M+; they spend on prequalification, proposals, trade shows. Owner controls discretionary spend.
- Pain: they win on relationships and price, but on paper they look identical to competitors; their capability statement is a tired PDF; they also can't recruit fast enough.
- Trigger event: a major bid/RFP they want badly; a prequalification package due; losing a bid they should have won; a hiring push for a new project award.
- What they use now: an old capability statement, a generic website, word of mouth.
- Why they care: differentiating on one significant bid is worth six figures; the dossier doubles as a recruiting asset.
- Why they say no: "we win on relationships," "procurement only cares about price/safety record," "we're too busy," "security won't let you in."

## 4. Core deliverable (what the buyer receives)

- **Proof narrative:** a tight, credible written capability story, who the firm/crew is, what they actually do, the scope they handle, the standards they hold, in operator language, not marketing fluff.
- **Team/capability visuals:** approved imagery of the people and the work, shot in non-secure, controlled settings (see section 5). The instrument, framed as proof of capability and people.
- **Approved non-secure image set:** a curated set the firm owns and can reuse (LinkedIn, careers page, proposals).
- **Hiring/bid-support copy blocks:** ready-to-paste sections, "why work here," "what we deliver," capability bullets, role-attraction copy, mapped to the outcome (hire or bid).
- **Short web/LinkedIn-ready profile:** a one-screen capability profile (company + crew + proof) formatted for a careers page or a proposal appendix.
- **Private delivery:** a Pixieset gallery (password-protected) holding the assets + copy, branded to the firm.
- **Optional one-page landing asset:** a single capability/careers page (the firm's, not BJ's) if they want a live link for candidates or clients.
- **Testimonial/referral capture:** a short structured ask after a win/hire (only if the engagement earned it).
- **Explicitly excluded:** anything inside a secure facility; equipment/security/layout imagery; client-confidential names or project details; "headshots" sold as a product; ongoing content retainers (not in v1); the "verified registry," any platform claim, any SNIPED/BASEPLATE branding lock.

## 5. Security-safe production rules

- **Can be shown:** people (with release), tools/PPE/vehicles, exterior/yard/shop, training rooms, mockups, office, controlled/neutral or studio-style settings, generic non-identifying work-in-progress the firm approves.
- **Cannot be shown:** secure data-center floors, cage/rack/equipment layouts, security systems, badges/access, client logos/names under NDA, capacities, anything customer-confidential, internal docs, PII without consent.
- **Where to shoot:** the firm's own shop/yard/office, exterior, a neutral/controlled location, or staged setups the firm fully controls. Default to non-secure by design.
- **Anonymize:** client identities, locations, project specifics, any classified capability detail. Capability is shown generically ("we commission mission-critical cooling systems"), not by naming a protected site.
- **Consent/release:** written model release from every person shown; written firm sign-off on every asset and copy block before delivery.
- **Approval workflow:** brief to BJ proposes shot/copy plan to firm approves scope (confirms nothing sensitive) to produce to firm reviews and approves every asset pre-delivery. Nothing publishes without firm written approval.
- **Hard rules:** no internal docs, no customer/confidential info, no facility-sensitive imagery, and check BJ's own employer NDA/non-compete before taking any client in the same supply chain.

## 10. Discovery call script (20 min)

- **Opening (1-2 min):** "Thanks for the time. I keep these short. I come from the operator side of critical facilities; I build capability proof assets that move a hire or a bid. I want to understand your situation, then tell you honestly if I can help."
- **Context (3 min):** "What does your firm do, and where does most of your data-center / mission-critical work sit right now?" "How's the buildout affecting you, more demand than you can staff, or more competition on bids?"
- **Pain (3 min):** "When you lose a hire or a bid you should've won, what usually decided it?" "How do clients/candidates form their first impression of you today?"
- **Current process (3 min):** "What do you use now for recruiting / for bids, careers page, capability statement, referrals?" "Who handles that, and how happy are you with it?"
- **Bid / hiring (3 min):** *(staffing)* "What's a hard role open right now, and what does a vacancy cost you per week?" *(subs)* "What's a bid coming up you really want, and what's it worth?"
- **Security (2 min):** "What can and can't be shown given your clients' security and NDAs? I design around that by default, nothing sensitive, your full approval."
- **Value (2 min):** "If one asset helped you fill that role / win that bid, what's that worth to you?"
- **Close / next step (2 min):** "Based on what you said, the Pilot Capability Dossier fits, $2,500-4,000, 1-2 weeks, you approve everything. Want to run one on [the specific role/bid]? I can start this week."

## 11. Qualification / disqualification

- **Green flags:** owner/decision-maker on the call; a specific open role or named bid; quantifiable cost of a vacancy/lost bid; discretionary budget; controls non-secure settings; frustrated with how they look on paper.
- **Yellow flags:** interested but no specific role/bid; "send me info"; marketing person involved but not the owner; vague budget; wants it but timing is "next quarter."
- **Red flags:** no decision-maker; "we win on price only"; can't show anything (all work secured under hard NDA with no non-secure angle); wants free/cheap content; treats it as headshots; no real role or bid.
- **Minimum viable buyer:** a decision-maker with one concrete outcome (a real open role or a real upcoming bid), discretionary budget covering a Pilot, and at least one non-secure setting to work in.

## 12. Proof-loop mapping (to T1/T2/T3)

- **T1 · Staffing firm test** · Pass: at least 1 staffing firm pays a Pilot (or signs a paid LOI). Fail: 0 paid after 5 real conversations. - **T2 · Subcontractor test** · Pass: at least 1 sub pays a Pilot or a concrete LOI tied to a live bid. Fail: interest but no payment/LOI after 5 conversations. Log: Proof entry + "infra-buyer pull (subs)"; price, paid y/n, named bid, bid-use y/n. Real proof = money + a named bid. Fake = "we'll keep you in mind."
- **T3 · Room-opening / access test** · Pass: at least 1 decision-maker, shown a spec/sample dossier, says "this would help me win [bid] / fill [role]" AND grants a non-secure shoot, an intro, or a bid-use commitment. Fail: compliments only. Log: Proof entry + "room opened y/n," "access granted y/n." Real proof = access or commitment. Fake = praise with no door.
