## TOP LESSONS (deduped, ranked by SNIPED signal weight)

### Tier 1: Directly Operational for SNIPED Now

**1. Photography IP default: you own it, you license it.**
Photographer retains copyright by default in North America unless contract specifies transfer. Clients receive a license for defined use cases, terms, and regions. This is not optional posturing; it is the legal default. Rights Managed (RM) licensing: strict control, negotiated per use. Royalty Free (RF): flat fee, broad use. SNIPED should never accept "unlimited in perpetuity" language.

**2. Three-fee structure for commercial work.**
Production expenses + creative day rate + rights/usage licensing fees. These are separately itemized for enterprise/mid-market clients. For smaller clients, bundle into all-in-one fee to reduce objection. Licensing fees vary by: use case (organic social < paid social < billboards < TV), term (1 month to perpetuity), region (local < national < worldwide).

**3. Payment terms are active cash-flow controls, not administrative defaults.**
Net 30 is B2B standard. Net 7-15 for new clients; Net 30-60 for trusted clients. Early payment discount (2/10 Net 30) annualizes to 36.7% ROI: a compelling lever. Milestone/progressive billing (30% upfront, 30% mid, 40% on delivery) fits SNIPED's shoot phases and protects working capital. Deposits on new clients (30-50%) de-risk before full credit is extended.

**4. Cancellation clauses must define enforceable consequences.**
Liquidated damages = reasonable estimate of lost income + opportunity cost. Enforceable window: 14-60 days. 90+ days may not hold in court. Force majeure applies only to genuinely unforeseeable events (hurricane, war), not inflation, illness, or cold feet. Small claims court ($100-150 filing fee) is viable enforcement, often triggers payment before appearance. Do not waive clauses at first cancellation; it sets precedent that policies are optional.

**5. Contracts are non-negotiable for every shoot, including free/test work.**
Core framework: Who, What, Where, When, Why, How Much. Required clauses: scope of services, deliverables (count, format, delivery method), payment schedule, IP/copyright ownership, model release, editing scope, cancellation/refund terms, revision limits, dispute resolution. Client resistance to contracts is a red flag, not a negotiation. Use HoneyBook ($39/mo) or Pixieset as contract + invoice platform.

**6. Human authorship is the copyright gate for AI-assisted work.**
US Copyright Office and federal courts: AI-generated outputs without human creative authorship have zero copyright protection. Prompts alone do not satisfy authorship. Human creative control over AI results determines copyrightability. Composite workflows (real subject + AI-generated environment + human-directed assembly) are defensible as authorship. Pure AI backgrounds with no human creative intent are not. This validates SNIPED's hybrid-operator stance explicitly.

**7. Fair use in AI training (June 2025 rulings).**
Bartz v. Anthropic and Kadrey v. Meta (June 2025): training AI on copyrighted work can be fair use if transformative, necessary for training, and no market displacement. Maintaining a side library of pirated training data beyond direct training need is not protected. Licensing beats piracy legally and reputationally.

**8. Model releases are required for commercial use of identifiable subjects.**
Editorial use (narrative, news, education): no release required. Commercial use (advertising a product, service, or idea): release required if subject is identifiable. Silhouettes or unidentifiable subjects: no release needed. Minors require parent/guardian signature and presence during shoot. Pre-shoot email notification of release requirement prevents day-of friction.

**9. Invoice automation multiplies stated payment terms.**
Clear terms plus passive follow-up still means slow pay. Clear terms plus automated reminders plus click-to-pay invoicing plus multiple payment methods (card, ACH, Apple Pay, PayPal) closes the gap. Late fees (1.5% monthly / 18% annualized) are self-enforcing when automated. 56% of small businesses are waiting on unpaid invoices; avg $17.5k owed.

**10. Independent contractor agreements require explicit scope to prevent disputes.**
IC signals: contractor controls work method, brings own gear, takes concurrent clients, negotiates rates, declines unfavorable terms. Employee signals: client controls method, schedule, shot list, revisions, delivery format. Over-controlling freelance talent in SNIPED shoots may trigger misclassification exposure. California law is strictest on IC classification.

### Tier 2: Useful Infrastructure and Business Hygiene

**11. Retainer vs. deposit distinction matters legally.**
Retainer: held in trust until services rendered (technically refundable). Deposit: non-refundable fee. Specify explicitly which applies. Non-refundable deposits compensate for scheduling opportunity cost. Ambiguity = client disputes.

**12. Contract language precision.**
Use "shall" for obligations, "may" for discretionary authority, "if" or "provided that" for conditions. Avoid "will" (implies futurity, not obligation). Vague scope = payment disputes. Explicit scope = reduced litigation risk.

**13. Bookkeeping cadence is non-optional for a lean operator.**
Weekly: record income/expenses, review expenses, enter bills, reconcile. Monthly: follow up past-due invoices, review cash flow, run P&L. Quarterly: estimated tax payments. Annually: 1099s, year-end close. Financial statements are useless unless reviewed and acted upon. Most business failures correlate with not analyzing numbers.

**14. Payment method diversity accelerates collection.**
Smart invoices (ACH, card, Apple Pay, recurring) outpace checks and COD. Mobile tap-to-pay for in-person transactions. Offering multiple methods is a competitive advantage and reduces the "I didn't know how to pay" friction.

**15. Business insurance baseline for a service operation.**
Four coverage categories: general liability, property (BPP + equipment), workers comp (if employees), commercial auto. Additions relevant to photography: professional liability (E&O), cyber liability, data compromise, equipment breakdown, employee dishonesty. Assess before scaling to team.

## SNIPED-RELEVANT EXTRACTS

**Payment and cash flow:**
- SNIPED's $1,500 reset floor benefits from milestone billing: partial at booking, partial mid-project, balance on delivery. Protects working capital for a lean operator.
- New clients get Net 7-15 or 50% upfront minimum. Trust-verified clients earn Net 30 standard.
- Early payment discount (2% off for 10-day payment) is a positioning move for premium clients: "pay fast, get better rate" signals exclusive-access psychology.
- Automated invoicing (HoneyBook or Pixieset) with click-to-pay links, automated reminders, and late fee triggers reduces AR drag without manual follow-up.

**IP, copyright, and AI:**
- SNIPED's composite workflow (real subject identity + AI-constructed environment + human-directed assembly) satisfies the human authorship standard and is defensible copyright. Pure AI backgrounds with no human orchestration are not protectable.
- June 2025 fair use rulings (Bartz, Kadrey) confirm AI training on copyrighted work can be fair use. SNIPED should not maintain side libraries of third-party material beyond direct training use.
- Nash Hagen case: $250K licensing revenue from existing photos. Photographers who treat work as IP assets (not deliverables) build passive income layers. SNIPED's existing portfolio is a licensable asset now.

**Contracts and operations:**
- SNIPED currently has no documented usage agreements for Chapter Card shoots or Brand System deliverables. Without a license doc, clients may assume unlimited use or redistribute work. This is an active gap.
- Cancellation policy enforcement: SNIPED's two-strike cast doctrine maps directly to no-show enforcement mechanics. Deposit required at booking, 72-hour notice minimum, one grace allowance, then charge on second miss.
- Model releases: pre-shoot email requirement prevents day-of friction. VIB casting subjects are identifiable; releases are required for commercial use of those images.
- Editing scope in contract prevents "strongest photograph != most processed" disputes with clients who expect over-retouching. Document SNIPED's visual standard in contract scope.

**Business risk:**
- California 2026 law changes (AB102, AB1327, SB517) apply if SNIPED scales to team or client project structure. AB1327 allows customers to cancel home-improvement projects via email in 3-day window; parallel for photography services is worth modeling.
- IC misclassification risk if SNIPED directs freelance talent closely (shot list, revision cycles, delivery format control). Solution: give IC talent defined deliverables + creative latitude. Document their independent gear/rate ownership.

**Insurance:**
- Shoot-day liability is uninsured without general liability coverage. Equipment breakdown and professional liability (E&O) should be assessed before SNIPED scales beyond test shoots into paid brand work.
