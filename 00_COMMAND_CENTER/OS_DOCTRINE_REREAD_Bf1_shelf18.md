### BOOK 1: Prompt Template - Combining Techniques-2.pdf

**Coverage:** 1/1 segments, complete.

**Thesis:** Three-technique composite prompting (Chain-of-Thought + Problem Decomposition + Self-Criticism) reliably produces deeper, more auditable AI outputs than single-technique prompts across any domain.

**Densest Frameworks / Lessons:**

1. **Composite prompt architecture:** Sequence CoT, then Decomposition, then Self-Critique in that order. Interleaving the three degrades each.
2. **Decomposition-first rule:** Break the task into three modular sub-problems before applying step-by-step reasoning. This prevents premature synthesis.
3. **Self-critique as validation layer:** The final step forces re-anchoring against the source brief or data, catching hallucinated logic or goal drift.
4. **Industry-agnostic template:** The pattern is domain-neutral (tested across marketing, retail, HR, strategy, finance) and therefore portable to photography production briefs.
5. **Three-beat output structure:** Each composite prompt naturally produces a three-section output (reasoning trace, decomposed execution, critique layer) that is auditable without re-reading the original prompt.

**SNIPED Application:**

The only load-bearing application is structured prompting for complex production briefs, such as a casting call that decomposes candidate criteria, applies CoT to tier-2 standby logic, and self-critiques against the Lineage Doctrine. Current SNIPED prompt usage is ad hoc. This template provides a repeatable scaffold for high-stakes, multi-variable briefs (composite environment selection, outreach sequence design, pricing option architecture).

**UPDATES / CONTRADICTS Existing Doctrine:**

No contradictions. Marginally reinforces the connected-toolchain default (tool-first routing) by offering a structured prompt format that reduces iteration waste. Does not touch photography, positioning, trust, hospitality, luxury market, or leverage, which are the OS spine. Low strategic weight; high operational utility for prompt-as-tool design.

### BOOK 2: The Founder's Dilemmas (Wasserman, 2012, Princeton)

**Coverage:** 23/23 segments, complete.

**Thesis:** 65% of startup failures trace to founding-team decisions, not market forces. Every founder repeatedly faces a single master tension (wealth vs. control), and the consistency of decisions across cofounders, hiring, capital, and succession determines outcomes more than any individual choice.

**Densest Frameworks / Lessons:**

1. **Rich vs. King Outcome Matrix.** Control-retaining founders sacrifice 52% of equity value vs. wealth-maximizing founders. Neither is wrong; the crime is inconsistency. Choosing King (control) means small-capital, slower growth, retained autonomy. Choosing Rich means board dilution, forced succession, larger exit. Hybrid strategies (mixing wealth and control choices across decisions) produce 80%+ failure-to-achieve-either rate (Appendix 11-1 math model).

2. **Three Rs Equilibrium: Relationships + Roles + Rewards.** These three founding-team dimensions must stay in alignment or the team implodes. Social-logic teams (friends, family) stabilize on equal equity; business-logic teams (coworkers, strangers) stabilize on equitable splits. The worst outcome is mismatching the logic type with the split structure. Friends who split equitably resent it; strangers who split equally feel conned by year 2.

3. **Playing-with-Fire Gap.** Social/family cofounders have the highest damage potential (treasured relationship at risk) AND the lowest likelihood of discussing conflict. Strangers naturally argue about expectations upfront. The gap requires deliberate mechanism: written agreements, third-party visibility (Geneva Convention model), disaster plans signed in calm moments. Damage is proportional to closeness.

4. **Human Capital Timing Framework (7-vector).** Founder readiness is a composite of industry knowledge, managerial experience, company-size experience, prior startup experience, social capital, financial capital, and lifecycle stage. 25-year optimal work-experience window before founding; beyond 25 years, specialization decay and handcuff accumulation outpace capital gains. Prior management experience strongly predicts solo-founding.

5. **Blueprint Consistency > Blueprint Quality (Stanford SPEC study).** Founders who deviate across their hiring blueprint (recruitment basis, reward structure, control style) are 2.3x more likely to fail than founders who maintain a consistent blueprint, even a suboptimal one. Pick a model and hold it.

6. **Hiring Stage Mismatch as the Killer.** Early-stage requires generalists with option value (redeployable across functions). Late-stage requires deep specialists. Founders who hire for the stage they want (not the stage they are in) trigger succession cascades, cultural breakdown, and role inflation. Big-company hires fail early-stage because they manage machinery rather than build it.

7. **Paradox of Entrepreneurial Success.** Product-development success accelerates founder-CEO obsolescence. When a founder solves Stage 1 (product), Stage 2 (sales/ops/multifunction management) emerges as the dominant problem, requiring different skills. VC-backed startups have replaced founder-CEOs in 50%+ of cases by the third round, 73% board-initiated (not voluntary). Success triggers replacement; the board recalibrates toward shareholder value.

8. **Dynamic Equity over Static Splits.** Founders overweight early contributions (ideas, seed capital) and underestimate future contributions (execution, sales, ops). Known/known-unknown/unknown-unknown taxonomy: contract terms handle knowns, vesting clauses handle foreseeable changes, trust handles surprises. UpDown's three renegotiations worked because the team built dynamic terms into the first handshake. Zipcar's Robin Chase's "stupid handshake" (equal 50/50, no adjustment for effort divergence) demonstrates the cost.

**SNIPED Application:**

- **Wealth vs. control gate is currently implicit in SNIPED and needs to be explicit.** Current OS signals suggest King-preference (Direction Stack ownership, locked aesthetic, Lineage Doctrine authority, scene-density over market scale), but some Empire OS language signals wealth-orientation (15-year revenue path, leverage logic, capital compound thesis). Wasserman's data says this mixed signal is dangerous before any capital, hiring, or partnership decision. BJ needs to name the gate, even provisionally, before the next non-trivial structural decision.
- **KOTS institutional reframe requires the Three Rs framework applied to the BJ + Eric (dad) dynamic.** This is a classic Playing-with-Fire scenario (family cofounder, highest damage potential, lowest discussion probability). Disaster plan, written role demarcation, third-party governance (school committee as referee) are not optional; they are the structural requirement for KOTS to survive operational stress.
- **Hiring sequencing is load-bearing.** Composite/retouching functions are ready for specialist hire. Outreach/partnerships remain fluid. Hiring a generalist now for fixed creative work is a blueprint deviation that 2.3x raises failure probability.
- **The OS itself is a firewall.** Documented systems, vesting-equivalent decision frameworks, lineage doctrine, casting discipline all reduce stewardship blindness and make decisions auditable. This is the analog to Wasserman's "contractual protection for friend/family cofounders."
- **Optimism bias is the live risk.** SNIPED's proof-sequencing doctrine (payment follows proof, full engagement before direction) is the correct countermeasure. Wasserman's Guy Kawasaki rule (multiply founder projections by 0.1, add 1 year) applies before any revenue timeline is committed externally.

**UPDATES / CONTRADICTS Existing Doctrine:**

- **PROMOTES (reinforces):** Payment follows proof, full engagement before direction, possibility engine / protect optionality, Company of One, connected OS as leverage layer, build internal-tight before market.
- **SHARPENS (adds precision):** The "possibility engine" rule in memory must now hold a specific gate: BJ's identity can stay open across lanes, but his *structural decisions* (equity, hiring, capital) must be consistent with a named motivation (King vs. Rich). Opening identity is fine. Mixing structural decisions is not.
- **NEW RISK FLAGGED:** The "old work informs, proof decides" doctrine is validated by Wasserman's Four Ignorances of first-time founders (they don't anticipate growth stages, don't know their own capabilities, haven't reflected on wealth/control preference). The OS has been building toward this; Wasserman supplies the empirical grounding. No contradiction, but adds urgency to the explicit wealth-vs-control naming gate.
- **WEAK SIGNAL AGAINST:** The Execution Governor's default-to-action posture (bias toward doing, not reporting) should be held in tension with Wasserman's finding that founders who move to market too early without structural clarity end up with locked-in equity splits, title inflation, and board compositions that remove their optionality. Action without structural gate-setting is the exact failure mode Wasserman documents in 3,600 startups.
- **SUPERSEDES NOTHING** in current doctrine, but supplies the empirical layer beneath several principles that were previously grounded in practitioner intuition (Blair Enns, Jarvis, Naval).

### EVALUATION GATES

1. **Wealth-vs-Control Consistency Gate.** Before any structural decision: does this choice align with the named motivation lane (King or Rich)? If BJ has not yet named the lane, this gate requires that naming before proceeding. Not a blocker on proof-phase actions; a blocker on structural commitments (equity, capital, formal roles).

2. **Founder Handcuff Audit Gate.** When considering any new commitment (retainer client, institutional partner, capital from personal network), run four-type handcuff check: psychological (does this title/brand association create identity lock?), golden (does this cash create lifestyle inflation that blocks pivoting?), legal (does this agreement create noncompete or IP claims?), family (does this commitment require time or geography that conflicts with support infrastructure?).

3. **Hiring Blueprint Deviation Gate.** When considering any hire: does this person/role/compensation structure match SNIPED's current hiring blueprint? If it deviates on two or more dimensions (recruitment source, reward structure, control style), flag and require explicit justification before proceeding. Deviations 2.3x failure rate per Wasserman's SPEC data.

4. **Playing-with-Fire Relationship Gate.** Before any collaboration with friend, family, or close referral partner: is there a written role demarcation? Is there a disaster plan (what happens if irreconcilable conflict)? Is there a third-party visibility mechanism (at minimum, one person outside the relationship who sees major decisions)? If none, the gate is open and the relationship is structurally unprotected.

5. **Composite Prompt Quality Gate.** For any multi-variable brief output: does the output contain a visible reasoning trace, a decomposed execution plan, and a self-critique against the source goal? If only one or two of the three are present, the output is incomplete.
