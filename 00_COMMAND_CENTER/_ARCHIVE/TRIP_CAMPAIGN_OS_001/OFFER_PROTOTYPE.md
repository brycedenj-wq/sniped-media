Concept, world, styling, visuals, scripts, captions, cold email, DMs, social rollout, sponsor angles, deck language, shot list, proof dashboard, and the assets themselves when the job calls for it.

This is the same wedge logic the OS already runs for the practice. The trip opens the room and earns the right to build something larger, the larger play is earned through the proof, not announced (`BASEPLATE_CANONICAL_STATEMENT.md` §4, §5; `sections/strategy.md` §1.1).

The market is real and already transacting. Operator-verified fact: the operator's friend is paying ten thousand dollars for a concert campaign package. The seller started at twenty-five thousand, then walked down through twenty, fifteen, twelve-five, and landed at ten. So ten thousand is the proven floor of live demand, and twenty-five thousand is a number the market actually entertained. That is the price reality this prototype is built against, not a number we invented.

One more thing, because it decides everything else. This is not AI content. This is the campaign brain that lets somebody with a real idea and real taste go crazy. The OS sells the meal, the built campaign for the client's idea, never the kitchen. We do not sell the OS, the prompt packs, the SREF slots, the doctrine, or AI access (`OS_MONEY_OFFER_DOCTRINE.md` value-chain logic; `OS_OFFER_STACK.md` active lane).

The pitch, in his voice, stays exactly this raw: bring me the idea, the world, the concert, the trip, the artist, the product, the script, whatever. If there is real taste or a real spark behind it, the OS builds the whole damn campaign around it.

## OFFER PROTOTYPE

### Who this serves
A person with a real input and real taste, and the budget to back it. A concert or an artist with a moment coming. A brand with a launch. A founder with a world in their head and no machine to build it. The screen runs before any pitch: in-lane, can say yes, cares about the outcome, low self-orientation. Price-shoppers and off-lane buyers are a pass, not a discount (`OS_MONEY_OFFER_DOCTRINE.md` screen-before-pitch; `sections/strategy.md` §3.4). This is not for someone who wants cheap content. It is for someone who has a spark and wants the whole machine built around it.

### What the client brings
The input and the taste. The concert, the trip, the artist, the brand, the idea, the script, the character, the world, the launch. They bring the spark and the real decision power. They do not need to bring a plan, a deck, a shot list, or a single asset. That is the work. If there is nothing real behind it, there is no job, and we say so.

### What the OS builds
The whole campaign brain around their input. Not one surface. The machine.
- Concept and positioning. The one reason this thing matters, stated as the audience's outcome, not the client's bio (`OS_MONEY_OFFER_DOCTRINE.md` grand-slam; `sections/strategy.md` §2.2).
- World and visual direction. The look, the rules, the color and motif system so every output is continuous instead of random.
- Styling direction for who and what appears on camera.
- The story and characters the campaign runs on.
- Scripts, captions, and the written voice across surfaces.
- Cold email and DM outreach, built off a written ICP before a single send (`sections/strategy.md` §3.1).
- The social rollout plan, sequenced, not a pile of posts.
- Sponsor and partner angles.
- Deck language for when they need to pitch it upward.
- Shot list and production plan.
- A proof dashboard that tracks what actually moved.
- The assets themselves, stills and motion, when the job calls for it.

### What deliverables are included
The campaign brain as a built, handed-over thing the client owns: the concept and positioning doc, the world and visual direction, the styling direction, the story and character spine, the script and caption set, the outreach copy and target logic, the sequenced rollout calendar, the sponsor-angle sheet, the deck language, the shot list, and the proof dashboard. Assets are scoped per tier below. The client owns what is built.

### Why this beats a normal creative director
A creative director gives you a deck and a vision and then leaves. They do not execute. They hand you a beautiful idea and a list of vendors, and now you are the producer, the writer, the outreach person, and the one stitching it together at midnight. This builds the whole machine, the concept and the captions and the cold email and the shot list and the proof dashboard, as one connected system, and hands it over built. A CD sells the idea. This ships the idea wired to every surface it has to live on.

### Why this beats a content agency
An agency is generic and slow, and it shows. They run your brand through the same template they ran the last ten through, route it across four account managers, and three weeks later you get safe, on-trend, forgettable. The Only-Them test kills that here: if a line could apply to a thousand other clients, it gets cut (`COLD_OUTREACH_ATOMS.md` §1, cited via `sections/strategy.md` §3.3). One operator with taste, building one machine around one real input, moving at the speed of one decision instead of four approvals. Agencies sell volume. This sells a thing that could only be yours.

### Why this beats a random AI operator
A random AI operator has the tools and none of the taste, and no proof. They will hand you a pile of generated slop, no story, no world rules, no reason any of it matters, and nothing that proves it worked. The edge here is the operator layer, the taste, the story, the world, the judgment about what to keep and what to kill, plus a real proof artifact from a real campaign. The moat was never the camera or the AI. It is the operator layer (`BASEPLATE_CANONICAL_STATEMENT.md` §4). An AI operator sells output. This sells judgment with a receipt.

### What proof the trip must produce before he can sell it
This offer is not proven until the trip proves it. The trip must produce, at minimum:
- One real room opened on the ground, a meeting taken, an intro made, a capture granted, that traces to the campaign being built, not to luck (`sections/strategy.md` §1.2 rung 2).
- A real built campaign artifact from the trip: the actual machine, concept through proof dashboard, not a mockup.
- A proof dashboard with real signal on it, what the campaign moved, so the case study is evidence and not a story.
- At least one in-lane conversation that converts toward a paid step, or a committed next step the proof-to-cash router would accept (`OS_MONEY_OFFER_DOCTRINE.md` payment-follows-proof; `sections/strategy.md` §1.3).
The opened room and its access proof is the load-bearing one. It is the single thing a creative director, an agency, and an AI operator cannot show, and it is the only honest justification for the price. Be blunt about the risk: if the trip produces a beautiful built campaign but no real room opened and no dashboard movement, the offer collapses back into a talented operator's content package, which is exactly the tier it claims to beat. There is no fallback differentiator if the access leg fails, so the trip must treat the opened room as the primary objective, not a nice-to-have. Until those exist, the offer is a prototype carried into the field, not a crowned product.

Anchored to the proven live floor of ten thousand. The full campaign brain, built and owned, for one input:

### What a 25k version includes
Anchored to the number the market already entertained. Everything in the 10k, plus the reasons it is worth the anchor:

- A built world bible deep enough that the campaign can keep producing after handover, the machine keeps running, not one drop.

- Direct operator involvement across the build, the taste applied hands-on, not delegated.
The 25k is worth the anchor because it is not more of the same. It is a system that keeps producing, owned by the client, built by the operator's hand. It is a world-build and IP-class job, which is exactly where the active lane anchors high (`OS_OFFER_STACK.md` world_build, ip_system).

### What a higher tier (owned world and retainer) anchors
Three-option pricing, anchored high, per the pricing doctrine (`OS_PRICING_GATE.md`; `OS_OFFER_STACK.md` world_build, ip_system). The 10k is the proven floor, the 25k is the number the market entertained, and the high anchor sits above both. The high tier (50k and up, scoped to the job) is an owned-world and retainer build: a full IP-class world system the client owns, an ongoing build relationship where the operator's hand stays on it across a season, and the campaign machine kept running and refreshed, not handed over once. This is also where the hands-on operator time lives, scoped and priced as its own tier instead of bleeding into the 25k. The high anchor makes the 10k and 25k read as the value buys they are. The exact high number is set per job and is anchored, never crowned, until the trip proves the offer.

## PROOF-CELL LINK

The trip is the proof cell. Here is exactly how its outputs become the case study that sells the offer.

The trip runs the offer on a real input. The whole machine gets built around that input, the same machine the 10k tier promises, concept through proof dashboard. That makes the trip a working demo of the product, not a vacation with a camera (`sections/strategy.md` §1.4 kill criteria).

Then three things from the trip become the sales asset:
1. The built campaign itself becomes the portfolio piece. This is what the machine produces, shown end to end.
2. The opened room and the conversation it produced become the access proof. This is the part a CD, an agency, and an AI operator cannot show, that it actually moved a real person in the real world.
3. The proof dashboard becomes the receipt. This is what separates the offer from everyone selling output, a campaign with evidence attached (`OS_MONEY_OFFER_DOCTRINE.md` proof-before-price).

Once those three exist and are real, the case study is honest, and only then does the offer move from prototype to sellable. Until then it carries into the field as a prototype. The trip proves it. Nothing else gets to (`BASEPLATE_CANONICAL_STATEMENT.md` §8 proof path).
