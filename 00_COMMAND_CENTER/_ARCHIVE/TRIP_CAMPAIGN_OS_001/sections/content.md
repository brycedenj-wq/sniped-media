## SECTION 8 · SOCIAL CONTENT PLAN BY DAY

### 8.0 What is determinable now vs UNKNOWN

Determinable now: the content-engine shape (a repeatable daily slot system), the post-type mix, the cadence law, the open-loop structure across a trip arc, the role each post plays in the funnel, and which OS source governs each.

UNKNOWN until operator fills: destination, dates, total duration (so the number of "during" days is unset), purpose/goal of the trip, who he is reaching, what is offered/sold, the public banner/identity, current audience size and platform split, and budget posture.

### 8.1 The three-phase trip arc (architecture)

Source that fires: `COLD_OUTREACH_ATOMS.md` §4 "Coil the spring upfront batching" + "Evergreen / trigger-based campaigns" (the trip itself is the trigger event); `COPY_DOCTRINE_CAPLES...md` §VII "Timely ads tied to current events, seasons, or holidays measure higher attention." A trip is a built-in timely event, which Caples says lifts attention in almost any month.

A trip campaign runs on three phases regardless of destination:

- **Phase A: Before (the spring-coil).** Build the audience's anticipation and pre-warm the people he will reach on the trip. This is where lists, templates, and the open loop get planted.
- **Phase B: During (the live arc).** One authored beat per day from the ground. The trip is the timely event; each day is an episode.
- **Phase C: After (the harvest).** Convert the attention earned during the trip into the real outcome (booked calls, replies, sales, relationships), then recycle the content into evergreen proof.

Gate: W4 (each phase ties to a real outcome, not vanity posting). W1 (both sources cited above are real and fire).

UNKNOWN: how many days Phase B spans (depends on trip duration). The slot system below scales to any length.

### 8.2 The daily slot system (the reusable engine)

Source that fires: `COLD_OUTREACH_ATOMS.md` §4 operator weekly routine ("one weekend block to build... then ~60 minutes daily"); `Bryceden_Voice_Style_Guide.md` PARAGRAPH_PATTERN_B_OPERATOR_WALKTHROUGH ("here's what actually happens on the ground, step by step"). The voice guide's operator-walkthrough register is the native format for a daily from-the-ground post.

Each Phase-B day fills the same four slots. This removes daily writer's block (the coil-the-spring principle) and keeps the voice consistent.

- **Slot 1 · The morning signal (1 post or story).** One sentence of intent for the day plus one visual. Format: operator-walkthrough opener. Role: keeps the open loop alive.
- **Slot 2 · The work beat (1 carousel or short video).** The actual thing being done/built/seen on the trip, taught as a process. Role: proof + authority. This is the load-bearing post of the day.
- **Slot 3 · The reality-check beat (1 story or short caption).** A REALITY_CHECK_LINE from the voice guide ("most people miss this," "here's where it breaks") applied to something observed that day. Role: credibility, low cost.
- **Slot 4 · The soft hand-raise (1 story with a single low-friction ask).** Role: converts watchers into repliers. Single CTA only.

Gate: W2 (voice + Caples single-CTA rule), W3 (rubric: each slot has a defined role, not filler), W5 applies to Slot 2 and any generated visual.

UNKNOWN: platform mix (which of these slots map to IG, TikTok, LinkedIn, X, email). The slot logic is platform-agnostic; routing per platform needs the audience facts.

### 8.3 Post-type mix and ratio

Source that fires: `COPY_DOCTRINE_CAPLES...md` §V "Long copy sells more" + §XII "Small ads run frequently beat a big ad run occasionally" (the small-and-frequent law maps directly to short daily stories beating one big drop); §VIII "photographs are proof." Caples' small-ad frequency law is the governing ratio.

Recommended weekly ratio inside Phase B (per 7 days):
- 60 percent small/frequent (stories, single-frame, one-line beats) · governs day-to-day presence.
- 30 percent proof posts (carousels, process videos, before/after) · the "long copy sells" lane, where the actual value lives.
- 10 percent hand-raise/offer posts · the close.

Gate: W3 (ratio is a measurable rubric), W4 (the 10 percent ties to outcome).

UNKNOWN: the offer being raised in the 10 percent (depends on what is sold).

### 8.4 The trip-long open loop

Source that fires: `COPY_DOCTRINE_CAPLES...md` §V "Arouse curiosity; do not satisfy it. An ad that fully reveals the secret kills the reason to buy." A trip has a natural narrative spine (he went somewhere to do something and we do not yet know how it ends), which is the strongest free open loop available.

Architecture: plant a single question on Day 1 of Phase B ("what happens if [the trip's stated goal] works / does not work") and pay it off only at the end of Phase C. Every daily beat advances it one notch without resolving it.

Gate: W2 (Caples curiosity law), W3.

UNKNOWN: the actual question (depends on the trip's purpose/goal). Placeholder: "[THE TRIP STAKE]."

### 8.5 Face-independence applied to the plan

Source that fires: the campaign constraint plus `COPY_DOCTRINE_CAPLES...md` §VIII "the picture is a label that signals the audience" and "photographs are proof." Proof can come from wardrobe, objects, places, POV, hands, the work itself, real models, or characters, none of which require the operator's face to carry the post.

Rule for every slot: the load-bearing proof element is the work/place/object/POV, not a selfie. The operator's voice and taste carry identity. Faces (models, characters, world faces, the operator's own when he opts in) are allowed as supporting elements, never as the sole reason a post works.

Gate: W5 where the visual is generated (route through the pixel-activation gate). W3 otherwise.

## SECTION 9 · SCRIPTS, HOOKS, CAPTIONS, AND POST FORMATS

### 9.0 Determinable now vs UNKNOWN

Determinable now: the hook formula library, the caption skeletons in the operator's real voice, the script structure for short video, the headline-quantity discipline, and the subject-line/first-line rules.

UNKNOWN: every concrete noun (destination, the offer, the audience, the stake). All templates below carry explicit placeholders so they go concrete the moment facts land.

### 9.1 Hook library (the stop-the-scroll layer)

Source that fires: `COPY_DOCTRINE_CAPLES...md` §III "the headline is 50 to 75 percent of the ad," the four headline qualities (self-interest, news, curiosity, quick-and-easy), and §III.formulas (29 proven types); cross-checked against `Bryceden_Voice_Style_Guide.md` REALITY_CHECK_LINE and SYSTEMS_REFRAME. The hook is the on-platform equivalent of the headline, so Caples' headline doctrine governs it directly.

Discipline rule (non-negotiable): write 12 to 25 hooks per important post, then cut to the strongest (Caples §III "write at least 12 to 25 headlines per ad"). Never ship the first hook.

Reusable hook skeletons (placeholders in brackets):
- How-to (Caples' most durable): "How to [desired outcome of the trip's audience] without [the thing they dread]."
- News/announcing: "[NEW]: [the thing happening on the trip]."
- Curiosity + self-interest fused (Caples warns pure curiosity fails alone): "The [surprising thing] about [TOPIC] that [AUDIENCE] keeps missing."
- Reality-check (voice guide signature): "Most people [do the obvious thing]. Here is where it breaks."
- Systems reframe (voice guide signature): "This is not a [surface problem]. It is a [system/sequence problem]."
- Selector hook (Caples split-test: adding a selector word lifts response 20 percent): "[AUDIENCE], [specific benefit]." Example shape: "[Founders in CITY], [outcome]."
- Story hook (Caples §XI durable narrative): "[Time marker] in [PLACE], [tension]. Then [turn]."

Gate: W2 (Caples + voice), W3.

UNKNOWN: [AUDIENCE], [TOPIC], [PLACE], the outcome, the dread. Cannot fill without trip facts.

### 9.2 Short-video script structure

Source that fires: `Bryceden_Voice_Style_Guide.md` PARAGRAPH_PATTERN_B_OPERATOR_WALKTHROUGH + VISUAL_CAUSE_AND_EFFECT_CHAIN + ONE_INSIGHT_PER_BEAT; `COPY_DOCTRINE_CAPLES...md` §V "first paragraph must continue the headline and state the key benefit" and "one insight, small pause, next." The voice guide's walkthrough and cause-and-effect chain are the spine of a from-the-ground clip.

Reusable structure (any clip length):
1. Hook (from 9.1) in the first 1 to 2 seconds. Must continue into the visual, not contradict it (Caples §VIII headline/illustration must tell the same story).
2. One insight only (ONE_INSIGHT_PER_BEAT). State the controlling claim.
3. Cause-and-effect chain showing why it matters (VISUAL_CAUSE_AND_EFFECT_CHAIN: "when X, then Y, and once Y, then the decisive consequence").
4. Reality-check line as the turn.
5. Single soft CTA or open-loop tag to the next day.

HALT rules from the voice guide: no motivational filler, no raw-transcription markers ("so basically," "yeah so"), no over-academic openers ("it is important to understand that").

Gate: W2, W3, W5 (the visual layer of any clip).

UNKNOWN: the insight (depends on what he is doing on the trip).

### 9.3 Caption skeletons in the operator's voice

Source that fires: `Bryceden_Voice_Style_Guide.md` CALM_AUTHORITY_NOT_LOUD, SENTENCE_RHYTHM_MIX, WHITE_SPACE_BREATHING, PARAGRAPH_PATTERN_A_CONTROL_INSIGHT_IMPACT, NORTH_STAR_FOUR_GATE; `COPY_DOCTRINE_CAPLES...md` §V "you, you, you," short sentences, present tense, "benefits early." These two doctrines combine: Caples for the selling mechanics, the voice guide for the register.

Skeleton A (proof post, Pattern A control-insight-impact):
- Line 1 (control): a short declarative claim. No throat-clearing.
- Lines 2 to 3 (insight): what is actually happening, operator register, varied sentence length.
- Line 4 (impact): the consequence for the reader.
- Final line: single soft CTA.

Skeleton B (small/frequent story): one reality-check line + one visual. No CTA or one micro-ask.

Voice HALT (from NORTH_STAR_FOUR_GATE): ship only if clear, structured, sounds like real field experience, and an operator would respect it. No em-dashes (global rule + house style).

Gate: W2, W3.

UNKNOWN: subject matter of each caption (trip-dependent).

### 9.4 Post-format catalogue

Source that fires: `COPY_DOCTRINE_CAPLES...md` §VIII (photographs over drawings, captions under every image, all-type can win if the headline is strong), §XII small-ad boil-down, §VII "use three or more subheads" (maps to carousel slide headers); `Bryceden_Voice_Style_Guide.md` WHITE_SPACE_BREATHING.

Formats and their governing rule:
- Carousel: each slide = one subhead (Caples §VII "three or more subheads tell the story to glancers"). One insight per slide (voice guide). First slide is the hook.
- Single-frame proof: photograph as proof + a selling caption beneath (Caples §VIII).
- Short video: structure from 9.2.
- Text-only post: allowed where the hook is strong enough (Caples §VIII "all-type ads can out-pull illustrated ads"). This is the cheapest, face-free format and leans entirely on voice.
- Story sequence: small/frequent law (§XII), single ask.

Gate: W3, W5 (image/video formats only).

### 9.5 The headline/hook test ritual

Source that fires: `COPY_DOCTRINE_CAPLES...md` §III "set headlines aside, read as a bored reader," §XIII Method 1 and 2 (cold read next day, read aloud), §II "always give at least two things to choose from." Caples' own test rituals, applied pre-post.

Pre-post ritual for any important post: write 12 to 25 hooks, set aside, reread cold as a bored scroller, read the caption aloud to catch stumbles, then pick from at least two finalists. Where audience volume allows, A/B the hook (Caples: split-test the offer/signal, not the CTA).

Gate: W2, W3.

UNKNOWN: whether audience volume supports real A/B (depends on current audience size).

## SECTION 10 · COLD EMAIL AND DM ANGLES

### 10.0 Determinable now vs UNKNOWN

Determinable now: the outreach structure, the cadence, the offer-first principle, the personalization mechanic, the deliverability guardrails, the objection bank shape, and the trip-as-trigger angle. The voice register is determinable (operator voice).

UNKNOWN: who he is reaching, what he is offering, the ICP definition, current list/infrastructure status, and whether outreach is B2B cold email at volume or warm DMs to a known audience. The architecture covers both lanes.

### 10.1 The governing law: offer and targeting beat copy

Source that fires: `COLD_OUTREACH_ATOMS.md` §3 "Offer beats copy every time" and §1 "Lead quality beats messaging quality... improving targeting by 10 percent often outperforms improving copy by 50 percent"; reinforced by `COPY_DOCTRINE_CAPLES...md` §IV "spend 11 of 12 months finding the right appeal." Both doctrines independently say the appeal/offer and the target matter more than the wording.

Implication for the trip: before any outreach copy, define the ICP (firmographics, role, pain, trigger) and the offer (outcome, not deliverable). The trip is the trigger signal that warms the angle.

Gate: W4 (offer/ICP is the business gate), W1.

UNKNOWN: ICP and offer. These are the highest-leverage blanks in this entire section.

### 10.2 The trip-as-trigger angle (the campaign's natural edge)

Source that fires: `COLD_OUTREACH_ATOMS.md` §1 "Trigger signals worth prioritizing for timing" + §4 "Evergreen / trigger-based campaigns fire on real-time events"; `COPY_DOCTRINE_CAPLES...md` §VII timely-event lift. A trip is a legitimate, specific, time-bound reason to reach out, which passes the "Only Them" test (§1) better than a generic blast.

Angle skeletons (placeholders):
- Geographic/proximity (if reaching people at the destination): "I am in [DESTINATION] [DATES] for [PURPOSE]. [One-line relevance to them]." This passes the Only-Them test because the date+place can only be true for that segment.
- Event-tie (if the trip centers on an event): reference the event's operational problem, not the event itself (§1 signal-to-pain mapping).
- Audience-warming (DM lane): for own-brand engagers who interacted with Phase-A content (§1 "own-brand engagers are the warmest leads").

Gate: W4, W2, W1.

UNKNOWN: [DESTINATION], [DATES], [PURPOSE], and who the recipients are.

### 10.3 Cold email structure and length caps

Source that fires: `COLD_OUTREACH_ATOMS.md` §3 "3-part cold email structure (4 to 6 sentences): (1) who I am, (2) top pain points + how you solve, (3) assumptive CTA with specific time," length caps (under 80 to 100 words, 8 to 10 words per sentence, 5th to 7th grade), single-CTA rule, plain text, no links in first email; cross-checked with `COPY_DOCTRINE_CAPLES...md` §V short sentences/short words and §IX single strong close. Both doctrines agree on brevity and one ask.

Reusable first-email skeleton:
- Sentence 1: cold-reading personalization (a real pattern in their public content), per §3.
- Sentence 2 to 3: the pain + the outcome you produce (outcome not deliverable, §3).
- Sentence 4: proof (one specific relevant win), §3 "cold prospects have zero trust."
- Sentence 5: assumptive or soft CTA (test variable per §3), single ask.
- PS: highest-read element (§3), use for the strongest nudge or the trip-trigger line.

Voice layer: operator register from `Bryceden_Voice_Style_Guide.md` (calm authority, no loud framing, no hedge). Human-tone signals from §3 (lowercase, contractions, plain text). No em-dashes.

Gate: W2, W4, W1.

UNKNOWN: the pain, the outcome, the proof point (all offer/ICP dependent).

### 10.5 DM lane (warm/audience-side)
