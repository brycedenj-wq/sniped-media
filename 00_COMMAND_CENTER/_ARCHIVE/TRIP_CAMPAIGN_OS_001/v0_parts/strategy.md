# TRIP CAMPAIGN v0 · STRATEGY / OUTREACH / PROOF

A runnable v0. Concrete structure around the blanks. UNKNOWN appears ONLY where a real trip fact is required (destination, dates, names). Everything determinable is built as rules and patterns, not filler. Fill the nine facts in `TRIP_FACT_INTAKE_CARD.md` and the [SLOTS] resolve.

Constraints: zero spend, zero generation, zero posting, no naming, no crown, no em-dashes, no hype. Face-independent (faces, models, characters, world faces, wardrobe, objects, places, POV, hands, body, and the operator's voice/taste/story all allowed; his face is never the load-bearing reason it works). Built on `sections/strategy.md` §1, §2, §3, §11 and `OFFER_PROTOTYPE.md`.

## 1 · CAMPAIGN THESIS

The trip is a wedge, not the product. It converts physical presence in one place into opened rooms, real relationships, and a proof artifact, on a lane the operator already owns (`sections/strategy.md` §1.1; `BASEPLATE_CANONICAL_STATEMENT.md` §4, §5).

Thesis sentence (fill the four slots, keep the shape):
> On this trip, the operator shows up in [PLACE = UNKNOWN] as the one who can build the whole campaign machine around a real input, so [TARGET = UNKNOWN] gets [their moment turned into a built, owned campaign = the dream outcome], and that presence earns the right to [the larger build = earned, not announced].

Three properties the thesis must hold (each gates the copy):
- Outcome-first. Leads with the target's result, never the trip or a bio (`sections/strategy.md` §2.2). The grand-slam ratio: dream outcome times perceived likelihood, over time and effort.
- In-lane and scope-traded. Built on the premium lane (campaign-house / world / IP), anchored high, scope traded never price. Old photo-shoot framing is fallback only (`sections/strategy.md` §2.3; `OS_OFFER_STACK.md`).
- Face-independent. Works if his face is swapped for a model, character, or world. If it only works with his face, it fails the rubric (`sections/strategy.md` §2.5).

The public thesis sells the present, concrete reason-to-meet. It never announces the bigger build, never claims a platform / registry / network, never carries fabricated proof (`sections/strategy.md` §2.4).

## 2 · ACCESS TARGET STRATEGY (how the trip opens a real room)

The load-bearing objective is a REAL OPENED ROOM plus an access receipt: a meeting taken, an intro made, a capture granted, a conversation that moves toward a paid step, traceable to the campaign. Pretty content is not enough (`sections/strategy.md` §1.2 rung 2; `OFFER_PROTOTYPE.md` proof section).

### Who the room is
Three target buckets, every one screened for fit before a single touch (`sections/strategy.md` §11.2):
- PEOPLE: individual decision-makers who can open or refer a room. Owners with discretion, real budget, acute urgency, reachable. [NAMES = UNKNOWN]
- BRANDS / FIRMS: in-lane organizations with a moment coming (a launch, a drop, a tour) that the campaign machine can move. Anchored to the active lane, not the fallback photo lane. [NAMES = UNKNOWN]
- VENUES / PLACES / SCENES: physical rooms, events, or locations that are themselves the access point on the ground. Trip-specific. [NAMES = UNKNOWN until destination + purpose set]

### Fit screen (run before any target enters the list)
A target only enters the roster if it passes (`sections/strategy.md` §3.4; `OS_MONEY_OFFER_DOCTRINE.md` screen-before-pitch):
- In-lane (the campaign machine actually serves them).
- Can say yes (decision capacity).
- Cares about the outcome, low self-orientation.
- Reachable on the ground during the trip window.
A price-shopper or off-lane target is a PASS, removed before the trip, not discounted during it.

### Warmest-first ordering (`sections/strategy.md` §11.3)
1. Anyone already engaging the operator's content who is in the trip location.
2. Targets warmed by one to two weeks of pre-trip commenting before any cold touch.
3. Cold-but-fit targets reached with an Only-Them reason-to-meet.
Positive replies during the trip get a response inside 30 minutes (mobile reality of being on the ground).

### Value-first, never a generic ask (`sections/strategy.md` §11.4)
Each target gets a value-first reason-to-meet (a teardown, a frame, a short take built for them), not a cold "can we meet." The micro-offer is the CTA; the meeting is the pivot after they engage. Lead with value before asking for a call.

## 3 · OUTREACH ANGLES

Targeting beats messaging. No outbound before a written one-page ICP exists with five fields: fit, problem presence, decision capacity, location/timing in the trip window, trigger signal (`sections/strategy.md` §3.1).

Segment the on-the-ground targets into 2 to 3 angle cohorts. Each cohort carries at least one fact that could only be true for that cohort, on this trip, in this place. The Only-Them test: if a line applies to a thousand other people, cut it (`sections/strategy.md` §3.3).

Determinable cohort frame (resolve the bracket when the target type lands):
- COHORT A · the moment-holder: someone with a dated trigger (a launch, a tour, a drop, a season) where the campaign machine turns the moment into an owned, built campaign.
- COHORT B · the under-built: someone with real taste and a real input but no machine, who is currently the producer, writer, and outreach person at midnight (`OFFER_PROTOTYPE.md` why-this-beats-a-CD).
- COHORT C · the venue / scene gatekeeper: someone whose room is the access point, reached with a value-first frame about their scene, not a pitch.

First-touch rules every angle obeys (the copy gate, W2): single CTA, under 80 words, no link, plain text, outcome-first, no hype, no em-dashes (`sections/strategy.md` §3.3, §11.4).

## 4 · FIRST 10 DM / COLD-EMAIL ANGLES

Concrete patterns in the operator's real voice (raw, plain, no hype, no em-dashes). [SLOTS] are UNKNOWN trip facts. Each is one angle, single CTA, value-first. Pick the channel per target; DMs run shorter than email. None send until the copy gate passes and the operator approves.

1. The teardown open (cohort B).
> Saw [THEIR THING = SLOT]. The idea is strong. The rollout around it is doing maybe a third of the work it could. I am in [PLACE = SLOT] [DATES = SLOT] and I build the whole campaign machine around one real input, concept through the proof dashboard. Want me to send you a two-minute teardown of where it is leaving reach on the table?

2. The moment-holder open (cohort A).
> [THEIR MOMENT = SLOT] is coming up. Most people treat that as one post. I treat it as a built campaign that keeps producing after the date. I am in [PLACE = SLOT] that week. Can I show you what that would look like for [THEIR MOMENT = SLOT]?

3. The one-line frame (DM, cohort A or B).
> You have the taste. You are missing the machine. I build the machine around the input. In [PLACE = SLOT] [DATES = SLOT]. Worth 15 minutes?

4. The venue / scene value-first (cohort C).
> I am shooting and building in [PLACE = SLOT] [DATES = SLOT] and [THEIR VENUE / SCENE = SLOT] keeps coming up as the room that gets it. Not pitching. I put together a short frame on how I would tell [THE SCENE = SLOT]. Can I send it?

5. The Only-Them open (cold email).
> This is not a template. I only reached out because of [THE FACT TRUE ONLY FOR THEM = SLOT]. I build a full campaign around one real input, and yours is one of the few where the spark is already there. I am on the ground in [PLACE = SLOT] [DATES = SLOT]. Open to a short call?

6. The proof-honest open (cohort A, post-warm).
> Straight version: I am building a real campaign in [PLACE = SLOT] as the proof of how I work, and I am picking the inputs carefully. [THEIR THING = SLOT] fits. I would build the whole thing around it, concept to proof dashboard. Want the outline?

7. The micro-offer CTA (any cohort, after a warm reply).
> Before any call, here is the value: I will map the single biggest gap in how [THEIR THING = SLOT] is being rolled out, free, this week. If it lands, we talk. If it does not, you keep the map.

8. The referral open (warm node who will not buy but knows who will).
> You know [THE SCENE / SLOT] better than anyone. I am in [PLACE = SLOT] [DATES = SLOT] building a full campaign around one real input. Who in your world has a moment coming that deserves the whole machine, not one post?

9. The post-engagement pivot (someone who engaged the warming).
> Appreciated you jumping in on [THE POST = SLOT]. Since you are in [PLACE = SLOT] and I am there [DATES = SLOT], worth grabbing 20 minutes? I build campaigns around real inputs and I think yours is one.

10. The trigger-signal open (target with a live reason to care).
> [THE TRIGGER = SLOT] tells me now is the moment for [THEIR THING = SLOT]. The operational problem that creates is [THE PROBLEM THE SIGNAL CREATES = SLOT]. That is exactly what the campaign machine solves. I am in [PLACE = SLOT] [DATES = SLOT]. Can I show you?

Note: angle 10 writes about the operational problem the signal creates, not the signal itself (`sections/strategy.md` §3.5).

## 5 · PROOF DASHBOARD

Proof, not vibes. Local CSV today (Airtable UNTESTED, ledger). Four metric tiers; the opened-room access receipt is the headline (`sections/ops.md` §13).

HEADLINE METRIC (load-bearing): ROOMS OPENED + ACCESS RECEIPT.
- Count of real rooms opened on the ground (meeting taken, intro made, capture granted, conversation toward a paid step).
- For each, the receipt: what it was, who, the date, and the campaign trace (which outreach or asset produced it). This is the single thing a creative director, an agency, and an AI operator cannot show (`OFFER_PROTOTYPE.md` proof section). No room opened, no proof, regardless of content quality.

Supporting tiers (`sections/ops.md` §13.1):
- TIER 1 · Build proof (pre-trip, binary): each scaffold passed its gate (offer W4, copy bank W2, world continuity, outreach list assembled).
- TIER 2 · Reach proof (live): assets published, views, manual save-rate / comprehension read. Virality predictor is a pre-post estimate, not public signal.
- TIER 3 · Response proof (live + post): reply rate, positive replies, calls booked.
- TIER 4 · Cash proof (post): real yes, proposal sent, paid. Hold the floor, trade scope not price.

Every numeric target is UNKNOWN until audience size, offer, and goal land (TIER 2-4 thresholds and kill criteria all need the baseline). The structure is fixed; the numbers wait on the intake card. Each lane (content, outreach, offer) carries a kill threshold so a dead lane is dropped, not nursed (`sections/ops.md` §13.3). If pixels ship, each frame logs a vision-reject pass, no self-crown (§13.2, W5).

## 6 · WHAT THE TRIP MUST PROVE FOR THE 10K OFFER

The offer is a prototype until the trip proves it. Minimum proof set (`OFFER_PROTOTYPE.md` proof section; `sections/strategy.md` §1.2, §1.3):
1. ONE REAL ROOM OPENED on the ground, traced to the campaign, not luck. LOAD-BEARING. It is the only honest justification for the price.
2. A real built campaign artifact from the trip: the actual machine, concept through proof dashboard, not a mockup.
3. A proof dashboard with real signal on it: what the campaign moved, so the case study is evidence not a story.
4. At least one in-lane conversation that converts toward a paid step, or a committed next step the proof-to-cash router would accept.

Blunt risk: if the trip produces a beautiful built campaign but no room opens and the dashboard does not move, the offer collapses back into a talented operator's content package, the exact tier it claims to beat. There is no fallback differentiator if the access leg fails. Treat the opened room as the primary objective, not a nice-to-have. Market reference, kept light: campaign packages are a thing people pay for; the offer stands on execution and the access receipt, not on any single anecdote. Until the four exist and are real, the offer carries into the field as a prototype, never crowned (`OFFER_PROTOTYPE.md` proof-cell link; `BASEPLATE_CANONICAL_STATEMENT.md` §8).

## 7 · COLD-ACCESS CONTINGENCY (the play when no room has moved)

The single biggest weak point in this v0 is the hardest access case: a cold-but-fit target who never engages the pre-warm, where the operator has no warm node to make an intro. Warmest-first (§2.3) covers the easy rooms. This section covers the hard one, so the load-bearing proof leg has a real fallback instead of hope.

TRIGGER (binds to the existing daily gate, no new metric): the end-of-day honesty gate (`content.md` Part 2) reports no room moved for two days running, OR the mid-trip mark is reached with zero rooms opened. Either one fires this ladder. Do not wait for the last day.

The escalation ladder, run in order. Every rung is value-first, on the ground, face-independent (the built work opens the room, not the operator's face), and never spam, never fabrication.

1. GO WHERE THE SCENE GATHERS. Stop working the inbox. Be physically in the room the target is already in (the venue, the event, the spot, the cohort-C scene). Presence on the ground is the one lever a remote operator does not have. Use it. No pitch on contact; a real reason to be there.
2. THE SHOW-DONT-ASK WALK-IN. Carry a tangible built piece: a printed one-pager, a phone-ready short cut, the [OBJECT] frame of THEIR thing already started. The ask becomes "I already started building this for you, want to see it," not "can I have your time." The artifact does the talking, so it works with no face on camera.
3. CAPTURE THE MICRO-CONTACT, SAME-DAY FOLLOW-UP. From any real-world contact, take the smallest next step (a handle, a "send it to me," a name). Send the value within hours while it is warm: single CTA, no link, copy-gate clean. Same-day cadence is the difference between a hallway hello and an opened room.
4. DOWNGRADE THE ASK, NEVER THE PRICE. If the big room will not open, take the smaller real yes: a 10-minute coffee, a DM thread that keeps moving, or a referral to the person who can open it. A referral to a real decision-maker IS an opened room and logs on the dashboard with its trace. This is scope-trade applied to access (`OFFER_PROTOTYPE.md` trade-scope-not-price), never a discount.
5. THE HONEST STOP. If the full ladder runs and no room moves by the trip's end, that is a real result, not a content problem. Log it on the dashboard as "access leg did not fire." Do not fabricate a room. Do not post a fake receipt. A failed access leg is the truth: the offer is not yet proven, and the next attempt needs a warmer pre-trip runway or a different target set. The honesty is part of the moat; a faked receipt would destroy the one thing a creative director, an agency, and an AI operator cannot show (`OFFER_PROTOTYPE.md` proof section).

Guardrails on the whole ladder: value-first only, no mass-DM, no fabrication, consent required for any capture, BJ-face-independent, nothing posts or sends without the operator. The contingency protects the access leg; it never lowers the offer floor.
