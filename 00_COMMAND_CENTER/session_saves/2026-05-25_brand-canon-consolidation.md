### Included sources (per-source The Brand Gap 4 / Designing Brand Identity 4 / Identity Designed 3 / Brand Naming 2 / Hello, My Name Is Awesome 2 · incl 1 synthesis)

- **The Brand Gap (Marty Neumeier)** · pdf · 22,630 words · 3 + 1 synthesis (015): a brand is a person's gut feeling (not what you say, what they say); the strategy-creativity gap and the charismatic brand; aesthetics as the language of feeling; the five disciplines (differentiate via who-you-are / what-you-do / why-it-matters, collaborate, innovate, validate, cultivate).
- **Designing Brand Identity (Alina Wheeler and Rob Meyerson)** · pdf · 97,191 words · 4: brand identity as recognition infrastructure (tangible, fuels recognition, amplifies differentiation); the brand-identity ideals (vision/meaning/authenticity/coherence/flexibility/commitment/value/differentiation/durability); the managed process and the primacy of touchpoints; positioning and the big idea as the foundation.
- **Identity Designed (David Airey)** · epub · 55,889 words · 3: visual identity as the instrument of distinction in saturated markets; consistency as consistently-distinctive (not sameness); the creative brief as the structural framework that disciplines the work.
- **Brand Naming (Rob Meyerson)** · epub · 43,009 words · 2: you do not have a brand until you have a name; the name as the number-one touchpoint and a strategic judgment; the generate-then-filter naming process. Held as naming-craft.
- **Hello, My Name Is Awesome (Alexandra Watkins)** · epub · 23,925 words · 2: the SMILE/SCRATCH test for memorable names; names land through familiar concepts and emotional resonance, not linguistic engineering. Held as naming-craft.
- **Synthesis (015 · operator-doctrine):** the brand-as-perception operator toolkit (shape perception, build recognition systems, settle positioning, name with craft) + the optionality guardrail · attributed to The Brand Gap.
