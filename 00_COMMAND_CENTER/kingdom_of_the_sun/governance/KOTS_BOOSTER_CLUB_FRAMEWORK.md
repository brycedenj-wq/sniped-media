# Vanguard Basketball Booster Club · Starter Framework

> Built 2026-06-01. The basics of how a booster club operates, kept clean and separate from the Kingdom tournament account. For BJ / Coach Jones to set up with the incoming president. Not legal or tax advice; confirm structure with the school and an accountant before opening accounts.

---

## What it is (and is not)

- **Is:** a parent/community organization that raises money and volunteers to support the **Vanguard basketball program** (the team), separate from the tournament.
- **Is not:** the Kingdom of the Sun. The Kingdom is the tournament, its own entity with its own account. The booster club supports the *school program*. Keep these two completely separate, separate accounts, separate books, separate purposes. This separation is the single most important rule.

---

## What it does
- Fundraising for the program (uniforms, gear, travel, meals, training, facility needs)
- Volunteers for games and events (concessions, gate, setup)
- Community building around the team (parents, alumni, local supporters)
- Optionally, supports the program's role in hosting the Kingdom (volunteer labor), without mixing the money

---

## Basic structure (keep it small to start)

| Role | Does what |
|---|---|
| **President** (the incoming lady) | Leads, sets the agenda, the public face, coordinates with the coach |
| **Treasurer** | Holds the money, keeps the books, signs checks, reports monthly. **Separate from the president** (two-person money control) |
| **Secretary** | Notes, membership list, communications |
| **Volunteers / committee** | Concessions, events, fundraising drives |

Start with President + Treasurer + Secretary. Add as it grows.

---

## How the money works (the part that matters most)

1. **Separate bank account**, in the booster club's name, never run through the Kingdom account or a personal account.
2. **Two-person control:** the treasurer manages, a second officer reviews. Two signatures on anything large.
3. **Simple monthly report:** money in, money out, balance. Shared at meetings. Transparency prevents every problem a booster club can have.
4. **Income sources:** membership dues, fundraisers, local sponsorships *for the program* (kept distinct from Kingdom sponsors), concessions.
5. **Spending:** approved by vote for anything beyond small routine costs, always toward the program's stated purpose.

---

## Legal / tax (decide early, with help)
- Most booster clubs register as a **501(c)(3) nonprofit** (tax-exempt, donations deductible) **or** operate as a **school-affiliated booster** under the district's umbrella. Each has rules.
- Talk to the **school/district** first (some require boosters to operate under district policy) and an **accountant** about 501(c)(3) vs school-affiliated.
- Get an **EIN**, open the bank account in the club's name, keep clean records from day one.

---

## First 5 steps to stand it up
1. Name it (e.g., "Vanguard Basketball Booster Club") and confirm the district's booster rules.
2. Lock President + Treasurer + Secretary.
3. Decide 501(c)(3) vs school-affiliated (with the accountant).
4. Get the EIN and open the separate bank account.
5. Set a simple monthly meeting + the first fundraiser.

---

## The one-line rule
**Kingdom money and booster money never touch.** Different accounts, different books, different purpose. Keep that line clean and the rest is easy.
