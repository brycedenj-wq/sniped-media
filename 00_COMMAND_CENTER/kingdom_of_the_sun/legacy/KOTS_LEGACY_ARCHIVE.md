## 2. How it all began (the founding story, verbatim-sourced)

Spring 1974. A young coach named **Jim Haley**, one year into the job at Vanguard, sat down with principal **Leon Rogers**, a Kentucky native who loved basketball. Haley, who had moved to Florida from Illinois less than a year earlier, said he wanted to host a tournament.

Rogers asked how many teams. "Eight?" Haley said, "Sixteen." Rogers told him he was crazy, that he would never line up sixteen teams in Florida. Haley said he could get all sixteen committed **within 24 hours**. Rogers said if he did, he could host it.

Twenty-four hours later, Haley walked back in with sixteen teams committed. The next day he brought in **Bob Murphy**, a friend and Vanguard supporter, and the two cold-called Ocala businesses for sponsorships. By the end of the day they had raised **$7,500**, and an Ocala institution was born.

Jim Haley passed away in November 2018. Vanguard named its court **Jim Haley Court** in December 2016. Bob Murphy served for decades as the tournament's Executive Director.

This story is the heart of the prestige layer. The 16-team bet, the 24-hour scramble, the $7,500 cold-call: that is the origin myth the brand is built on, and it maps directly onto what we are doing now (modernizing, sponsoring, scaling).

## 3. The lineage (why "the original" is literally true)

The Kingdom took off from the late 1970s through the mid-1990s and became one of the premier high school tournaments in the country. Others copied the blueprint:

- **King of the Bluegrass** (Kentucky) and the **Arby's Classic** (Tennessee) are established tournaments that spun off the Kingdom model.
- The **City of Palms Classic** (Ft. Myers), often regarded as the number one high school tournament in the country, was originally an eight-team event and **changed its format after seeing the Kingdom take off**.

This is the defensible claim: Ocala's tournament is the original 16-team national-flair tournament in America. The others followed it. Use this carefully and exactly, it is on the record in the program.
