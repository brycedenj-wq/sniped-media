# Negotiation Leverage & Black Swan Analysis

**Skill ID:** 16.2  
**Category:** Negotiation  
**Source Document(s):** NEGOTIATION_FRAMEWORKS.docx

## Purpose

Identify and build leverage (positive, negative, normative) before any negotiation, and systematically hunt for Black Swans that could change the entire dynamic.

## When to Use

When preparing for or conducting any high-stakes negotiation involving pricing, contracts, partnerships, or resource allocation.

## Instructions

Follow this workflow precisely. Each step is grounded in the source document(s) listed above. Do not skip steps. Do not invent frameworks, models, or terminology not present in the source material. Execute each step in order, using the exact logic and decision criteria documented.

## Workflow

1. Map Positive Leverage: What do you have that they want? Delay making offers to preserve this leverage. Identify what they want beyond the obvious.
2. Map Negative Leverage: What do they fear losing? Reputation, audience, relationships, status? Never make direct threats; instead, label the negative: 'It seems like you value your reputation for...'
3. Map Normative Leverage: What are their stated principles, past commitments, and public positions? Show inconsistency between their standards and their current offer.
4. Identify potential Black Swans: What don't you know that could change everything? What questions might reveal it?
5. Apply detection techniques: Ask 'Why are they communicating this right now?' Listen for omissions and topic avoidance. Watch for incongruence between words and tone.
6. Get face time: Black Swans are almost never discovered via email. Prioritize in-person or phone interactions.
7. Observe unguarded moments: Before/after the formal negotiation, in hallways, over coffee, in small talk.
8. Look for their 'religion': Their moral framework and norms that reveal hidden constraints and give you normative leverage.

## Output Format

Produce all of the following deliverables:

- A three-type leverage map (positive, negative, normative)
- A Black Swan hypothesis list (what unknowns could change everything?)
- Discovery questions designed to surface hidden information
- Leverage deployment plan for the specific negotiation
