## THE FRAME
Founders buy premium photography for status signaling, not photos:
- **In-group signaling** · "I work with the photographer who shoots [named peers]"
- **Costly signal** · the price IS the message. $1,500 says one thing, $15K says another.
- **Scarcity signal** · selectivity > availability
- **Authorship signal** · "I commissioned this from a named artist" beats "I hired a photographer"
- **Hidden motive** · founders won't say "I want to look elite." Copy must SHOW it without naming it.

The Elephant in the Brain lesson: signaling is real, but humans don't admit signaling motives. Don't write copy that says "this will make you look elite." Write copy that DEMONSTRATES elite-tier (selectivity, scarcity, named clients, refusal language, premium pricing visible).

## OUTPUT
- The status signal the situation should optimize for
- Specific copy / experience moves that surface it
- The moves that would BREAK the signal (cheap framing, availability-heavy language, "we can work with any budget")

## REFUSE
- "Affordable luxury" framing
- "We work with founders at every stage" availability signals
- Discounting language
- Explicit status-claim copy ("you'll look elite" type writing · breaks the elephant rule)
