# Comparative Advantage & Resource Allocation Analysis

**Skill ID:** 17.2  
**Category:** Economic Analysis  
**Source Document(s):** ECONOMIC_THINKING_FRAMEWORKS.docx

## Purpose

Determine what a business should do in-house versus outsource, where to allocate scarce resources, and how to evaluate trade-offs using economic thinking principles.

## When to Use

When evaluating business operations, financial structure, or revenue growth opportunities.

## Instructions

Follow this workflow precisely. Each step is grounded in the source document(s) listed above. Do not skip steps. Do not invent frameworks, models, or terminology not present in the source material. Execute each step in order, using the exact logic and decision criteria documented.

## Workflow

1. Identify all activities the business performs or could perform.
2. For each activity, assess opportunity cost: What else could you do with the time, capital, and attention spent here?
3. Determine comparative advantage: Which activities can you do at a lower opportunity cost than competitors or outsource partners?
4. Assess marginal returns: For each resource allocation, what is the incremental benefit of the next unit of effort versus the incremental cost?
5. Check for economies of scale: Where do high fixed costs create cost advantages with volume? Find the minimum efficient scale.
6. Assess diseconomies of scale: Where has the organization grown past the point of efficiency?
7. Apply the price signal: If a product or service isn't selling, the price signal is telling you something about consumer demand relative to alternatives.
8. Produce a resource allocation recommendation: What to do in-house (comparative advantage), what to outsource, what to stop doing entirely.

## Output Format

Produce all of the following deliverables:

- A comparative advantage map (in-house vs. outsource for each activity)
- An opportunity cost analysis for current resource allocation
- Marginal returns assessment for key investment areas
- Resource reallocation recommendation with trade-off analysis
